## A

### Abstract noun

A noun for an idea, quality or feeling that you cannot touch or see. *Honesty, fear, freedom* and *childhood* are abstract nouns.

> ***Honesty** is the best policy.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Active voice

A sentence is in the active voice when the subject **does** the action.

> ***Ravi** broke the window.*

Lesson: [Active and passive voice (Class 11)](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-11/)

### Adjective

A word that describes a noun or pronoun. It answers *what kind?*, *which?* or *how many?*

> *a **tall** building*, *The idli was **soft**.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Adjective clause (relative clause)

A subordinate clause that describes a noun. It usually begins with *who, whom, whose, which, that, where* or *when*, and comes right after the noun.

> *The man **who came here** is my uncle.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Adjective phrase

A phrase that does the job of an adjective, or a phrase whose main word is an adjective.

> *a woman **of great courage*** / *The coffee was **very hot**.*

Lesson: [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/)

### Adverb

A word that describes a verb, an adjective or another adverb. It answers *how?*, *when?*, *where?* or *how often?*

> *Ravi ran **fast**.* / *The tea is **very** hot.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Adverb clause

A subordinate clause that does the job of an adverb. It tells when, where, why, how, on what condition, with what purpose or result, or in spite of what.

> *We left **when the rain stopped**.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Adverb phrase

A phrase that does the job of an adverb, or a phrase whose main word is an adverb.

> *He did the work **with great care**.* / *She spoke **quite softly**.*

Lesson: [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/)

### Adverbial

Any word or group that tells when, where, how or why in a sentence. It is marked **A** in sentence patterns.

> *My uncle lives **in Coimbatore**.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Affirmative sentence

A positive statement, with no *not* or negative word.

> *He is always punctual.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Agent

The doer of an action in a passive sentence. It comes after *by*, and is often left out.

> *The letter was written **by Kavya**.*

Lesson: [Active and passive voice (Class 11)](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-11/)

### Agreement (concord)

The rule that the verb must match its subject in number (singular or plural) and person.

> *The boy **plays**. / The boys **play**.*

Lesson: [Subject–verb agreement](/topics/t05-subject-verb-agreement/class-12/)

### Apposition

A noun or noun phrase placed next to another noun to explain it, usually between commas.

> *Mr Rajan, **our principal**, gave a speech.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Article

The words *a, an* and *the*. They are a kind of determiner and come before a noun.

> ***a** mango, **an** hour, **the** sun*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 11)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-11/)

### Aspect

The way a tense shows an action: as a fact (simple), going on (continuous), finished (perfect), or going on for a period (perfect continuous).

> *She **writes** / **is writing** / **has written** / **has been writing**.*

Lesson: [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/)

### Assertive sentence (declarative sentence)

A sentence that states a fact or an opinion. It can be positive or negative and ends with a full stop.

> *Chennai is a coastal city.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Auxiliary verb (helping verb)

A verb that comes before the main verb and helps it make tenses, questions, negatives and the passive. The primary auxiliaries are *be, have* and *do*; the others are modals.

> *She **is** reading.* / *They **have** gone.* / ***Do** you know?*

Lesson: [Auxiliary verbs](/foundation/f3-auxiliaries/)

## B

### Backshift

Moving a tense one step back into the past in reported speech.

> *"I **am** tired."* → *He said that he **was** tired.*

Lesson: [Reported speech (Class 11)](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-11/)

### Bare infinitive

The base form of a verb without *to*. It is used after modals, *make, let, help, see* and *hear*.

> *You must **go**.* / *Let me **explain**.*

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

### Base form (V1)

The simple form of a verb, as listed in a dictionary.

> *go, write, play, eat*

Lesson: [Verb forms (V1 to V5)](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/)

## C

### Cardinal number

A counting number: *one, two, three*.

> ***three** books*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/)

### Clause

A group of words with a subject and a finite verb. A sentence has one clause for each finite verb.

> ***the bus left*** / ***because it was raining***

Lesson: [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/)

### Collective noun

A noun for a group seen as one: *team, family, class, jury, committee*. It takes a singular verb for the group as a unit, and a plural verb when the members act separately.

> *The **team** is strong.* / *The **jury** are divided.*

Lesson: [Subject–verb agreement](/topics/t05-subject-verb-agreement/class-12/)

### Common noun

A noun for any member of a group, not a particular one. It does not start with a capital letter.

> *girl, city, river, festival*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Comparative degree

The form of an adjective or adverb that compares two people or things. It usually ends in *-er* or uses *more*, and is followed by *than*.

> *Chennai is **hotter** than Ooty.*

Lesson: [Degrees of comparison](/topics/t08-degrees-of-comparison/class-12/)

### Complement

A word or group that completes the meaning by describing or naming the subject (after a linking verb) or the object.

> *Meena is **a doctor**.* / *They made him **captain**.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Complex sentence

A sentence with one main clause and at least one subordinate clause.

> ***As** he was ill, he stayed at home.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Compound preposition (prepositional phrase)

A group of words that works as one preposition, such as *in spite of, because of, instead of*. It is followed by a noun, pronoun or V4.

> ***In spite of** the rain, we played.*

Lesson: [Prepositions (Class 12)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-12/)

### Compound sentence

A sentence with two or more main clauses, joined by a coordinating conjunction (*and, but, or, so...*).

> *He was ill, **so** he stayed at home.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Compound-complex sentence

A sentence with two or more main clauses and at least one subordinate clause.

> *I called Meena, but she did not answer because she was asleep.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Conditional sentence

A sentence with an *if* clause (the condition) and a main clause (the result). There are four main types (0, 1, 2, 3) and mixed types.

> *If it rains, we will stay at home.*

Lesson: [Conditional clauses (Class 11)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-11/)

### Conjunction

A word that joins words, phrases or clauses.

> *Arun **and** Kavya* / *We stayed home **because** it rained.*

Lesson: [Connectives and linkers](/topics/t06-connectives/class-12/)

### Connective (linker)

Any word or phrase that joins ideas and shows how they are related: conjunctions and linking adverbs.

> *It rained. **However**, we went out.*

Lesson: [Connectives and linkers](/topics/t06-connectives/class-12/)

### Consonant sound

Any speech sound that is not a vowel sound. *A* is used before a consonant sound, even if the letter is a vowel.

> ***a** university* (sounds "you")

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 11)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-11/)

### Continuous aspect (progressive)

The aspect that shows an action as going on. It is made with *be* + V4.

> *She **is writing**.* / *They **were playing**.*

Lesson: [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/)

### Contraction (short form)

A shortened form where two words join, with an apostrophe in place of the missing letters.

> *isn't, don't, won't, I'm, she's*

Lesson: [Auxiliary verbs](/foundation/f3-auxiliaries/)

### Coordinating conjunction

A conjunction that joins two equal parts: *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so* (FANBOYS).

> *I called her, **but** she did not answer.*

Lesson: [Connectives and linkers](/topics/t06-connectives/class-12/)

### Correlative conjunctions

Conjunctions used in pairs: *both ... and, either ... or, neither ... nor, not only ... but also, no sooner ... than, hardly ... when*.

> ***Neither** Ravi **nor** Arun came.*

Lesson: [Connectives and linkers](/topics/t06-connectives/class-12/)

### Countable noun

A noun that can be counted and has a plural form.

> *one book, two books*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 11)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-11/)

## D

### Dangling participle

An error where a participle clause has no correct subject in the main clause, so it seems to describe the wrong thing.

> *~~Walking along the road, a snake bit him.~~* (The snake was not walking.)

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

### Deduction

A conclusion drawn from evidence, shown with *must* (sure: yes), *may / might* (perhaps) or *can't* (sure: no).

> *The road is wet. It **must have rained**.*

Lesson: [Modal auxiliaries (Class 12)](/topics/t02-modal-auxiliaries/class-12/)

### Defining and non-defining clauses

A defining relative clause tells us **which** person or thing (no commas). A non-defining clause adds extra information about someone already known (with commas, never *that*).

> *My brother **who lives in Delhi** is a doctor.* / *My brother, **who lives in Delhi**, is a doctor.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Definite article

The word *the*. It points to a particular thing that the listener knows.

> ***The** sun rises in the east.*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 11)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-11/)

### Demonstrative

A word that points: *this, that, these, those*. Before a noun it is a determiner; alone it is a pronoun.

> ***This** book is mine.* / ***This** is mine.*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/)

### Dependent preposition

A preposition that always goes with a particular adjective, verb or noun.

> *good **at**, depend **on**, reason **for***

Lesson: [Prepositions (Class 12)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-12/)

### Determiner

A word that comes before a noun and tells which one, whose, or how much / how many. Articles, demonstratives, possessives, quantifiers, distributives and numbers are determiners.

> ***the** bus, **my** bag, **some** water, **every** student*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/)

### Direct object

The person or thing that receives the action of the verb. Ask "verb + what / whom?"

> *Priya wrote **a letter**.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Direct speech

The exact words of a speaker, inside quotation marks.

> *She said, **"I like mangoes."***

Lesson: [Reported speech (Class 11)](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-11/)

### Distributive

A determiner that refers to members of a group one by one: *each, every, either, neither*. It takes a singular noun.

> ***Each** student has a book.*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/)

### Double negative

Two negative words for one negative idea. It is wrong in standard English.

> *~~I have not never seen it.~~* → *I have **never** seen it.*

Lesson: [Conditional clauses (Class 12)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-12/)

## E

### Exclamatory sentence

A sentence that shows strong feeling, often starting with *What* or *How*, and ending with an exclamation mark.

> *What a beautiful sunset!*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

## F

### Finite verb

A verb that shows tense and changes with the subject. Every clause has exactly one.

> *She **writes**. / She **wrote**. / They **write**.*

Lesson: [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/)

## G

### Gerund

The V4 (*-ing*) form used as a noun: as a subject, an object, or after a preposition.

> ***Swimming** is good exercise.* / *He is fond of **reading**.*

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

## H

### Head noun

The main noun in a long noun group. It decides the form of the verb.

> *The **box** of mangoes **is** heavy.*

Lesson: [Subject–verb agreement](/topics/t05-subject-verb-agreement/class-12/)

## I

### Imperative sentence

A sentence that gives a command, request or advice. The subject *you* is usually not written.

> *Close the door.* / *Please sit down.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Impersonal passive

A passive used to report what people say or believe, with *it* or with the person as subject.

> ***It is said that** he is rich.* / *He **is said to be** rich.*

Lesson: [Active and passive voice (Class 12)](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-12/)

### Indefinite article

The words *a* and *an*. They mean "one" or "any one", and are used with singular countable nouns.

> *I bought **a** pen and **an** eraser.*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 11)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-11/)

### Indefinite pronoun

A pronoun that does not refer to a particular person or thing: *someone, anybody, everyone, nothing, nobody*. Most take a singular verb.

> ***Somebody** has taken my pen.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Indirect object

The person who receives the direct object. It comes before the direct object, or after *to / for*.

> *Amma made **me** a cake.* / *Amma made a cake for **me**.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Indirect question (embedded question)

A question placed inside another sentence. It uses statement order and no *do / does / did*.

> *Can you tell me where **the office is**?*

Lesson: [Interrogatives (Class 12)](/topics/t10-questions/class-12/)

### Indirect speech (reported speech)

A speaker's words reported without quotation marks, with changes of tense, pronouns and time words.

> *She said **that she liked mangoes**.*

Lesson: [Reported speech (Class 11)](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-11/)

### Infinitive

The base form of a verb, usually with *to* (*to*-infinitive), sometimes without (bare infinitive). It is a non-finite verb.

> *I want **to go**.* / *Let me **go**.*

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

### Interjection

A word that shows a sudden feeling, such as surprise, joy or pain. It is usually followed by an exclamation mark.

> ***Wow!** We won.* / ***Alas!** He failed.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Interrogative sentence

A sentence that asks a question. It ends with a question mark.

> *Where do you live?*

Lesson: [Interrogatives (Class 12)](/topics/t10-questions/class-12/)

### Intransitive verb

A verb that does not take an object. It has no passive form.

> *The baby **slept**.* / *They **arrived** late.*

Lesson: [Active and passive voice (Class 11)](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-11/)

### Inversion

Putting the helping verb before the subject. It is used in questions, and in some formal statements.

> ***Is** she coming?* / ***Had I** known, I would have come.*

Lesson: [Conditional clauses (Class 12)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-12/)

### Irregular verb

A verb whose V2 or V3 does not end in *-ed*. Its forms must be learnt.

> *write – wrote – written*

Lesson: [Verb forms (V1 to V5)](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/) · List: [Irregular verbs](/reference/irregular-verbs/)

## L

### Latin comparatives

Adjectives from Latin ending in *-ior* (*senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior*). They take *to*, not *than*.

> *She is **senior to** me.*

Lesson: [Degrees of comparison](/topics/t08-degrees-of-comparison/class-12/)

### Linking adverb (conjunctive adverb)

A word that connects the ideas of two sentences or clauses, such as *however, therefore, moreover*. It needs a full stop or semicolon before it.

> *It was raining; **however**, we went out.*

Lesson: [Connectives and linkers](/topics/t06-connectives/class-12/)

### Linking verb

A verb that joins the subject to a word that describes or names it: *be, become, seem, look, feel, appear, remain*.

> *She **looks** tired.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

## M

### Main clause (independent clause)

A clause that makes complete sense on its own.

> ***I will call you** when I reach Chennai.*

Lesson: [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/)

### Main verb (lexical verb)

The verb that carries the meaning of the action or state.

> *She is **singing**.*

Lesson: [Auxiliary verbs](/foundation/f3-auxiliaries/)

### Material noun

A noun for a substance: *gold, water, rice, cotton*. Used in general, it takes no article.

> ***Gold** is a precious metal.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Mixed conditional

A conditional where the *if* clause and the main clause refer to different times.

> *If I **had taken** that job, I **would be** rich now.*

Lesson: [Conditional clauses (Class 12)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-12/)

### Modal auxiliary (modal verb)

A helping verb that adds meaning such as ability, permission, possibility or duty: *can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must, ought to*. It is followed by V1 and never takes *-s*.

> *You **can** go.* / *It **might** rain.*

Lesson: [Modal auxiliaries (Class 11)](/topics/t02-modal-auxiliaries/class-11/) · Chart: [Modals chart](/reference/modals-chart/)

### Modal perfect

The pattern modal + *have* + V3. It talks about the past.

> *She **must have forgotten**.* / *You **should have told** me.*

Lesson: [Modal auxiliaries (Class 12)](/topics/t02-modal-auxiliaries/class-12/)

### Modify

To add to the meaning of a word by describing or limiting it.

> *a **tall** boy* (*tall* modifies *boy*)

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

## N

### Narration

An older name for direct and indirect speech.

> *Change the narration: He said, "I am ready."* → *He said that he was ready.*

Lesson: [Reported speech (Class 11)](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-11/)

### Negative sentence

A sentence with *not* (*n't*) or a negative word such as *never, nobody, hardly*.

> *Ravi **never** smiles.*

Lesson: [Question tags (Class 11)](/topics/t10-questions/class-11/)

### Nominative absolute

A phrase made of a noun (or pronoun) + a participle, with its own subject, used to combine sentences that have different subjects.

> ***The sun having set**, we went home.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Non-finite verb

A verb form that does not show tense and does not change with the subject: the infinitive, the gerund and the participles.

> *She wants **to sing**.* / ***Singing** is fun.*

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

### Noun

A word that names a person, place, thing, animal or idea.

> *Karthik, Salem, cow, honesty*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Noun clause

A subordinate clause that does the job of a noun: subject, object or complement. You can usually replace it with *it*.

> *I know **that he is honest**.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Noun phrase

A phrase whose main word is a noun or pronoun, or a phrase that does the job of a noun.

> ***a beautiful red saree*** / *I like **to play chess**.*

Lesson: [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/)

## O

### Object

The person or thing that receives the action of the verb. See also *direct object* and *indirect object*.

> *The cat ate **the fish**.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Object complement

A word or group that comes after the object and describes or names it.

> *The news made her **happy**.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Object pronoun

The form of a pronoun used as an object or after a preposition: *me, you, him, her, it, us, them, whom*.

> *Come with **me**.* / *between you and **me***

Lesson: [Prepositions (Class 11)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-11/)

### Optative sentence

A sentence that expresses a wish or a prayer.

> *May God bless you!*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Ordinal number

A number that shows order: *first, second, third*. It usually takes *the* and comes before a cardinal number.

> ***the first two** chapters*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/)

## P

### Participle

A verb form used as an adjective or to make a short clause. The present participle is V4 (*running*), the past participle is V3 (*broken*), and the perfect participle is *having* + V3.

> *a **running** stream*, *a **broken** chair*

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

### Participle clause

A group of words that starts with a participle and works like a short clause.

> ***Seeing the police**, the thief ran away.*

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

### Parts of speech (word classes)

The groups that words belong to, decided by their job in a sentence: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection and determiner.

> *We sat at a **round** (adjective) table.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Passive voice

A sentence is in the passive voice when the subject **receives** the action. The verb is *be* + V3.

> *The window **was broken** by Ravi.*

Lesson: [Active and passive voice (Class 11)](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-11/) · Chart: [Passive voice table](/reference/passive-voice-table/)

### Past participle (V3)

The third form of a verb. It is used after *have* (perfect tenses), after *be* (passive), and as an adjective.

> *She has **written**.* / *a **broken** chair*

Lesson: [Verb forms (V1 to V5)](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/)

### Past simple form (V2)

The second form of a verb, used alone (with no helping verb) for the past simple tense.

> *She **wrote** a letter.*

Lesson: [Verb forms (V1 to V5)](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/)

### Perfect aspect

The aspect that shows an action as finished before a certain point. It is made with *have* + V3.

> *She **has finished**.* / *The train **had left**.*

Lesson: [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/)

### Perfect continuous aspect

The aspect that shows an action going on for a period up to a certain point. It is made with *have been* + V4.

> *She **has been waiting** since 8.*

Lesson: [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/)

### Perfect participle

*Having* + V3. It shows that one action was complete before another.

> ***Having finished** the work, he left.*

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

### Phrasal verb

A verb + a small word (*after, up, off, on*) that together have a new meaning. In the passive, the small word stays.

> *She **looks after** her grandmother.* / *The baby was **looked after**.*

Lesson: [Prepositions (Class 12)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-12/)

### Phrase

A group of words that works as one unit but has no subject + finite verb.

> ***in the morning*** / ***a very old temple***

Lesson: [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/)

### Positive degree

The plain form of an adjective or adverb, with no comparison. It is used with *as ... as*.

> *Meena is **as tall** as Priya.*

Lesson: [Degrees of comparison](/topics/t08-degrees-of-comparison/class-12/)

### Possessive determiner

*My, your, his, her, its, our, their*. It comes before a noun and shows who owns it. It never takes an article.

> ***our** school*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/)

### Possessive pronoun

*Mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs*. It stands alone, without a noun after it.

> *This pen is **mine**.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Predicate

Everything in a sentence except the subject.

> *Kavya **sings beautifully at every festival**.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Preposition

A word that shows how a noun or pronoun is related to another word, usually in place, time or direction. It is followed by a noun, an object pronoun or a V4.

> *The book is **on** the table.* / *He is good **at** swimming.*

Lesson: [Prepositions (Class 11)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-11/)

### Prepositional phrase

A preposition + its object (*in the morning, on the wall*). The term is also used for compound prepositions like *in spite of*.

> *We met **after lunch**.*

Lesson: [Prepositions (Class 11)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-11/)

### Present participle (V4)

The *-ing* form of a verb. It is used after *be* (continuous tenses), as an adjective, and to begin participle clauses.

> *They are **playing**.* / *a **sleeping** baby*

Lesson: [Verb forms (V1 to V5)](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/)

### Pronoun

A word used in place of a noun, so that the noun is not repeated.

> *Meena is my friend. **She** lives near **me**.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Proper noun

The name of one particular person, place or thing. It starts with a capital letter.

> *Kavya, Chennai, Pongal, the Kaveri*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

## Q

### Quantifier

A determiner that shows amount: *some, any, much, many, few, a few, little, a little, enough, several, all, both*.

> ***A few** students passed.*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/)

### Question tag (tag question)

A short question added to the end of a statement, to ask for agreement or to check.

> *Priya is your sister, **isn't she**?*

Lesson: [Question tags (Class 11)](/topics/t10-questions/class-11/) · Chart: [Question tag rules](/reference/question-tag-rules/)

### Question word (interrogative word)

A word that begins a wh- question: *what, which, who, whom, whose, when, where, why, how*.

> ***Where** do you live?*

Lesson: [Interrogatives (Class 12)](/topics/t10-questions/class-12/)

## R

### Reflexive pronoun

A pronoun ending in *-self* or *-selves*, used when the subject and object are the same person.

> *Arun hurt **himself**.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Regular verb

A verb whose V2 and V3 end in *-ed*.

> *walk – walked – walked*

Lesson: [Verb forms (V1 to V5)](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/)

### Relative pronoun

*Who, whom, whose, which, that*. It begins an adjective clause and refers to a noun before it.

> *the girl **who** won the prize*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Reported speech

See *indirect speech*.

> *He told me that he was busy.*

Lesson: [Reported speech (Class 11)](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-11/) · Chart: [Reported speech changes](/reference/reported-speech-changes/)

### Reporting verb

The verb that introduces direct or reported speech: *said, told, asked, ordered, suggested, apologised*.

> *She **asked** if I was ready.*

Lesson: [Reported speech (Class 12)](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-12/)

### Rhetorical question

A question asked for effect, where the answer is obvious. It is used when changing assertive sentences into interrogative ones.

> *Who does not love a holiday?* (= Everyone loves a holiday.)

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

## S

### Semi-modal

An expression that works like a modal but is formed differently: *have to, need to, be able to, used to, ought to, had better, be supposed to*.

> *I **have to** catch the bus.*

Lesson: [Modal auxiliaries (Class 12)](/topics/t02-modal-auxiliaries/class-12/)

### Sentence

A group of words that makes complete sense, with a subject and a finite verb.

> *Ravi plays cricket.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Sentence pattern

The order of the main parts of a sentence: SV, SVO, SVC, SVA, SVOO, SVOC, SVOA.

> *Amma (S) gave (V) me (O) a pen (O).* → SVOO

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Sequence of tenses

The rule that when the main verb is in the past, the verb in the subordinate clause is usually in the past too.

> *He **said** that he **was** tired.*

Lesson: [Tenses (Class 12)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-12/)

### Signal words

Words in a sentence that point to a tense, such as *yesterday* (past simple), *now* (present continuous) or *since* (present perfect).

> *I have lived here **since** 2015.*

Lesson: [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/)

### Simple aspect

The aspect that shows an action as a fact, a habit or a complete event, with no helping verb in the positive (present and past).

> *She **writes** every day.* / *She **wrote** yesterday.*

Lesson: [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/)

### Simple sentence

A sentence with only one finite verb (one independent clause).

> ***Being** ill, he **stayed** at home.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Split infinitive

An infinitive with a word placed between *to* and the verb. It is accepted in modern English, but many writers avoid it in formal writing.

> *to **clearly** explain* (formal: *to explain clearly*)

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

### Statement

A sentence that tells something (an assertive sentence).

> *Ravi is tall.*

Lesson: [Question tags (Class 11)](/topics/t10-questions/class-11/)

### Stative verb

A verb that shows a state rather than an action, such as *know, believe, like, own, belong*. It is not normally used in the continuous tenses.

> *I **know** the answer.* (not *~~am knowing~~*)

Lesson: [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/)

### Subject

The person or thing that does the action, or that the sentence is about. Ask "Who or what + verb?"

> ***Kavya** sings.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Subject complement

A word or group after a linking verb that describes or names the subject.

> *Meena is **a doctor**.* / *The soup tastes **delicious**.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Subject question

A question in which the question word is the subject. It does not use *do / does / did*.

> ***Who told** you?* / ***What happened**?*

Lesson: [Interrogatives (Class 12)](/topics/t10-questions/class-12/)

### Subordinate clause (dependent clause)

A clause that cannot stand alone and depends on the main clause. It usually begins with a subordinating word.

> *I will call you **when I reach Chennai**.*

Lesson: [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/)

### Subordinating conjunction

A conjunction that joins a subordinate clause to a main clause: *because, although, when, if, unless, so that, that*.

> *We stayed home **because** it was raining.*

Lesson: [Connectives and linkers](/topics/t06-connectives/class-12/)

### Superlative degree

The form of an adjective or adverb that compares one with all others in a group. It usually ends in *-est* or uses *most*, with *the*.

> *Mount Everest is **the highest** mountain.*

Lesson: [Degrees of comparison](/topics/t08-degrees-of-comparison/class-12/)

### Syllable

A beat in a word. It decides whether a word takes *-er / -est* or *more / most*.

> *tall* (1 syllable), *hap-py* (2), *beau-ti-ful* (3)

Lesson: [Degrees of comparison](/topics/t08-degrees-of-comparison/class-12/)

### Synthesis

Combining two or more sentences into one.

> *He saw the police. He ran away.* → ***Seeing** the police, he ran away.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

## T

### Tense

The form of a verb that shows time (present, past, future) and aspect (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous). English has 12 tenses.

> *She **has written**.* (present perfect)

Lesson: [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/) · Chart: [Tense table](/reference/tense-table/)

### To-infinitive

*To* + V1. It can be a subject, an object or a complement, and it shows purpose.

> *He came **to help**.*

Lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/)

### Transformation

Changing the form of a sentence without changing its meaning (for example, simple to complex, or affirmative to negative).

> *As he was ill, he stayed home.* → *Being ill, he stayed home.*

Lesson: [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/)

### Transitive verb

A verb that takes an object. Only transitive verbs have a passive.

> *Arun **bought** a bike.*

Lesson: [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/)

### Types 0, 1, 2 and 3 (conditionals)

The four main kinds of conditional sentences. Type 0 is for facts, Type 1 for likely situations, Type 2 for imaginary present or unlikely future, and Type 3 for the imaginary past.

> *If I **had** studied, I **would have** passed.* (Type 3)

Lesson: [Conditional clauses (Class 11)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-11/) · Chart: [Conditionals chart](/reference/conditionals-chart/)

## U

### Uncountable noun

A noun that cannot be counted and has no plural, such as *water, milk, advice, information, furniture*. It does not take *a / an*.

> *Can you give me **some advice**?*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 11)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-11/)

### Universal truth

A fact that is always true. It stays in the present simple, even in reported speech after a past verb.

> *The teacher said that the earth **goes** round the sun.*

Lesson: [Reported speech (Class 11)](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-11/)

### Unless

A condition word that means *if ... not*. It is followed by a positive verb.

> ***Unless** you hurry, you will miss the bus.*

Lesson: [Conditional clauses (Class 12)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-12/)

## V

### V1, V2, V3, V4, V5

The five forms of a verb: V1 base form (*write*), V2 past simple (*wrote*), V3 past participle (*written*), V4 *-ing* form (*writing*), V5 *-s* form (*writes*).

> *She has **written** (V3) three letters.*

Lesson: [Verb forms (V1 to V5)](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/)

### Verb

A word that shows an action or a state. Every sentence needs at least one.

> *The children **are playing**.* / *Divya **knows** the answer.*

Lesson: [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/)

### Verb phrase

The helping verb(s) + the main verb, working together as one verb.

> *She **has been waiting** for an hour.*

Lesson: [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/)

### Voice

The form of the verb that shows whether the subject does the action (active) or receives it (passive).

> *Active: Ravi broke the window.* / *Passive: The window was broken.*

Lesson: [Active and passive voice (Class 11)](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-11/)

### Vowel sound

The sounds of *a, e, i, o, u* as in *apple, egg, ink, orange, umbrella*. *An* is used before a vowel sound, even if the letter is a consonant.

> ***an** hour* (the *h* is silent)

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 11)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-11/)

## W

### Wh- question

A question that begins with a question word and asks for information.

> ***Where** does Ravi live?*

Lesson: [Interrogatives (Class 12)](/topics/t10-questions/class-12/)

## Y

### Yes/no question

A question that can be answered with *yes* or *no*. It begins with a helping verb.

> ***Is** Ravi at home?*

Lesson: [Interrogatives (Class 12)](/topics/t10-questions/class-12/)

## Z

### Zero article

Using no article before a noun, for example with meals, languages, games, most names, and plural or uncountable nouns in general.

> *We have **breakfast** at 7.* / ***Dogs** are loyal.*

Lesson: [Articles and determiners (Class 11)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-11/)
