## What you will learn

- The three **primary auxiliaries**: *be*, *have* and *do*
- What each one does: continuous tenses, perfect tenses, passive voice, questions and negatives
- How the same three verbs also work as **main verbs** (*He **has** a car* vs *He **has** gone*)
- How to make any sentence negative or into a question, using the first auxiliary
- A short preview of **modal auxiliaries** (*can, will, must...*)

## Why this matters

Helping verbs are everywhere. You need them to make tenses, to make questions, to make negatives, to change into the passive voice and to add question tags.

The exam often asks you to "fill in with *is / are / was / were / has / have / do / does / did*". Other exercises, such as question tags and framing questions, depend on finding the auxiliary in the sentence.

## The idea in simple words

Look at these sentences.

> *Ravi **is** reading.*
> *Ravi **has** read the book.*
> *Ravi **does** not read comics.*

In each sentence, *read* (or *reading*) carries the meaning. The words in bold come before it and **help** it. They show time, they show whether the action is going on or finished, and they help us make negatives.

These helping words are called **auxiliary verbs**. *Auxiliary* means "helping".

Now look at these:

> *Ravi **is** a student.*
> *Ravi **has** a bicycle.*
> *Ravi **does** his homework.*

Here *is, has* and *does* are the only verbs. They carry the meaning themselves. So they are **main verbs** in these sentences.

## Grammar words you need

**Main verb (lexical verb):** the verb that carries the meaning of the action or state. In *She is **singing***, the main verb is *singing*.

**Auxiliary verb (helping verb):** a verb that comes before the main verb and helps it. In ***She is** singing*, *is* is the auxiliary.

**Primary auxiliaries:** *be* (am, is, are, was, were, be, been, being), *have* (have, has, had) and *do* (do, does, did). They can also be main verbs.

**Modal auxiliaries (modals):** *can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must, ought to*. They add meanings like ability, permission or duty. They are always helpers, never main verbs.

**Verb phrase:** the auxiliary (or auxiliaries) plus the main verb: *has been working*, *will be going*.

**V1 to V5:** the five forms of a verb. See [Verb forms](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/).

## The rules

### Rule 1: *Be* + V4 makes the continuous tenses.

> *I **am writing**.* (present continuous)
> *They **were playing**.* (past continuous)
> *She **will be travelling**.* (future continuous)

Choose the form of *be* by the subject and the time:

| Subject | Present | Past |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | was |
| he / she / it / one person or thing | is | was |
| we / you / they / more than one | are | were |

### Rule 2: *Be* + V3 makes the passive voice.

> *The letter **is written** by Kavya.*
> *The match **was won** by Chennai.*
> *The work **has been done**.*

Full lesson: [Active and passive voice (Class 11)](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-11/).

### Rule 3: *Have* + V3 makes the perfect tenses.

> *She **has finished** her work.* (present perfect)
> *The train **had left**.* (past perfect)
> *We **will have reached** by noon.* (future perfect)

Use *has* with *he / she / it* and one person or thing. Use *have* with *I / you / we / they* and more than one. Use *had* with every subject in the past.

### Rule 4: *Do* helps make questions and negatives when there is no other auxiliary.

A sentence in the **present simple** or **past simple** has no auxiliary:

> *Arun **plays** cricket.*
> *Arun **played** cricket.*

To make a question or a negative, we bring in *do, does* or *did*. The main verb then goes back to **V1**.

| Statement | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| *They **play** cricket.* | *They **do not play** cricket.* | ***Do** they **play** cricket?* |
| *Arun **plays** cricket.* | *Arun **does not play** cricket.* | ***Does** Arun **play** cricket?* |
| *Arun **played** cricket.* | *Arun **did not play** cricket.* | ***Did** Arun **play** cricket?* |

Notice: *plays* and *played* become *play*. The *-s* and the past time move into *does* and *did*.

### Rule 5: To make a negative, put *not* after the first auxiliary.

> *She **is** coming.* → *She **is not** coming.*
> *They **have** been waiting.* → *They **have not** been waiting.*
> *You **should** go.* → *You **should not** go.*
> *He **will** have left.* → *He **will not** have left.*

When there are two or more auxiliaries, *not* goes after the **first** one.

### Rule 6: To make a yes/no question, move the first auxiliary before the subject.

> *She **is** coming.* → ***Is** she coming?*
> *They **have** been waiting.* → ***Have** they been waiting?*
> *Priya **can** swim.* → ***Can** Priya swim?*

Only the first auxiliary moves. The rest stay after the subject.

Full lesson on questions: [Interrogatives (Class 12)](/topics/t10-questions/class-12/).

### Rule 7: *Be, have* and *do* can also be main verbs.

| Verb | As an auxiliary | As a main verb |
|---|---|---|
| be | *She **is** dancing.* | *She **is** a dancer.* (= exists as; links subject and description) |
| have | *He **has** gone.* | *He **has** a car.* (= owns) / *We **had** lunch.* (= ate) |
| do | *I **do** not know.* | *I **do** my homework.* (= complete, perform) |

**How to tell them apart:** look for another verb after it. If a V3 or V4 follows (*has **gone***, *is **dancing***), it is an auxiliary. If no other verb follows, it is the main verb.

### Rule 8: A main verb *have* or *do* needs the auxiliary *do* for questions and negatives.

> *He **has** a car.* → ***Does** he **have** a car?* / *He **does not have** a car.*
> *She **did** her homework.* → ***Did** she **do** her homework?*

You will see *do* twice in a sentence like *Did she do it?* That is correct: the first *did* is the auxiliary, the second *do* is the main verb.

A main verb *be* is different. It never uses *do*. It moves itself:

> *He **is** at home.* → ***Is** he at home?* (not *~~Does he be at home?~~*)

### Rule 9: Modal auxiliaries are always helpers, and they are followed by V1.

*Can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must* and *ought to* are **modal auxiliaries**.

> *You **can** go now.*
> *It **might** rain.*
> *We **must** finish this today.*

They are different from *be, have* and *do* in four ways:

| Feature | Primary auxiliaries | Modals |
|---|---|---|
| Can be a main verb? | Yes (*She has a car*) | No |
| Change for the subject? | Yes (*is / are, has / have, does / do*) | No (*he can, they can*) |
| Form after them | V3 or V4 (be, have), V1 (do) | always V1 |
| Add meaning? | Mostly grammar (time, question, negative) | Yes (ability, permission, duty, possibility...) |

Full lesson: [Modal auxiliaries (Class 11)](/topics/t02-modal-auxiliaries/class-11/).

## Special cases

### 1. Short forms (contractions)

In speech and informal writing, auxiliaries join with *not* or with the subject:

| Long form | Short form |
|---|---|
| is not, are not, was not, were not | isn't, aren't, wasn't, weren't |
| has not, have not, had not | hasn't, haven't, hadn't |
| do not, does not, did not | don't, doesn't, didn't |
| will not, shall not, cannot | **won't**, **shan't**, **can't** |
| I am, she is, they are, we have, he will | I'm, she's, they're, we've, he'll |

*Am not* has no short form *~~amn't~~* in standard English. Use *I'm not*. (In question tags, *I am* takes *aren't I?*)

*She's* can mean *she is* or *she has*. Look at the next word: *She's **going*** (= is), *She's **gone*** (= has).

### 2. *Do* for emphasis

In a positive sentence, *do / does / did* + V1 adds strong feeling:

> *I **do** like your new house!*
> *He **did** call you. I saw him.*

This is correct and common in speech.

### 3. *Have got* (British English)

In British English, *have got* means the same as *have* (own):

> *I've **got** a new phone.* = *I **have** a new phone.*
> ***Have** you **got** a pen?* = ***Do** you **have** a pen?*

Here *have* is the auxiliary, so questions and negatives use *have*, not *do*.

### 4. Two auxiliaries in one sentence

A verb phrase can have two or three auxiliaries:

> *She **has been** working.* (*has* + *been*)
> *The road **will have been** finished.* (*will* + *have* + *been*)

The **first** one is the one that moves in questions, takes *not*, and is used in question tags.

### 5. *Be* as a main verb uses no *do*, even in the past

> *They **were** late.* → ***Were** they late?* / *They **were not** late.*

Never *~~Did they be late?~~*

### 6. The imperative uses *do* even with *be*

Commands (imperatives) are the one place where *be* takes *do*:

> ***Don't be** late.*
> ***Do be** careful.* (emphasis)

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Does she ~~likes~~ coffee?* | ***Does** she **like** coffee?* | After *does*, use V1. The *-s* has moved to *does*. |
| *He ~~didn't went~~ home.* | *He **didn't go** home.* | After *did*, use V1. |
| *~~Do~~ you ~~be~~ happy?* | ***Are** you happy?* | The main verb *be* moves itself to make questions. It does not use *do*. |
| *She ~~don't~~ eat meat.* | *She **doesn't** eat meat.* | With *she*, the present form is *does*. |
| *They ~~was~~ playing.* | *They **were** playing.* | *They* takes *were*, not *was*. |
| *He ~~have~~ finished.* | *He **has** finished.* | *He* takes *has*. |
| *I ~~amn't~~ ready.* | *I'**m not** ready.* | There is no *amn't* in standard English. |
| *~~Has~~ he a car?* (modern speech) | ***Does** he **have** a car?* / ***Has** he **got** a car?* | *Has he a car?* is old-fashioned. With main-verb *have*, use *do*, or use *have got*. |
| *He can ~~swims~~.* | *He can **swim**.* | After a modal, always V1. Modals do not add *-s*. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Fill in the blanks** with *is / am / are / was / were / has / have / had / do / does / did*.
- **Change into negative** or **into a question**.
- **Identify** whether the underlined verb is an auxiliary or a main verb.
- Used inside other exercises: question tags, framing questions, voice and tenses.

Follow these steps to fill in an auxiliary:

1. **Find the subject.** Is it *I*, one person or thing, or more than one?
2. **Find the time.** Look for words like *now, yesterday, every day, since, already*.
3. **Look at the main verb after the blank.**
   - V4 (*-ing*) → a form of *be*.
   - V3 → *have* (perfect) or *be* (passive). Check the meaning.
   - V1, in a question or negative → *do / does / did*.
4. **Match the form** to the subject and the time.
5. **Read the whole sentence** again.

### Worked example 1

> *The children ______ playing in the ground now.*

1. Subject: *the children* (more than one).
2. Time: *now* → present.
3. Main verb: *playing* (V4) → a form of *be*.
4. Present, more than one → *are*.

**Answer:** *The children **are** playing in the ground now.*

### Worked example 2

> *______ your father work in Chennai?*

1. Subject: *your father* (one person).
2. Time: present (no past word; *work* is V1 in a question).
3. Main verb after the subject: *work* (V1) in a question → *do / does / did*.
4. Present, one person → *does*.

**Answer:** ***Does** your father work in Chennai?*

### Worked example 3

> *Meena ______ already finished her project.*

1. Subject: *Meena* (one person).
2. Time: *already* → a finished action connected to now (present perfect).
3. Main verb: *finished* (V3), and the action is done by Meena (not to her) → *have*.
4. One person → *has*.

**Answer:** *Meena **has** already finished her project.*

### Worked example 4

> *Change into a question: They have been living in Salem for ten years.*

1. Auxiliaries: *have* and *been*. The first is *have*.
2. Move *have* before the subject *they*.

**Answer:** ***Have** they been living in Salem for ten years?*

## Quick check

1. *I ______ not like loud music.*
2. *The thieves ______ caught by the police last night.*
3. Is *has* an auxiliary or a main verb? *Divya **has** two brothers.*
4. Make negative: *He went to school yesterday.*
5. *______ you seen my book?*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***do.*** A negative with V1 (*like*) needs *do / does / did*. Present with *I* → *do*.
2. ***were.*** The thieves did not do the catching; it was done to them. That is the passive: *be* + V3. Past, more than one → *were*.
3. **Main verb.** No other verb follows *has*. It means "owns" or "is related to".
4. ***He did not go to school yesterday.*** No auxiliary in the statement, so add *did*. The main verb goes back to V1 (*went → go*).
5. ***Have.*** *Seen* is V3 and the action is done by *you*, so use *have*. *You* takes *have*.

</details>

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- Primary auxiliaries: *be* (+ V4 continuous, + V3 passive), *have* (+ V3 perfect), *do* (+ V1 questions and negatives).
- Negative: *not* after the first auxiliary. Question: first auxiliary before the subject.
- No auxiliary? Use *do / does / did*, and change the main verb to V1.
- *Be, have, do* can be main verbs. If no other verb follows, it is the main verb.
- Modals (*can, must, will...*) are always helpers, never change for the subject, and take V1.
:::
