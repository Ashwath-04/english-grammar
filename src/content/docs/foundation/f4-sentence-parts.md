## What you will learn

- How to find the **subject** and the **main verb** of any sentence
- What a **direct object** and an **indirect object** are
- What a **subject complement** and an **object complement** are
- What an **adverbial** is
- The basic **sentence patterns**: SV, SVO, SVC, SVA, SVOO, SVOC and SVOA
- How to answer "Identify the sentence pattern"

## Why this matters

Many grammar topics depend on these parts:

- **Subject–verb agreement:** you must find the real subject before choosing *is* or *are*.
- **Passive voice:** the **object** of the active sentence becomes the subject. A sentence with no object has no passive.
- **Question tags and questions:** the tag uses a pronoun for the **subject**.
- **Sentence patterns** are sometimes asked directly: "Identify the pattern of this sentence."

## The idea in simple words

A sentence is like a small story. It answers some simple questions.

> ***Ravi** **gave** **his sister** **a book** **yesterday**.*

| Question | Answer | Name of the part |
|---|---|---|
| Who did something? | Ravi | Subject (S) |
| What did he do? | gave | Verb (V) |
| To whom? | his sister | Indirect object (IO) |
| What did he give? | a book | Direct object (DO) |
| When? | yesterday | Adverbial (A) |

Every English sentence is built from a few of these parts, in a fairly fixed order. Once you can find them, you can understand and correct almost any sentence.

## Grammar words you need

**Subject (S):** the person or thing that does the action, or that the sentence is about. ***Kavya** sings.*

**Predicate:** everything in the sentence except the subject. *Kavya **sings beautifully at every festival**.*

**Verb (V):** the word or group of words that shows action or state. In a sentence pattern, V means the whole verb phrase: *sings*, *has been singing*.

**Transitive verb:** a verb that needs an object: *Arun **bought** a bike.* (bought what?)

**Intransitive verb:** a verb that does not take an object: *The baby **slept**.*

**Linking verb:** a verb that joins the subject to a word that describes or names it: *be, become, seem, look, feel, appear, grow, turn, remain*. *She **looks** tired.*

**Object (O):** the person or thing that receives the action. It usually comes after the verb.

**Complement (C):** a word or group that completes the meaning by describing or naming the subject or the object.

**Adverbial (A):** a word or group that tells **when, where, how, why** or **how often**: *yesterday, in Chennai, slowly, because of the rain*.

## The rules

### Rule 1: Every sentence has a subject and a verb.

> ***Birds** **fly**.*
> ***The match** **has started**.*

A sentence can be very short, but it needs both. *Flying in the sky* is not a sentence: it has no subject and no full verb.

The one exception is a **command** (imperative). The subject *you* is understood:

> ***Sit** down.* (= *You sit down.*)

### Rule 2: Find the verb first, then ask "Who or what + verb?" to find the subject.

> *The old man in the white shirt **sells** flowers.*

1. Verb: *sells*.
2. Who sells? *The old man in the white shirt*.

So the subject is the whole group *The old man in the white shirt*. The main word in the subject is *man*. This main word decides the verb form (*man sells*).

### Rule 3: The direct object receives the action. Ask "Verb + what?" or "Verb + whom?"

> *Priya **wrote** **a letter**.* (wrote what? *a letter*)
> *The teacher **praised** **Karthik**.* (praised whom? *Karthik*)

If there is an answer, the verb is **transitive** and the answer is the **direct object**.

If there is no answer, the verb is **intransitive**:

> *The child **laughed**.* (laughed what? No answer. No object.)

### Rule 4: The indirect object is the person who receives the direct object. It comes before the direct object.

Some verbs, like *give, send, show, tell, buy, make, teach, offer, lend*, can take two objects.

> *Amma **made** **me** **a cake**.*
> *The coach **showed** **the players** **the video**.*

The indirect object (*me*, *the players*) is a person, and it comes **first**. The direct object (*a cake*, *the video*) is the thing.

You can move the indirect object to the end with *to* or *for*:

> *Amma made a cake **for me**.*
> *The coach showed the video **to the players**.*

### Rule 5: A subject complement comes after a linking verb and describes or names the subject.

> *Meena **is** **a doctor**.* (names Meena)
> *The soup **tastes** **delicious**.* (describes the soup)
> *He **became** **the captain**.* (names him)
> *The leaves **turned** **yellow**.*

The subject complement is the same person or thing as the subject. *Meena = a doctor*. An object is a different person or thing: in *Meena met a doctor*, Meena and the doctor are two people.

### Rule 6: An object complement comes after the object and describes or names the object.

> *The news **made** **her** **happy**.* (her = happy)
> *They **elected** **Arun** **class leader**.* (Arun = class leader)
> *We **painted** **the wall** **blue**.* (the wall = blue)
> *I **found** **the book** **interesting**.*

Verbs that often take an object complement: *make, elect, appoint, call, name, find, keep, paint, consider*.

### Rule 7: An adverbial tells when, where, how or why. It is usually optional, but some verbs need one.

Most adverbials can be removed and the sentence is still complete:

> *We played cricket **in the evening**.* → *We played cricket.* (still complete)

Some verbs need an adverbial to be complete. Common ones are *be* (of place), *live, stay, go, lie, put, keep, place*.

> *My uncle **lives** **in Coimbatore**.* (*My uncle lives* is incomplete.)
> *The keys **are** **on the table**.*
> *She **put** the book **on the shelf**.*

In sentence patterns, we only mark an **A** when the adverbial is needed.

### Rule 8: English sentences follow a few basic patterns.

| Pattern | Parts | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **SV** | Subject + Verb | *The baby / slept.* |
| **SVO** | Subject + Verb + Object | *Kavya / plays / the veena.* |
| **SVC** | Subject + Verb + Complement | *Ravi / is / a teacher.* |
| **SVA** | Subject + Verb + Adverbial | *My uncle / lives / in Madurai.* |
| **SVOO** | Subject + Verb + Indirect object + Direct object | *Amma / gave / me / a pen.* |
| **SVOC** | Subject + Verb + Object + Complement | *They / made / him / captain.* |
| **SVOA** | Subject + Verb + Object + Adverbial | *She / put / the plate / on the table.* |

Extra adverbials (*yesterday, slowly, at the station*) can be added to any pattern. Many textbooks write them in brackets, for example *SVO(A)*: *Kavya plays the veena **every evening**.*

## Special cases

### 1. The same verb in different patterns

Many verbs can be transitive in one sentence and intransitive in another.

> *Divya **sings**.* (SV)
> *Divya **sings** **a song**.* (SVO)
> *The bell **rang**.* (SV) / *He **rang** **the bell**.* (SVO)

Some can even be linking verbs:

> *She **grew** tomatoes.* (SVO) / *She **grew** **tired**.* (SVC)
> *He **looked** carefully.* (SV) / *He **looked** **worried**.* (SVC)

Always decide by **this** sentence.

### 2. The subject after the verb: *there is* and questions

In a sentence starting with *There is / There are*, the real subject comes **after** the verb.

> *There **are** **two mangoes** in the basket.* (real subject: *two mangoes*)

In a question, the subject comes after the first auxiliary.

> *Has **your sister** finished her exam?* (subject: *your sister*)

### 3. The subject can be a long group, a gerund or a clause

> ***The boys in the last bench** are noisy.*
> ***Swimming** keeps you fit.* (a gerund as subject)
> ***What he said** surprised everyone.* (a clause as subject)

### 4. *It* as an empty subject

English sentences need a subject, even when there is no real "doer". We use *it*:

> ***It** is raining.*
> ***It** is ten o'clock.*
> ***It** is easy to learn English.* (Here *it* stands for *to learn English*.)

### 5. Object or complement?

After *be* and other linking verbs, the noun is a **complement**, not an object.

> *He **became** a doctor.* (complement: he = a doctor)
> *He **met** a doctor.* (object: he ≠ a doctor)

**Test:** can you make it passive? *A doctor was met by him* works, so *a doctor* is an object. *~~A doctor was become by him~~* is impossible, so it is a complement.

### 6. Verb + preposition

Some verbs are followed by a preposition before their object: *look at, listen to, wait for, depend on*.

> *The children **listened to** the story.*

Most school grammars treat *listened to* as the verb and *the story* as the object (SVO), because the passive is possible: *The story was listened to*.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *The box of mangoes **~~are~~** heavy.* | *The box of mangoes **is** heavy.* | The subject's main word is *box* (singular), not *mangoes*. |
| *She is a teacher.* → "SVO" | **SVC** | *A teacher* is the same person as *she*. After *is*, it is a complement, not an object. |
| *My uncle lives in Salem.* → "SV" | **SVA** | *Lives* needs a place to be complete. *In Salem* is a needed adverbial. |
| *They made him captain.* → "SVOO" | **SVOC** | *Him* and *captain* are the same person. *Captain* is the object complement. |
| *Amma gave me a pen.* → "SVOC" | **SVOO** | *Me* and *a pen* are different. *Me* is the indirect object, *a pen* the direct object. |
| *There is many reasons.* | *There **are** many reasons.* | After *there*, the verb agrees with the real subject (*reasons*). |
| *The baby slept soundly.* → "SVA" | **SV** (with an optional adverbial) | *Soundly* can be removed: *The baby slept* is complete. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Identify the sentence pattern** (SV, SVO, SVC, SVA, SVOO, SVOC, SVOA).
- **Label the parts** of a sentence: subject, verb, object, complement, adverbial.
- **Frame sentences** in a given pattern.
- Hidden in other exercises: subject–verb agreement, passive voice.

Follow these steps for "Identify the sentence pattern":

1. **Find the verb** (the whole verb phrase).
2. **Find the subject:** ask "Who or what + verb?"
3. **Ask "Verb + what / whom?"** If there is an answer and it is a different person or thing from the subject, it is an **object**.
4. **If the verb is a linking verb** (*be, become, seem, look...*), the word after it is a **complement**.
5. **If there are two words after the verb**, check: two different things → **OO**; object + its description → **OC**; object + needed place → **OA**.
6. **Remove extra adverbials.** Keep only the parts the sentence needs.
7. **Write the pattern**, with the parts marked.

### Worked example 1

> *The students elected Divya their leader.*

1. Verb: *elected*.
2. Who elected? *The students* → S.
3. Elected whom? *Divya* → O.
4. *Their leader*: is it a different person? No. Divya = their leader → C (object complement).

**Answer:** *The students (S) / elected (V) / Divya (O) / their leader (C)* → **SVOC**.

### Worked example 2

> *My grandfather told us a story last night.*

1. Verb: *told*.
2. Who told? *My grandfather* → S.
3. Told what? *a story* → DO. Told whom? *us* → IO. *Us* and *a story* are different.
4. *Last night* is an extra adverbial; the sentence is complete without it.

**Answer:** *My grandfather (S) / told (V) / us (IO) / a story (DO) / last night (A)* → **SVOO** (with an optional adverbial).

### Worked example 3

> *The festival seemed very colourful.*

1. Verb: *seemed* (a linking verb).
2. What seemed? *The festival* → S.
3. *Very colourful* describes the festival → C (subject complement).

**Answer:** *The festival (S) / seemed (V) / very colourful (C)* → **SVC**.

### Worked example 4

> *Please keep the milk in the fridge.*

1. Verb: *keep*. It is a command, so the subject *you* is understood.
2. Keep what? *the milk* → O.
3. *In the fridge*: can we remove it? *Please keep the milk* changes the meaning (keep it where?). With *keep* in this sense, the place is needed → A.

**Answer:** *(You) (S) / keep (V) / the milk (O) / in the fridge (A)* → **SVOA**.

## Quick check

Write the pattern of each sentence.

1. *The sun rises.*
2. *Arun bought a new bat.*
3. *The teacher looked angry.*
4. *Grandma sent me a parcel.*
5. *The guests are in the hall.*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. **SV.** *Rises* takes no object and needs nothing else.
2. **SVO.** Bought what? *a new bat* (a different thing from Arun).
3. **SVC.** *Looked* is a linking verb here. *Angry* describes the teacher.
4. **SVOO.** *Me* (indirect object, the person) and *a parcel* (direct object, the thing).
5. **SVA.** *Are* needs the place *in the hall*. *The guests are* is not complete.

</details>

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- Find the **verb** first, then the **subject** (who / what + verb?).
- **Object:** verb + what / whom? A different person or thing from the subject.
- **Complement:** after a linking verb (*be, become, seem, look*), the same as the subject; or after an object, the same as the object.
- **Adverbial:** when, where, how, why. Mark **A** only when it is needed.
- Patterns: **SV, SVO, SVC, SVA, SVOO, SVOC, SVOA**.
:::
