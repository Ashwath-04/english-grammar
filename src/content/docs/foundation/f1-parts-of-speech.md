## What you will learn

- The nine parts of speech: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection and determiner
- How to find each one by the **job** it does in the sentence, not by memorising lists
- Why the same word can be a different part of speech in different sentences (*round*, *fast*, *before*, *that*)
- How to answer "Identify the part of speech of the underlined word" step by step

## Why this matters

Every other grammar topic uses these names. Tenses talk about **verbs**. Articles are **determiners**. Degrees of comparison are about **adjectives** and **adverbs**. If the names are clear, every later lesson becomes easier.

The exam sometimes asks you to name the part of speech of a word. More often, it expects you to know the names so that you can follow a rule, such as "an adjective comes before a noun" or "a preposition is followed by a noun or a gerund".

## The idea in simple words

Think of a cricket team. One player bowls, one keeps wicket, others field. You know a player's role by **what they do on the field**, not by their name.

Words work the same way. Each word in a sentence has a job.

> ***Priya** **quickly** **wrote** **a** **long** **letter** **to** **her** **grandmother** **in** **Madurai**.*

| Word | Its job | Part of speech |
|---|---|---|
| Priya | names a person | noun |
| quickly | tells *how* she wrote | adverb |
| wrote | tells the action | verb |
| a | points to the noun *letter* | determiner (article) |
| long | describes the letter | adjective |
| letter | names a thing | noun |
| to | shows the link between *letter* and *grandmother* | preposition |
| her | shows whose grandmother | determiner (possessive) |
| grandmother | names a person | noun |
| in | shows the place | preposition |
| Madurai | names a place | noun |

Now look at one word in two sentences:

> *We sat at a **round** table.* (describes the table: adjective)
> *The car went **round** the corner.* (shows position, before a noun: preposition)

The word is the same. The job is different, so the part of speech is different.

## Grammar words you need

**Part of speech (word class):** the group a word belongs to, decided by the job it does in a sentence. *Noun* and *verb* are parts of speech.

**Sentence:** a group of words that makes complete sense. It has a subject and a verb. *Ravi plays cricket.*

**Subject:** the person or thing the sentence is about. In *Ravi plays cricket*, the subject is *Ravi*.

**Modify:** to add to the meaning of a word, by describing it or limiting it. In *a tall boy*, the word *tall* modifies *boy*.

## The rules

### Rule 1: A noun names a person, place, thing, animal or idea.

> ***Karthik** lives in **Salem**.* (person, place)
> *The **cow** is eating **grass**.* (animal, thing)
> ***Honesty** is the best **policy**.* (ideas)

Kinds of nouns you will meet:

| Kind | What it names | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Proper noun | one particular person, place or thing (capital letter) | Kavya, Chennai, Pongal, the Kaveri |
| Common noun | any member of a group | girl, city, festival, river |
| Collective noun | a group seen as one | team, family, class, committee |
| Abstract noun | an idea, quality or feeling you cannot touch | kindness, fear, freedom, childhood |
| Material noun | a substance | gold, water, rice, cotton |

**How to test:** can you put *a*, *an* or *the* in front of it, or make it plural? Then it is probably a noun: *a city, the cities*. (Proper, abstract and material nouns often do not take *a*, so use the job test too.)

### Rule 2: A pronoun takes the place of a noun.

We use pronouns so that we do not repeat the noun again and again.

> *Meena is my friend. **She** lives near **me**.*

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Personal | I, me, you, he, him, she, her, it, we, us, they, them |
| Possessive | mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs |
| Reflexive | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves |
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those (when used alone) |
| Interrogative | who, whom, whose, what, which (in questions) |
| Relative | who, whom, whose, which, that (joining clauses) |
| Indefinite | someone, anybody, everything, nobody, each, all, few |

> *This pen is **mine**.* (*mine* stands for *my pen*)
> *Arun hurt **himself**.*
> ***Who** broke the glass?*

### Rule 3: A verb shows an action or a state.

Most verbs show an **action**: *run, eat, write, play*. Some show a **state** (how something is): *be, seem, know, belong, have*.

> *The children **are playing** in the ground.* (action)
> *Divya **knows** the answer.* (state)
> *The soup **seems** cold.* (state)

Every sentence needs at least one verb. A verb can be one word (*plays*) or a group of words (*has been playing*). The group is made of **helping verbs** plus a **main verb**. See [Auxiliary verbs](/foundation/f3-auxiliaries/).

**How to test:** can you change it for time? *play → played → will play*. If yes, it is a verb.

### Rule 4: An adjective describes a noun or pronoun.

An adjective answers questions like *what kind?*, *which?*, *how many?* about a noun.

> *a **tall** building* (what kind?)
> *a **red** saree* (what kind?)
> *The idli was **soft**.* (what kind? It comes after the verb *was*.)
> *She is **happy**.* (describes the pronoun *she*)

An adjective can come **before** a noun (*a **soft** idli*) or **after** a linking verb like *be, seem, look, become* (*The idli was **soft***).

### Rule 5: An adverb describes a verb, an adjective or another adverb.

An adverb answers *how?*, *when?*, *where?*, *how often?* or *how much?*

> *Ravi ran **fast**.* (how did he run? describes the verb *ran*)
> *We will leave **tomorrow**.* (when?)
> *Please sit **here**.* (where?)
> *She **always** wins.* (how often?)
> *The tea is **very** hot.* (how hot? describes the adjective *hot*)
> *He spoke **too** quickly.* (describes the adverb *quickly*)

Many adverbs end in *-ly* (*slowly, happily*), but not all. *Fast, hard, well, soon, here, never, very* are adverbs without *-ly*.

### Rule 6: A preposition shows how a noun or pronoun is linked to another word.

A preposition usually shows **place**, **time** or **direction**. It is always followed by a noun, a pronoun or an *-ing* word. This group is called a **prepositional phrase**.

> *The book is **on** the table.* (place)
> *The train leaves **at** six.* (time)
> *She walked **towards** the temple.* (direction)
> *This gift is **for** you.*
> *He is fond **of** reading.* (followed by an *-ing* word)

Common prepositions: *in, on, at, by, for, with, from, to, into, of, about, under, over, between, among, after, before, since, during*. Full lessons: [Prepositions (Class 11)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-11/).

### Rule 7: A conjunction joins words, phrases or clauses.

> *Arun **and** Kavya are cousins.* (joins two words)
> *I called him, **but** he did not answer.* (joins two clauses)
> *We stayed at home **because** it was raining.* (joins two clauses)

Two main kinds:

| Kind | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinating | joins two equal parts | and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet |
| Subordinating | joins a less important clause to the main clause | because, although, when, if, unless, since, that |

Full lesson: [Connectives and linkers](/topics/t06-connectives/class-12/).

### Rule 8: An interjection shows a sudden feeling.

An interjection is a word or short sound that shows surprise, joy, pain or sorrow. It is not grammatically joined to the rest of the sentence. It is usually followed by an exclamation mark (!).

> ***Wow!** India won the match.*
> ***Ouch!** That hurts.*
> ***Alas!** He is no more.*
> ***Hurray!** We have a holiday.*

### Rule 9: A determiner comes before a noun and tells us which one or how much.

A determiner comes at the start of a noun group. It does not describe the noun. Instead, it **points to** it or tells its **amount**.

> ***the** bus, **a** mango, **this** book, **my** bag, **some** water, **every** student, **two** pencils*

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Articles | a, an, the |
| Demonstratives | this, that, these, those |
| Possessives | my, your, his, her, its, our, their |
| Quantifiers | some, any, many, much, few, little, all, both, enough |
| Distributives | each, every, either, neither |
| Numbers | one, two, first, second |

**Note for teachers:** older school grammars (such as Wren & Martin) count **eight** parts of speech and treat articles and words like *this, my, some* as kinds of adjectives. Modern grammars, and the TN textbooks, treat **determiners** as a separate class. Both views are found in exams. This site follows the modern view. Full lessons: [Articles and determiners (Class 11)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-11/).

### Rule 10: Decide the part of speech by the word's job in this sentence.

A dictionary lists many words under more than one part of speech. You cannot know which one it is until you see the sentence. Always ask: **what is this word doing here?**

> *I want a **drink** of water.* (a thing: noun)
> *Please **drink** some water.* (an action: verb)

## Special cases

### 1. Words that change class: *round*

The word *round* can be five different parts of speech.

| Sentence | Job | Part of speech |
|---|---|---|
| *We sat at a **round** table.* | describes the noun *table* | adjective |
| *India won the first **round** of talks.* | names a thing (a stage) | noun |
| *The shop is **round** the corner.* | shows position, followed by a noun | preposition |
| *The children gathered **round**.* | tells where, with no noun after it | adverb |
| *Let us **round** off the number to 50.* | shows an action | verb |

### 2. *Fast*, *hard*, *late*, *early*: adjective or adverb

These words have the same form as adjective and adverb.

> *He is a **fast** runner.* (describes the noun *runner*: adjective)
> *He runs **fast**.* (tells how he runs: adverb)
> *It was a **hard** exam.* (adjective) / *She works **hard**.* (adverb)

Be careful: *hardly* is not the adverb of *hard*. *Hardly* means "almost not". *He hardly works* means he does almost no work.

### 3. Words ending in *-ly* that are adjectives

Some words end in *-ly* but describe nouns. They are adjectives: *friendly, lovely, lonely, likely, ugly, silly, costly, elderly*.

> *She is a **friendly** girl.* (adjective)

There is no adverb *friendlily*. We say *She spoke **in a friendly way***.

### 4. *Before*, *after*, *since*: preposition, conjunction or adverb

| Sentence | What follows | Part of speech |
|---|---|---|
| *Come **before** six.* | a noun (*six*) | preposition |
| *Come **before** the bell rings.* | a clause (*the bell rings*) | conjunction |
| *I have seen him **before**.* | nothing | adverb |

The same test works for *after*, *since*, *until*: noun after it → preposition; clause after it → conjunction; nothing after it → adverb.

### 5. *This, that, my, his*: determiner or pronoun

> ***This** book is mine.* (*this* comes before the noun *book*: determiner)
> ***This** is mine.* (*this* stands alone, in place of a noun: pronoun)

> *It is **his** bag.* (before a noun: determiner)
> *The bag is **his**.* (alone: pronoun)

### 6. *That*: four jobs

| Sentence | Part of speech |
|---|---|
| ***That** pen is broken.* | determiner (before a noun) |
| *I want **that**.* | pronoun (alone) |
| *I know **that** he is honest.* | conjunction (joins a clause) |
| *The pen **that** I bought is broken.* | relative pronoun (joins a clause and stands for *the pen*) |

### 7. *-ing* words

A word ending in *-ing* can be part of a verb, a noun or an adjective.

> *She **is singing**.* (part of the verb *is singing*)
> ***Singing** is her hobby.* (names an activity, used as subject: a noun, called a **gerund**)
> *I heard a **singing** bird.* (describes *bird*: an adjective, called a **participle**)

Full lesson: [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/).

### 8. Nouns used as adjectives

A noun can come before another noun to describe it.

> *a **school** bus, a **cricket** ball, a **Chennai** train*

Here *school* describes *bus*. Some grammars call it a noun used as an adjective (a noun modifier). In an exam, write "noun used as an adjective".

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *He runs **fast**.* → "fast is an adjective" | adverb | It tells **how** he runs, so it describes the verb. |
| *She is a **lovely** girl.* → "lovely is an adverb" | adjective | It describes the noun *girl*. An *-ly* ending does not always mean adverb. |
| *Come **after** lunch.* → "after is a conjunction" | preposition | It is followed by a noun (*lunch*), not a clause. |
| *I know **that** he is honest.* → "that is a pronoun" | conjunction | It joins the clause *he is honest* to *I know*. It does not stand for a noun. |
| *She speaks English **good**.* | *She speaks English **well**.* | *Good* is an adjective. To describe the verb *speaks*, use the adverb *well*. |
| *He works **hardly**.* (meaning "with effort") | *He works **hard**.* | *Hard* is the adverb. *Hardly* means "almost not". |
| *This is **my**.* | *This is **mine**.* | *My* must be followed by a noun. When there is no noun, use the pronoun *mine*. |
| *Walking* in *Walking is good exercise* → "verb" | gerund (a noun) | It is the subject of the sentence. It names an activity. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Identify the part of speech** of the underlined (or bold) word.
- **Use a word as two parts of speech** in two sentences of your own (for example, use *light* as a noun and as an adjective).
- **Choose the correct word** (*good / well*, *hard / hardly*) where knowing the part of speech helps.

Follow these steps for "Identify the part of speech":

1. **Read the whole sentence.** Never decide from the word alone.
2. **Ask what the word is doing.** Is it naming, replacing a noun, showing action, describing, linking or showing feeling?
3. **Look at the word next to it.**
   - Before a noun and describing it → adjective. Before a noun and pointing or counting → determiner.
   - Followed by a noun or pronoun, showing place or time → preposition.
   - Followed by a clause (subject + verb) → conjunction.
   - Describing a verb, adjective or adverb → adverb.
4. **Test it.** For a verb, change the time (*play → played*). For a noun, try *the* or a plural.
5. **Write the answer** with a short reason if the question asks for one.

### Worked example 1

> *The **light** in this room is poor.*

1. Read the sentence: it is about something in the room.
2. What is *light* doing? It names a thing. It is the subject of *is*.
3. It comes after *the*, and *the* points to a noun.
4. Test: *the lights* works.

**Answer:** *light* is a **noun**.

### Worked example 2

> *Wait here **until** I come back.*

1. Read the sentence: it gives a time limit.
2. What is *until* doing? It joins *Wait here* and *I come back*.
3. What follows it? *I come back*, which has a subject (*I*) and a verb (*come*). That is a clause.
4. A word that joins a clause is a conjunction.

**Answer:** *until* is a **conjunction**. (In *Wait **until** six*, it would be a preposition, because *six* is a noun.)

### Worked example 3

> *The **well** in our village has gone dry.*

1. Read the sentence: it is about something in the village that has gone dry.
2. What is *well* doing? It names a thing (a hole in the ground for water). It is the subject of *has gone*.
3. It comes after *the*.
4. Test: *the wells* works.

**Answer:** *well* is a **noun**. Compare: *She sings **well*** (adverb) and *I am **well** now* (adjective, meaning "healthy").

### Worked example 4

> Use *light* as a verb and as an adjective.

1. A verb shows an action: to *light* something is to set it burning.
2. An adjective describes a noun: *light* can mean "not heavy" or "pale".

**Answer:** *Please **light** the lamp.* (verb) / *She wore a **light** blue saree.* (adjective)

## Quick check

Name the part of speech of the word in bold.

1. *She came **late** to school.*
2. *We waited **for** the bus.*
3. ***Alas!** We lost the match.*
4. ***Each** student got a prize.*
5. *I have not seen him **since**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. **Adverb.** *Late* tells **when** she came, so it describes the verb *came*.
2. **Preposition.** *For* is followed by the noun group *the bus* and links it to *waited*.
3. **Interjection.** *Alas* shows a sudden feeling of sorrow and stands apart from the sentence.
4. **Determiner** (a distributive). *Each* comes before the noun *student* and tells us "one by one, all of them". Older grammars call it an adjective.
5. **Adverb.** Nothing follows *since*, so it is not a preposition or a conjunction. It tells **when**.

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- The part of speech depends on the **job** of the word in **this** sentence.
- Noun names · pronoun replaces a noun · verb shows action or state · adjective describes a noun · adverb describes a verb, adjective or adverb.
- Preposition + noun · conjunction + clause · interjection shows feeling · determiner points to or counts a noun.
- Same word, different jobs: *a **round** table* (adj) / *a **round** of talks* (noun) / ***round** the corner* (prep) / *gathered **round*** (adv) / *to **round** off* (verb).
:::
