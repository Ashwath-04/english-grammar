## What you will learn

- The five forms of a verb, V1 to V5, and the name of each one
- Which helping verb goes with which form
- The spelling rules for adding *-s*, *-ed* and *-ing*
- Irregular verbs in five easy groups (AAA, ABB, ABA, ABC, i–a–u)
- Verbs that are easy to confuse: *lie / lay*, *rise / raise*, *sit / set*, *fall / fell*, *find / found*

## Why this matters

Almost every grammar topic on this site uses V1 to V5. Tenses are made from them. The passive voice needs V3. Reported speech changes V1 to V2. Many exam errors are verb-form errors: *~~He has went~~*, *~~Did she wrote?~~*

If you know the five forms and which one goes after which helping verb, you can correct most of these errors at once.

## The idea in simple words

Look at the verb *write* in these sentences.

> *I **write** a letter every week.*
> *Yesterday I **wrote** a letter.*
> *I have **written** three letters today.*
> *I am **writing** a letter now.*
> *Priya **writes** a letter every week.*

The verb changes its shape depending on **time** and on the **helping verb** in front of it. English verbs have five shapes. We call them V1, V2, V3, V4 and V5.

## Grammar words you need

**Verb:** a word that shows an action (*run, eat*) or a state (*be, know*).

**Helping verb (auxiliary verb):** a verb that comes before the main verb and helps it: *am, is, are, was, were, have, has, had, do, does, did, will, can...* See [Auxiliary verbs](/foundation/f3-auxiliaries/).

**Main verb:** the verb that carries the meaning. In *She has **written***, the main verb is *written*.

**Regular verb:** a verb whose V2 and V3 end in *-ed*: *walk → walked → walked*.

**Irregular verb:** a verb whose V2 or V3 does not end in *-ed*: *write → wrote → written*.

**Participle:** a verb form that works with a helping verb, or as an adjective. V3 is the **past participle**. V4 is the **present participle**.

## The rules

### Rule 1: Every verb has five forms.

| Form | Name | Regular | Irregular |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Base form | walk | write |
| V2 | Past simple (past tense form) | walked | wrote |
| V3 | Past participle | walked | written |
| V4 | Present participle (*-ing* form) | walking | writing |
| V5 | *-s* form (present, with *he / she / it*) | walks | writes |

V4 and V5 are always made in a regular way, even for irregular verbs: add *-ing* and *-s*. Only V2 and V3 can be irregular.

### Rule 2: Use V1 after *to*, after modals, after *do / does / did*, and in the present with *I, you, we, they*.

> *I want **to go** home.* (after *to*)
> *She can **swim**.* (after a modal: *can, will, must, should...*)
> *Did he **come**?* (after *did*)
> *He does not **like** tea.* (after *does*)
> *We **play** cricket on Sundays.* (present, with *we*)

### Rule 3: Use V5 in the present simple with *he, she, it* or one person or thing.

> *Ravi **plays** the veena.*
> *The shop **opens** at nine.*
> *She **goes** to Trichy every month.*

### Rule 4: Use V2 alone, with no helping verb, for the past simple.

> *Arun **went** to Coimbatore last week.*
> *We **watched** the match yesterday.*

V2 never comes after a helping verb. *~~He has went~~* and *~~Did she wrote?~~* are wrong.

### Rule 5: Use V3 after *have / has / had*, and after a form of *be* in the passive.

> *She **has finished** her homework.* (present perfect)
> *The train **had left** before we arrived.* (past perfect)
> *The letter **was written** by Kavya.* (passive)

V3 never stands alone as the main verb of a sentence. *~~I seen it~~* is wrong. Say *I **saw** it* or *I **have seen** it*.

### Rule 6: Use V4 after a form of *be* for the continuous tenses.

> *They **are playing** in the ground.*
> *I **was reading** when you called.*
> *We **will be travelling** to Madurai tomorrow.*

V4 can also be used as a noun (a **gerund**): ***Swimming** is good exercise.* See [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/).

### Rule 7: Regular verbs add *-ed* for both V2 and V3.

> *walk → walked → walked*
> *open → opened → opened*
> *play → played → played*

For regular verbs, V2 and V3 always look the same.

### Rule 8: Learn irregular verbs in groups by pattern.

There are about 200 irregular verbs in English. Learning them in groups is much easier than learning one long list.

| Group | Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| AAA | all three forms the same | cut – cut – cut; put – put – put; let – let – let; hit – hit – hit |
| ABB | V2 and V3 the same | bring – brought – brought; teach – taught – taught; keep – kept – kept; buy – bought – bought |
| ABA | V1 and V3 the same | come – came – come; run – ran – run; become – became – become |
| ABC | all three different | go – went – gone; write – wrote – written; take – took – taken; see – saw – seen |
| i–a–u | the vowel changes i → a → u | sing – sang – sung; drink – drank – drunk; swim – swam – swum; begin – began – begun |

For the full list, see [Irregular verbs](/reference/irregular-verbs/).

### Rule 9: Add *-s* or *-es* to make V5.

| Verb ends in | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| most letters | add *-s* | play → plays, eat → eats, write → writes |
| *-s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -z* | add *-es* | pass → passes, wash → washes, catch → catches, fix → fixes |
| *-o* after a consonant | add *-es* | go → goes, do → does |
| consonant + *-y* | change *y* to *i*, add *-es* | study → studies, cry → cries, carry → carries |
| vowel + *-y* | add *-s* | play → plays, enjoy → enjoys, pay → pays |

Two verbs are special: *be → is* and *have → has*.

### Rule 10: Follow the spelling rules for *-ed*.

| Verb ends in | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| most letters | add *-ed* | walk → walked, help → helped |
| *-e* | add *-d* only | like → liked, dance → danced, agree → agreed |
| consonant + *-y* | change *y* to *i*, add *-ed* | study → studied, cry → cried, marry → married |
| vowel + *-y* | add *-ed* | play → played, enjoy → enjoyed |
| one syllable, one vowel + one consonant | double the last consonant | stop → stopped, plan → planned, rob → robbed |
| two syllables, stress on the **last** syllable, ending in one vowel + one consonant | double the last consonant | prefer → preferred, admit → admitted, occur → occurred |
| two syllables, stress on the **first** syllable | do not double | visit → visited, open → opened, offer → offered |

Never double *w*, *x* or *y*: *snow → snowed*, *fix → fixed*, *play → played*.

**British and American spelling:** British English doubles a final *-l* after one vowel, even when the stress is on the first syllable: *travel → travelled*, *cancel → cancelled*. American English does not: *traveled, canceled*. Use the British form in exams.

### Rule 11: Follow the spelling rules for *-ing*.

| Verb ends in | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| most letters | add *-ing* | play → playing, eat → eating |
| silent *-e* | drop the *e*, add *-ing* | write → writing, come → coming, make → making |
| *-ee*, *-ye*, *-oe* | keep the *e* | see → seeing, agree → agreeing, dye → dyeing |
| *-ie* | change *ie* to *y*, add *-ing* | lie → lying, die → dying, tie → tying |
| *-y* | keep the *y* | study → studying, cry → crying |
| one vowel + one consonant (same doubling rule as *-ed*) | double the last consonant | run → running, sit → sitting, begin → beginning, travel → travelling (British) |

*Be* keeps its *e*: *be → being*.

## Special cases

### 1. *Be* has eight forms

*Be* is the most irregular verb in English.

| V1 | Present | V2 | V3 | V4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| be | am (I), is (he / she / it), are (we / you / they) | was (I / he / she / it), were (we / you / they) | been | being |

> *I **am** ready. She **is** ready. They **are** ready.*
> *I **was** tired. We **were** tired.*
> *He has **been** ill.*

### 2. V2 and V3 that students mix up

These irregular verbs have different V2 and V3 forms. They cause the most errors.

| V1 | V2 (alone) | V3 (after *have / has / had / be*) |
|---|---|---|
| go | went | gone |
| do | did | done |
| see | saw | seen |
| come | came | come |
| begin | began | begun |
| drink | drank | drunk |
| write | wrote | written |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| take | took | taken |
| give | gave | given |

> *She **went** home.* / *She **has gone** home.*
> *I **did** it.* / *I **have done** it.*

### 3. Two correct forms

Some verbs have two correct V2 / V3 forms. The *-t* form is more common in British English. The *-ed* form is more common in American English. Both are correct.

> *learnt / learned, burnt / burned, dreamt / dreamed, spelt / spelled, smelt / smelled*

*Get → got → got* in British English. American English also uses *gotten* as V3.

### 4. *Read*: same spelling, different sound

*read – read – read*. V1 sounds like "reed". V2 and V3 sound like "red".

### 5. Verbs that are easy to confuse

| V1 | Meaning | V2 | V3 | Takes an object? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lie | to rest flat | lay | lain | no: *He **lay** on the bed.* |
| lie | to say something untrue | lied | lied | no: *She **lied** to me.* |
| lay | to put something down | laid | laid | yes: *She **laid** the plates on the table.* |
| rise | to go up (by itself) | rose | risen | no: *The sun **rose** at six.* |
| raise | to lift something | raised | raised | yes: *Ravi **raised** his hand.* |
| sit | to be seated | sat | sat | no: *We **sat** on the floor.* |
| set | to put something in place | set | set | yes: *She **set** the table.* |
| fall | to drop down | fell | fallen | no: *The mango **fell** from the tree.* |
| fell | to cut down a tree | felled | felled | yes: *They **felled** the old tree.* |
| find | to discover | found | found | yes: *I **found** my keys.* |
| found | to start (a school, a company) | founded | founded | yes: *He **founded** a school in 1950.* |

The trap: *lay* is the V2 of *lie* (to rest), and also the V1 of *lay* (to put). *Yesterday I **lay** down* is correct. *~~Yesterday I laid down~~* is wrong, because *laid* needs an object.

The quick test: *rise, sit, lie* (to rest) and *fall* **never** take an object. *Raise, set, lay* and *fell* (to cut) **always** do.

### 6. *Hang*: hung or hanged

*Hang* (an object) → *hung*: *She **hung** the clothes out to dry.*
*Hang* (a person, as punishment) → *hanged*: *The criminal was **hanged**.*

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *He has ~~went~~ home.* | *He has **gone** home.* | After *has*, use V3. *Went* is V2. |
| *Did she ~~wrote~~ the letter?* | *Did she **write** the letter?* | After *did*, use V1. *Did* already shows the past. |
| *I ~~seen~~ that film.* | *I **saw** that film.* / *I **have seen** that film.* | V3 cannot stand alone. Use V2, or *have* + V3. |
| *She ~~go~~ to school by bus.* | *She **goes** to school by bus.* | Present simple with *she* needs V5. |
| *He ~~studys~~ hard.* | *He **studies** hard.* | Consonant + *y*: change *y* to *i* and add *-es*. |
| *We are ~~writeing~~.* | *We are **writing**.* | Drop the silent *e* before *-ing*. |
| *The bus ~~stoped~~.* | *The bus **stopped**.* | One syllable, one vowel + one consonant: double the consonant. |
| *Prices have ~~raised~~.* | *Prices have **risen**.* | Prices go up by themselves (no object), so use *rise – rose – risen*. |
| *I ~~laid~~ down for a rest.* | *I **lay** down for a rest.* | *Lie* (to rest) → *lay* → *lain*. *Laid* needs an object. |

## How it comes in the exam

Verb forms are not usually a separate exercise. They are tested inside other exercises:

- **Fill in the blank with the correct form of the verb in brackets.**
- **Spot and correct the error** in a sentence.
- **Change the tense or the voice**, which needs the right form.

Follow these steps for "Fill in with the correct form":

1. **Look at the word right before the blank.** Is it a helping verb, *to*, or a subject?
2. **Choose the form** from this table:

| Word before the verb | Form to use |
|---|---|
| *to*, a modal (*can, will, must...*), *do / does / did* | V1 |
| *have / has / had* | V3 |
| *am / is / are / was / were / be / been* (action going on) | V4 |
| *am / is / are / was / were / be / been* (action done to the subject, passive) | V3 |
| no helping verb, past time | V2 |
| no helping verb, present, *he / she / it* | V5 |
| no helping verb, present, *I / you / we / they* | V1 |

3. **Check the spelling** (*-ies, -ied*, doubled consonants, dropped *e*).
4. **Read the sentence again** with your answer in it.

### Worked example 1

> *Kavya has ______ (write) a poem for the school magazine.*

1. Word before the blank: *has*.
2. After *has*, use V3.
3. *Write* is irregular: *write – wrote – written*.

**Answer:** *Kavya has **written** a poem for the school magazine.*

### Worked example 2

> *Did your brother ______ (pass) the exam?*

1. Word before the blank: the subject *your brother*, but the helping verb is *did*.
2. After *did*, use V1.
3. V1 of *pass* is *pass*.

**Answer:** *Did your brother **pass** the exam?* (not *passed*: *did* already shows the past)

### Worked example 3

> *The sun ______ (rise) in the east every day.*

1. No helping verb. The time is present (*every day*). The subject is *the sun* (one thing).
2. Use V5.
3. *Rise* ends in *-e*: add *-s* → *rises*. Check: the sun goes up by itself, so *rise* (not *raise*) is right.

**Answer:** *The sun **rises** in the east every day.*

### Worked example 4

> *Correct the error: The children were swiming in the pond.*

1. Word before the verb: *were*, an action going on → V4.
2. *Swim* has one syllable, one vowel + one consonant → double the *m*.

**Answer:** *The children were **swimming** in the pond.*

## Quick check

1. *Meena ______ (go) to the temple yesterday.*
2. *They have ______ (begin) the work.*
3. *He ______ (study) for three hours every day.*
4. *The workers ______ (lay) the new road last month.*
5. *Will you ______ (help) me?*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***went.*** No helping verb and past time (*yesterday*), so use V2.
2. ***begun.*** After *have*, use V3. *Begin – began – begun* (the i–a–u group).
3. ***studies.*** Present with *he* → V5. Consonant + *y* → *-ies*.
4. ***laid.*** *Lay* (to put something down) takes an object (*the new road*). Past time → V2 *laid*.
5. ***help.*** After the modal *will*, use V1.

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- V1 base · V2 past · V3 past participle · V4 *-ing* · V5 *-s*.
- *to* / modal / *do, does, did* → V1. *have, has, had* → V3. *be* + action going on → V4. *be* + passive → V3.
- V2 stands alone. V3 never stands alone.
- Learn irregular verbs in groups: AAA, ABB, ABA, ABC, i–a–u.
- *rise, sit, lie* take no object. *raise, set, lay* always take one.
:::
