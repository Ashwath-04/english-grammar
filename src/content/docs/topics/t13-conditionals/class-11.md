## What you will learn

- What a **conditional sentence** is: an ***if* clause** (the condition) and a **main clause** (the result)
- The four types and their verb patterns:
  - **Type 0** (general truths): *If you heat ice, it **melts**.*
  - **Type 1** (possible, likely): *If it rains, we **will stay** at home.*
  - **Type 2** (unlikely, imaginary present): *If I were rich, I **would travel**.*
  - **Type 3** (impossible, the past): *If I had studied, I **would have passed**.*
- Why we say ***If I were*** for every person in Type 2
- How to choose the right type, and how to answer "Fill in the verbs", "Match the halves", "*unless* ↔ *if*" and "Rewrite with inversion"

## Why this matters

We use conditionals all the time: to give advice (*If I were you, I would rest*), to make plans (*If it rains, we'll stay in*), to state facts (*If you mix red and yellow, you get orange*), and to talk about regrets (*If I had left earlier, I wouldn't have missed the bus*).

In the exam, conditionals come as fill-in-the-verb questions, matching clause halves, and rewriting sentences. Each type has a fixed verb pattern. Learn the four patterns and most questions become easy. This lesson uses the tenses from [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/) and the modals *will, would, could* from [Modal auxiliaries](/topics/t02-modal-auxiliaries/class-11/).

## The idea in simple words

Look at these four sentences about Ravi and a cricket match.

> *If it **rains**, the match **is** cancelled.* (This always happens. It is a rule.)
> *If it **rains** tomorrow, the match **will be** cancelled.* (Rain is possible tomorrow.)
> *If it **rained** tomorrow, the match **would be** cancelled.* (I don't think it will rain. I am imagining.)
> *If it **had rained** yesterday, the match **would have been** cancelled.* (It didn't rain. The match was played. I am imagining a different past.)

Each sentence has two parts:
- the ***if* clause** gives the **condition**: *If it rains*
- the **main clause** gives the **result**: *the match will be cancelled*

The **type** of conditional depends on how real or possible the condition is. As the condition becomes **less real**, the verbs move **further back** into the past.

## Grammar words you need

**Conditional sentence:** a sentence that says one thing happens (or would happen) if another thing happens.

***If* clause (condition clause):** the part that gives the condition. It starts with *if* (or *unless, when*...). It is a subordinate clause. See [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/).

**Main clause (result clause):** the part that gives the result.

**Real condition:** a condition that is true or possible.

**Unreal (imaginary) condition:** a condition that is not true, unlikely, or impossible because it is about a past that cannot change.

**V1, V2, V3:** base form, past form, past participle (*go, went, gone*). See [Verb forms](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/).

## The rules

### Rule 1: A conditional has an *if* clause and a main clause. Either can come first.

> ***If you study hard**, you will pass.*
> *You will pass **if you study hard**.*

Both are correct and mean the same.

**Punctuation:** when the *if* clause comes **first**, put a **comma** after it. When the main clause comes first, no comma is needed.

### Rule 2: Type 0 (general truths): *if* + present simple, present simple.

| *If* clause | Main clause |
|---|---|
| *if* + **present simple** | **present simple** |

> *If you **heat** ice, it **melts**.*
> *If you **mix** blue and yellow, you **get** green.*
> *If plants **don't get** water, they **die**.*
> *If I **drink** coffee at night, I **can't** sleep.* (a personal habit that is always true)

Type 0 is for **facts, scientific truths and habits**: things that are **always** true when the condition happens. Here, *if* means the same as ***when*** or ***whenever***.

> *When you heat ice, it melts.* (same meaning)

### Rule 3: Type 1 (possible, likely): *if* + present simple, *will* + V1.

| *If* clause | Main clause |
|---|---|
| *if* + **present simple** | ***will* + V1** |

> *If it **rains**, we **will stay** at home.*
> *If you **hurry**, you **will catch** the bus.*
> *If Kavya **practises** every day, she **will win** the competition.*
> *If I **see** Arun, I **will give** him your message.*

Type 1 is for a **real, possible situation in the future**. The speaker thinks the condition may well happen.

The main clause can also use other modals or a command:

> *If you **finish** early, you **can go** home.*
> *If it **rains**, we **may cancel** the trip.*
> *If you **feel** ill, you **should see** a doctor.*
> *If you **see** Meena, **tell** her to call me.* (imperative)

### Rule 4: Never use *will* in the *if* clause.

Even though the *if* clause is about the future, it takes the **present simple**.

> *~~If it will rain, we will stay at home.~~* ✘
> *If it **rains**, we will stay at home.* ✔

This is the same rule as for time clauses (*when, as soon as, before*): see [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/).

(*If you **will** wait a moment, I'll call the manager* is correct but special: here *will* means "are willing to". It is a polite request. Do not use it in normal Type 1 answers.)

### Rule 5: Type 2 (unlikely or imaginary): *if* + past simple, *would* + V1.

| *If* clause | Main clause |
|---|---|
| *if* + **past simple** | ***would* + V1** |

> *If I **had** a car, I **would drive** to Ooty.* (I don't have a car.)
> *If Ravi **knew** the answer, he **would tell** us.* (He doesn't know it.)
> *If I **won** a lottery, I **would buy** a house for my parents.* (It is very unlikely.)
> *If we **lived** in Chennai, we **would go** to the beach every day.* (We don't live there.)

Type 2 is for something that is **not true now**, or **unlikely to happen** in the future. It is **imaginary**.

**Important:** the past simple here does **not** mean past time. It shows that the situation is **unreal**. *If I had a car* is about **now**.

*Could* and *might* can replace *would*:

> *If I **had** more time, I **could learn** the guitar.* (= I would be able to)
> *If you **asked** her, she **might agree**.* (= perhaps she would)

### Rule 6: In Type 2, use *were* for every person, especially in formal English.

> *If I **were** rich, I would help the poor.*
> *If she **were** here, she would know what to do.*
> *If he **were** taller, he would join the basketball team.*
> *If it **were** not raining, we would go out.*

In everyday speech, *was* is also used with *I, he, she, it*: *If I **was** rich...* But ***were*** is the correct form in formal writing and exams.

The fixed phrase ***If I were you*** is used to give advice:

> ***If I were you**, I **would apologise** to her.*
> ***If I were you**, I **wouldn't** buy that phone.*

### Rule 7: Type 3 (the past, impossible now): *if* + past perfect, *would have* + V3.

| *If* clause | Main clause |
|---|---|
| *if* + **past perfect** (*had* + V3) | ***would have* + V3** |

> *If I **had studied** harder, I **would have passed**.* (I didn't study hard. I failed.)
> *If you **had left** earlier, you **would have caught** the train.* (You didn't leave early. You missed it.)
> *If Meena **had known** about the party, she **would have come**.* (She didn't know. She didn't come.)
> *If it **had not rained**, we **would have won** the match.* (It rained. We lost.)

Type 3 is about the **past**. The condition did **not** happen, so the result did not happen either. It is **impossible** to change now. We use it for **regrets**, **criticism** and imagining a different past.

*Could have* and *might have* can replace *would have*:

> *If he **had asked** me, I **could have helped** him.*
> *If they **had played** better, they **might have won**.*

### Rule 8: Choose the type by asking how real the condition is.

| Question | Answer | Type | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is it always true (a fact, a rule, a habit)? | Yes | **0** | present, present |
| Is it possible or likely in the future? | Yes | **1** | present, *will* + V1 |
| Is it untrue now, or unlikely in the future? | Yes | **2** | past, *would* + V1 |
| Is it about the past, and did the opposite happen? | Yes | **3** | past perfect, *would have* + V3 |

**Notice the step-back pattern:**

| Type | *If* clause | Main clause |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | present | present |
| 1 | present | *will* + V1 |
| 2 | past | *would* + V1 |
| 3 | past perfect | *would have* + V3 |

Each step to the "less real" side moves the verbs **one step back** into the past.

### Rule 9: Compare Type 1 and Type 2 to see the difference in meaning.

The same idea can be Type 1 or Type 2. The speaker's view decides.

> *If I **pass** the exam, I **will join** an engineering college.* (Type 1: I think I may pass. It is a real plan.)
> *If I **passed** the exam, I **would join** an engineering college.* (Type 2: I don't think I will pass. I am dreaming.)

> *If India **wins** the final, there **will be** a holiday.* (Type 1: India has a good chance.)
> *If India **won** the final, there **would be** a holiday.* (Type 2: it seems unlikely.)

## Special cases

### 1. *Unless* means *if ... not*

> ***Unless** you study, you will fail.* = ***If** you do **not** study, you will fail.*

*Unless* is already negative. Do not add *not*: *~~Unless you do not study~~* ✘. This is covered fully in [Conditional clauses (Class 12)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-12/).

### 2. *If* or *when*

> ***When** I see Ravi, I will give him the book.* (I am sure I will see him.)
> ***If** I see Ravi, I will give him the book.* (I may or may not see him.)

In Type 0, *if* and *when* mean the same: *If / When you heat water, it boils.*

### 3. Other tenses in the *if* clause of Type 1

The *if* clause can use the present continuous or present perfect when the meaning needs it:

> *If you **are going** to the market, please buy some bananas.*
> *If you **have finished** your work, you can go home.*

### 4. *Would* never goes in the *if* clause

> *~~If I would have money, I would buy it.~~* ✘ → *If I **had** money, I would buy it.* ✔
> *~~If I would have known, I would have come.~~* ✘ → *If I **had known**, I would have come.* ✔

### 5. Short forms

In speech, *would* becomes *'d*, and *had* also becomes *'d*. Do not mix them up:

> *If **I'd** known, **I'd** have come.* (= *If I **had** known, I **would** have come.*)

The first *'d* (in the *if* clause) is *had*. The second *'d* (before *have*) is *would*.

### 6. Inversion without *if* (preview)

In formal English, Type 2 and Type 3 can drop *if* and put the verb first:

> ***Were I** rich, I would travel.* (= If I were rich)
> ***Had I known**, I would have come.* (= If I had known)

This is taught in [Conditional clauses (Class 12)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-12/).

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *If it ~~will rain~~, we will stay home.* | *If it **rains**, we will stay home.* | Type 1: present simple in the *if* clause, not *will*. |
| *If I ~~would have~~ money, I would buy a car.* | *If I **had** money, I would buy a car.* | Type 2: past simple in the *if* clause, not *would*. |
| *If I ~~would have known~~, I would have helped.* | *If I **had known**, I would have helped.* | Type 3: past perfect in the *if* clause. |
| *If I ~~was~~ you, I would go.* | *If I **were** you, I would go.* | *If I were you* is the fixed form for advice. |
| *If he had come, he ~~would help~~ us.* | *If he had come, he **would have helped** us.* | Type 3: main clause is *would have* + V3. |
| *If you heat ice, it ~~will melt~~.* | *If you heat ice, it **melts**.* | A scientific fact → Type 0 (present, present). (*will melt* is not wrong, but Type 0 is the expected answer.) |
| *If I had a car, I ~~will~~ drive to Ooty.* | *If I had a car, I **would** drive to Ooty.* | Type 2: past + *would*, not *will*. |
| *If she studies, she ~~would~~ pass.* | *If she studies, she **will** pass.* | Type 1: present + *will*. Don't mix the types. |
| *If I had studied, I would have ~~pass~~.* | *If I had studied, I would have **passed**.* | After *would have*, use V3. |
| *Unless you do ~~not~~ hurry, you'll be late.* | ***Unless** you hurry, you'll be late.* | *Unless* already means *if not*. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Fill in** the verbs in brackets in the correct form.
- **Match** the *if* clauses with the right main clauses.
- **Rewrite** with *unless* instead of *if* (or the reverse).
- **Rewrite** with **inversion** (no *if*): see the Class 12 lesson.
- **Complete** the sentence with your own words.

### Steps for "Fill in the verbs"

1. **Look at the verb that is already given** (in the *if* clause or the main clause).
2. **Identify the type** from it:
   - present + present → Type 0
   - present in the *if* clause, or *will* in the main clause → Type 1
   - past in the *if* clause, or *would* + V1 in the main clause → Type 2
   - past perfect in the *if* clause, or *would have* in the main clause → Type 3
3. **If both verbs are blank**, decide the type from the **meaning**: fact? likely future? imaginary? past?
4. **Write the missing verb** using the pattern for that type.
5. **Check:** no *will* or *would* in the *if* clause; *were* for *I / he / she* in Type 2.

### Steps for "Match the halves"

1. Look at the verb in each *if* clause and find its type.
2. Find the main clause with the matching pattern (*will* → Type 1; *would* + V1 → Type 2; *would have* → Type 3).
3. Check that the **meaning** fits.

### Worked example 1

> *If you ______ (water) the plants every day, they will grow well.*

1. Given verb: *will grow* (main clause).
2. *Will* + V1 → Type 1.
3. Type 1 *if* clause → present simple. Subject *you* → *water*.

**Answer:** *If you **water** the plants every day, they will grow well.*

### Worked example 2

> *If I ______ (be) the Prime Minister, I ______ (give) free books to all students.*

1. Both verbs are blank.
2. Meaning: I am not the Prime Minister. This is imaginary → Type 2.
3. *If* clause: past simple, *were* for *I* → *were*.
4. Main clause: *would* + V1 → *would give*.

**Answer:** *If I **were** the Prime Minister, I **would give** free books to all students.*

### Worked example 3

> *If Arun ______ (not miss) the bus, he ______ (reach) school on time.* (He missed the bus this morning.)

1. Meaning: the past (*this morning*), and the opposite happened → Type 3.
2. *If* clause: past perfect, negative → *had not missed*.
3. Main clause: *would have* + V3 → *would have reached*.

**Answer:** *If Arun **had not missed** the bus, he **would have reached** school on time.*

### Worked example 4

> *If you ______ (press) this button, the machine ______ (start).*

1. Meaning: a fixed rule of how the machine works → Type 0.
2. Both present simple.

**Answer:** *If you **press** this button, the machine **starts**.* (Type 1, *will start*, is also acceptable if the speaker means one particular time.)

### Worked example 5 (matching)

> *(a) If I had wings, ... (b) If you mix red and white, ... (c) If you had told me, ...*
> *(i) ... I would have helped you. (ii) ... I would fly to the moon. (iii) ... you get pink.*

1. (a) *had* (past) → Type 2 → *would* + V1 → (ii).
2. (b) *mix* (present), a fact → Type 0 → present → (iii).
3. (c) *had told* (past perfect) → Type 3 → *would have* + V3 → (i).

**Answer:** (a)–(ii), (b)–(iii), (c)–(i).

## Quick check

1. *If you ______ (boil) water, it turns into steam.*
2. *If it ______ (rain) tomorrow, we will postpone the picnic.*
3. *If I ______ (be) you, I would accept the offer.*
4. *If she had asked me, I ______ (help) her.*
5. Correct the error: *If he will come, I will tell him.*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***boil.*** A scientific fact → Type 0: present, present.
2. ***rains.*** Type 1: the *if* clause takes the present simple, even for the future.
3. ***were.*** Type 2, advice → *If I were you*.
4. ***would have helped.*** *Had asked* (past perfect) → Type 3 → *would have* + V3.
5. ***If he comes, I will tell him.*** No *will* in the *if* clause of Type 1.

</details>

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- **Type 0** (always true): *if* + present → present. *If you heat ice, it melts.*
- **Type 1** (possible): *if* + present → *will* + V1. *If it rains, we will stay.*
- **Type 2** (imaginary now): *if* + past → *would* + V1. *If I were rich, I would travel.*
- **Type 3** (imaginary past): *if* + *had* + V3 → *would have* + V3. *If I had studied, I would have passed.*
- **Never** *will* or *would* in the *if* clause.
- ***If I were*** for all persons in Type 2. ***If I were you*** for advice.
- Comma after the *if* clause when it comes first.
:::
