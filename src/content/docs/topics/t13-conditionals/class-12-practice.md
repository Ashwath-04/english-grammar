## Part A: *Unless* and *if*

**1.** *If you don't wear a helmet, the police will fine you.* (use *unless*)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Unless you wear a helmet, the police will fine you.*** Replace *if ... don't* with *unless*; the verb becomes positive.

</details>

**2.** *If she doesn't practise, she won't win.* (use *unless*)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Unless she practises, she won't win.*** *Doesn't practise* → *practises* (V5 comes back). The main clause stays the same.

</details>

**3.** *Unless it rains soon, the crops will dry up.* (use *if*)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***If it doesn't rain soon, the crops will dry up.*** *Unless* → *if ... not*. *Rains* → *doesn't rain*.

</details>

**4.** *Unless you hurry, you will be late for the exam.* (use *if*)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***If you don't hurry, you will be late for the exam.***

</details>

**5.** *You can't enter the hall if you don't have a hall ticket.* (use *unless*)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***You can't enter the hall unless you have a hall ticket.*** The *if* clause can come second; *unless* takes its place in the same position.

</details>

**6.** *I will be surprised if Ravi doesn't pass.* (Can this be rewritten with *unless*?)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**No.** **Trap:** here *if he doesn't pass* is the **cause** of the surprise, not a condition to avoid. *~~I will be surprised unless Ravi passes~~* does not mean the same thing. *Unless* means "except if".

</details>

## Part B: Choose the correct condition word

**7.** *Take a torch ______ the power goes off.* (if / in case)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***in case.*** Take it now, as a precaution, because the power **may** go off.

</details>

**8.** *You can watch TV ______ you finish your homework first.* (provided / in case)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***provided*** (or *provided that / as long as*). It is a condition: only if you finish first.

</details>

**9.** *______ you won a trip abroad, where would you go?* (Supposing / Unless)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Supposing*** (or *Suppose / If*). An imagined situation, Type 2 pattern (*won ... would go*).

</details>

**10.** *Leave now, ______ you will miss the last bus.* (otherwise / even if)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***otherwise*** (or *or*). It means "if you don't leave now".

</details>

**11.** *______ you offer me a lot of money, I won't sell my grandfather's watch.* (Even if / Provided)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Even if.*** The result (*I won't sell*) is the same whether or not you offer the money.

</details>

**12.** *______ fire, break the glass and press the alarm.* (In case / In case of)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***In case of.*** It is followed by a noun (*fire*). *In case* is followed by a clause.

</details>

## Part C: Rewrite without *if* (inversion)

**13.** *If I had known you were in Madurai, I would have visited you.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Had I known you were in Madurai, I would have visited you.*** Type 3 → *Had* + subject + V3.

</details>

**14.** *If they had left earlier, they would have avoided the traffic.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Had they left earlier, they would have avoided the traffic.***

</details>

**15.** *If I were you, I would talk to the teacher.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Were I you, I would talk to the teacher.*** Type 2 with *were* → *Were* + subject.

</details>

**16.** *If he were rich, he would help the village.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Were he rich, he would help the village.***

</details>

**17.** *If you need any information, please contact the office.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Should you need any information, please contact the office.*** Formal Type 1 → *Should* + subject + V1.

</details>

**18.** *If it had not been for your advice, I would have made a big mistake.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Had it not been for your advice, I would have made a big mistake.*** (or ***But for your advice, ...***) Negative inversion keeps the full form *Had it not*.

</details>

**19.** *If she studied regularly, she would get better marks.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Were she to study regularly, she would get better marks.*** **Trap:** the verb is *studied*, not *were*. Only *had, were* and *should* can be inverted, so use *Were* + subject + *to* + V1.

</details>

**20.** *If I hadn't seen the snake, I would have stepped on it.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Had I not seen the snake, I would have stepped on it.*** **Trap:** never *Hadn't I seen*. In inversion, *not* goes after the subject.

</details>

## Part D: Mixed conditionals

**21.** *If I ______ (bring) my umbrella this morning, I ______ (not be) wet now.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***had brought*** ... ***wouldn't be.*** Past condition (*this morning*) + present result (*now*) → *had* + V3, *would* + V1.

</details>

**22.** *If Kavya ______ (take) the medicine yesterday, she ______ (feel) better today.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***had taken*** ... ***would feel.*** Past condition (*yesterday*) + present result (*today*).

</details>

**23.** *If I ______ (be) good at maths, I ______ (choose) the science group last year.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***were*** ... ***would have chosen.*** Present condition (I am not good at maths, generally) + past result (*last year*) → past simple, *would have* + V3.

</details>

**24.** *If he hadn't lost his job, he ______ (live) in Chennai now.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***would be living*** (or ***would live***). Past condition + present result (*now*) → *would* + V1 (or *would be* + V4 for an action going on now).

</details>

## Part E: Fill in the correct form

**25.** *Unless you ______ (tell) me the truth, I can't help you.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***tell.*** Type 1 → present simple after *unless*, and no *not*.

</details>

**26.** *Should you ______ (see) Meena, give her this letter.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***see.*** *Should* + subject + V1.

</details>

**27.** *Were I ______ (win) the lottery, I would buy a farm.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to win.*** *Were* + subject + *to* + V1.

</details>

**28.** *But for the lifeguard, the boy ______ (drown).*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***would have drowned.*** *But for* (= without) about a past event → *would have* + V3.

</details>

## Part F: Find and correct the mistake

**29.** *If I will see him, I will tell him.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*If I **see** him, I will tell him.* No *will* in the *if* clause.

</details>

**30.** *If I would have known, I would have come.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*If I **had known**, I would have come.* Type 3 → past perfect in the *if* clause, never *would have*.

</details>

**31.** *Unless you don't apologise, she won't forgive you.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Unless you apologise**, she won't forgive you.* *Unless* already means *if not*.

</details>

**32.** *Hadn't I helped him, he would have failed.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Had I not helped** him, he would have failed.* In inversion, use the full form *Had I not*, not the short form *Hadn't I*.

</details>
