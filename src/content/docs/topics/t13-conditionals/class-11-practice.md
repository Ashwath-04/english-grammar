## Part A: Fill in the verbs in the correct form

**1.** *If you ______ (heat) butter, it melts.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***heat.*** A general truth → Type 0: present, present.

</details>

**2.** *If plants ______ (not get) sunlight, they die.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***don't get*** (*do not get*). Type 0: a fact of nature. Present simple negative.

</details>

**3.** *If you hurry, you ______ (catch) the 8 o'clock bus.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***will catch.*** *Hurry* (present) in the *if* clause → Type 1 → *will* + V1.

</details>

**4.** *If it ______ (rain) this evening, we will play indoors.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***rains.*** Type 1. **Trap:** *this evening* is the future, but the *if* clause takes the present simple, not *will rain*.

</details>

**5.** *If Kavya ______ (practise) regularly, she will become a good singer.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***practises.*** Type 1 → present simple. *Kavya* → V5 (*practises*, British spelling of the verb).

</details>

**6.** *If you see Arun, ______ (tell) him to call me.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***tell.*** Type 1 with a command (imperative) in the main clause. The imperative uses V1.

</details>

**7.** *If I ______ (have) a lot of money, I would build a school in my village.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***had.*** *Would build* → Type 2 → past simple in the *if* clause. (I don't have a lot of money.)

</details>

**8.** *If Ravi ______ (be) here, he would help us.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***were.*** Type 2 → *were* for every person in formal English. (*was* is common in speech.)

</details>

**9.** *If I were you, I ______ (not lend) him money.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***wouldn't lend*** (*would not lend*). *If I were you* → Type 2 advice → *would* + V1.

</details>

**10.** *If we lived near the sea, we ______ (go) swimming every day.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***would go.*** *Lived* (past) → Type 2 → *would* + V1. (We don't live near the sea.)

</details>

**11.** *If you ______ (ask) me earlier, I would have helped you.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***had asked.*** *Would have helped* → Type 3 → past perfect in the *if* clause.

</details>

**12.** *If Meena had left home on time, she ______ (not miss) the train.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***wouldn't have missed*** (*would not have missed*). *Had left* → Type 3 → *would have* + V3.

</details>

**13.** *If the team ______ (play) better, they ______ (win) yesterday's match.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***had played*** ... ***would have won.*** *Yesterday* → the past, and the team lost → Type 3. *Win – won – won*.

</details>

**14.** *If I ______ (know) the answer, I would tell you.* (I don't know it.)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***knew.*** **Trap:** the past form *knew* is about **now**. Type 2 uses the past to show an unreal present.

</details>

**15.** *If I ______ (know) the answer, I would have told you.* (I didn't know it yesterday.)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***had known.*** Now the main clause has *would have told* → Type 3 → past perfect. Compare with question 14.

</details>

**16.** *If you finish your homework, you ______ (can) watch TV.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***can.*** Type 1 → the main clause can use *can* in place of *will*. (*will be able to* is also correct.)

</details>

## Part B: Name the type of conditional

**17.** *If you press this switch, the light comes on.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Type 0.** Present + present: a fixed rule of how something works.

</details>

**18.** *If the bus is late, I will take an auto.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Type 1.** Present + *will*: a real, possible future situation.

</details>

**19.** *If I were a bird, I would fly over the mountains.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Type 2.** Past (*were*) + *would* + V1: imaginary (I am not a bird).

</details>

**20.** *If they had invited us, we would have gone.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Type 3.** Past perfect + *would have* + V3: an imaginary past (they did not invite us).

</details>

**21.** *If India won the World Cup again, there would be huge celebrations.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Type 2.** Past + *would*: the speaker sees it as unlikely or imaginary. (*If India wins ..., there will be ...* would be Type 1, a real possibility.)

</details>

> *(b) If I were taller, ...*
> *(c) If you mix yellow and blue, ...*
> *(d) If he had saved money, ...*
>
> *(i) ... you get green. (ii) ... he could have bought a bike. (iii) ... you will pass. (iv) ... I would join the basketball team.*

**22.** *(a) If you study hard, ...*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**(iii) ... you will pass.** *Study* (present) → Type 1 → *will* + V1.

</details>

**23.** *(b) If I were taller, ...*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**(iv) ... I would join the basketball team.** *Were* → Type 2 → *would* + V1.

</details>

**24.** *(c) If you mix yellow and blue, ...*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**(i) ... you get green.** A fact → Type 0 → present.

</details>

**25.** *(d) If he had saved money, ...*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**(ii) ... he could have bought a bike.** *Had saved* → Type 3 → *could have* + V3.

</details>

## Part D: Rewrite using *unless* or *if*

**26.** *If you don't water the plants, they will die.* (use *unless*)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Unless you water the plants, they will die.*** *If ... not* = *unless*. Remove *don't*.

</details>

**27.** *Unless you leave now, you will be late.* (use *if*)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***If you don't leave now, you will be late.*** *Unless* = *if ... not*, so add *don't*.

</details>

**28.** *If he doesn't apologise, I won't talk to him.* (use *unless*)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Unless he apologises, I won't talk to him.*** **Trap:** *doesn't apologise* becomes *apologises* (with *-s*) once *not* is removed.

</details>

## Part E: Find and correct the mistake

**29.** *If it will be sunny tomorrow, we will go to the beach.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*If it **is** sunny tomorrow, we will go to the beach.* No *will* in the *if* clause of Type 1.

</details>

**30.** *If I would have a bike, I would ride to school.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*If I **had** a bike, I would ride to school.* Type 2 → past simple in the *if* clause, not *would*.

</details>

**31.** *If she had studied, she would pass.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*If she had studied, she **would have passed**.* *Had studied* → Type 3 → *would have* + V3. (*She would pass* could only be correct as a mixed conditional about the present; see Class 12.)

</details>

**32.** *Unless you don't work hard, you will fail.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Unless you work hard**, you will fail.* *Unless* already means *if not*. A second *not* is wrong.

</details>
