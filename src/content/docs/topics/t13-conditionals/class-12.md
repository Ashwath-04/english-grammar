:::note[Revise first]
This lesson builds on [Conditional clauses (Class 11)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-11/). It assumes you already know:
- **Type 0:** *if* + present, present (*If you heat ice, it melts.*)
- **Type 1:** *if* + present, *will* + V1 (*If it rains, we will stay.*)
- **Type 2:** *if* + past, *would* + V1 (*If I were rich, I would travel.*)
- **Type 3:** *if* + past perfect, *would have* + V3 (*If I had studied, I would have passed.*)
- that *will* and *would* never go in the *if* clause
:::

## What you will learn

- ***unless*** = ***if ... not***, and how to change one into the other without a double negative
- Other condition words: ***provided (that), as long as, in case, suppose / supposing, otherwise, even if***
- **Inversion** without *if*: ***Had I known ...***, ***Were I you ...***, ***Should you need help ...***
- **Mixed conditionals**: *If I had taken the job, I would be rich now.*
- The common errors: *~~If I will go~~*, *~~If I would have known~~*, *~~Unless you don't~~*

## Why this matters

Class 12 questions go beyond the four basic types. You will be asked to rewrite sentences with *unless* and with inversion, and to choose between *if*, *unless*, *in case* and *provided that*. These are also the forms used in formal letters and essays: ***Should** you have any questions, please contact me.*

## The idea in simple words

All the sentences below are about one condition: doing (or not doing) homework.

> ***If** you do **not** finish your homework, you can't play.*
> ***Unless** you finish your homework, you can't play.* (same meaning)
> *You can play **provided** you finish your homework.* (only on this condition)
> *Finish your homework, **otherwise** you can't play.* (if you don't)
> ***Had** you finished your homework, you could have played.* (= If you had finished...)

The condition is the same. The **condition word** and the **sentence pattern** change. Each one has its own shade of meaning, and its own grammar.

## Grammar words you need

**Condition word (conditional conjunction):** a word that introduces a condition: *if, unless, provided, as long as, in case, suppose*.

**Inversion:** putting the helping verb **before** the subject, as in a question, but in a statement: ***Had I** known* (= *If I had known*).

**Mixed conditional:** a sentence where the *if* clause and the main clause refer to **different times** (for example, past condition, present result).

**Double negative:** two negative words for one negative idea: *~~Unless you do not~~*. It is wrong in standard English.

## The rules

### Rule 1: *Unless* means *if ... not*. Use a positive verb after it.

> ***If** you do **not** work hard, you will fail.*
> = ***Unless** you work hard, you will fail.*

> ***If** it does **not** rain, the crops will die.*
> = ***Unless** it rains, the crops will die.*

**Changing *if ... not* into *unless*:**
1. Replace *if* with *unless*.
2. Remove *not* (and *do / does / did* if they were only there for *not*).
3. Put the verb back in its normal form (*does not rain → rains*).

**Changing *unless* into *if ... not*:**
1. Replace *unless* with *if*.
2. Add *not* (with *do / does / did* if needed).

> ***Unless** he apologises, I won't forgive him.* → ***If** he does**n't** apologise, I won't forgive him.*

**No double negative:**

> *~~Unless you do not hurry, you will miss the bus.~~* ✘
> ***Unless** you hurry, you will miss the bus.* ✔

### Rule 2: *Unless* is not used in every *if ... not* sentence.

*Unless* means "except if". It is used when the *if* clause gives the **only way to avoid** the result. It is **not** used when the *if not* clause gives a **reason for a feeling**, or in most Type 3 regrets.

> *I'll be surprised **if** he does**n't** come.* ✔ (not *~~unless he comes~~*: his not coming would be the cause of my surprise)
> *I'd be sad **if** you did**n't** call.* ✔
> ***If** I had**n't** missed the bus, I would have been on time.* ✔ (Type 3 regret: *unless* sounds unnatural here)

In exams, *unless* is almost always used with **Type 1** sentences. Use it there with confidence.

### Rule 3: *Provided (that)*, *providing* and *as long as* mean "only if".

These show a **strong condition**: the result will happen only if the condition is met.

> *You can borrow my bike **provided (that)** you return it by six.*
> *I will lend you the book **as long as** you take care of it.*
> *You can go to the party **on condition that** you are home by ten.*
> ***Providing** the weather is good, we will go to Kodaikanal.*

Like *if*, they take the **present simple** for the future (not *will*).

### Rule 4: *In case* means "as a precaution, because something may happen". It does not mean *if*.

> *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* (Take it now, because it might rain.)
> *Take an umbrella **if** it rains.* (Take it only when it is raining.)

> *I'll write down your number **in case** I forget it.*
> *Keep some money with you **in case** you need it.*

***In case of*** + noun = "if there is" (in notices): ***In case of** fire, use the stairs.* See [Prepositions (Class 12)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-12/).

### Rule 5: *Suppose / supposing* introduces an imagined situation, often in a question.

> ***Suppose** it rains tomorrow, what will we do?* (a real possibility: Type 1 pattern)
> ***Supposing** you won a lottery, what would you buy?* (imaginary: Type 2 pattern)
> ***Suppose** you had missed the train, what would you have done?* (past: Type 3 pattern)

*Suppose* and *supposing* can be used with any type. The verbs follow the normal pattern for that type.

### Rule 6: *Otherwise* and *or (else)* mean "if not". They come after the condition.

> *Hurry up, **otherwise** you will miss the bus.* = *If you don't hurry up, you will miss the bus.*
> *Wear a helmet, **or** you may get hurt.*
> *I didn't have my phone. **Otherwise**, I would have called you.* (Type 3 meaning: if I had had my phone...)

*Otherwise* usually comes after a comma, a semicolon or a full stop. See [Connectives and linkers](/topics/t06-connectives/class-12/).

### Rule 7: *Even if* means that the result happens whether the condition is true or not.

> ***Even if** it rains, the match will go on.* (Rain or no rain, the match will go on.)
> ***Even if** I had money, I wouldn't buy that phone.*

Compare with *even though*, which is about a fact that **is** true: ***Even though** it is raining, the match is going on.*

### Rule 8: In formal English, drop *if* and invert *had*, *were* or *should*.

This is called **inversion**. The helping verb moves to the front, and *if* disappears. The meaning does not change.

| Type | With *if* | With inversion |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | ***If I had** known, I would have come.* | ***Had I** known, I would have come.* |
| 3 | ***If she had** left earlier, she would have caught the bus.* | ***Had she** left earlier, she would have caught the bus.* |
| 2 | ***If I were** you, I would accept.* | ***Were I** you, I would accept.* |
| 2 | ***If he were** here, he would help.* | ***Were he** here, he would help.* |
| 2 (unlikely future) | ***If I were to** win, I would donate the money.* | ***Were I to** win, I would donate the money.* |
| 1 (formal, unlikely) | ***If you should** need help, call me.* | ***Should you** need help, call me.* |
| 1 (formal) | ***If** you **have** any questions, please ask.* | ***Should you have** any questions, please ask.* |

**Only *had*, *were* and *should*** can be inverted like this. Other verbs cannot:

> *~~Did I know, I would tell you.~~* ✘ → ***If I knew**, I would tell you.* / ***Were I to know**, ...* ✔

**Negative inversion:** put *not* after the subject. Never use a short form.

> ***Had I not** seen the car, I would have been hit.* ✔
> *~~Hadn't I seen the car~~* ✘
> ***Were it not** for your help, I would fail.* ✔
> ***Should it not** rain, we will play.* ✔

**Changing a Type 2 sentence with a main verb other than *be***: use *were* + *to*-infinitive.

> *If I **won** the prize, I would buy a house.* → ***Were I to win** the prize, I would buy a house.*

### Rule 9: Use *If it were not for / But for* (present) and *If it had not been for* (past) for "without".

> ***If it were not for** your help, I would fail.* = ***Were it not for** your help, ...* = ***But for** your help, ...* (present / general)
> ***If it had not been for** the doctor, he would have died.* = ***Had it not been for** the doctor, ...* = ***But for** the doctor, ...* (past)

*But for* + noun means "without". It is followed by the main clause with *would* or *would have*.

### Rule 10: A mixed conditional joins a past condition with a present result, or a present condition with a past result.

Sometimes the condition and the result are at **different times**.

**(a) Past condition → present result** (*if* + past perfect, *would* + V1):

> *If I **had taken** that job (past), I **would be** rich now (present).*
> *If she **had caught** the flight, she **would be** in Delhi now.*
> *If you **had listened** to the doctor, you **wouldn't feel** ill today.*

**(b) Present condition → past result** (*if* + past simple, *would have* + V3):

> *If I **were** braver (always, now), I **would have spoken** up at the meeting (past).*
> *If he **didn't** have an exam tomorrow, he **would have come** to the party last night.*

**Test:** look for time words. *now, today* in the main clause → present result. *yesterday, last year* in the main clause → past result.

### Rule 11: Never use *will* or *would* in the *if* clause.

| Type | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *~~If I will go~~ to Chennai, I'll visit you.* | ***If I go** to Chennai, I'll visit you.* |
| 2 | *~~If I would be~~ rich, I'd travel.* | ***If I were** rich, I'd travel.* |
| 3 | *~~If I would have known~~, I would have come.* | ***If I had known**, I would have come.* |

The only exception is polite *will / would* meaning "be willing": ***If you would** wait here, I'll call the manager.* (= Please wait.) This is not a normal conditional.

## Special cases

### 1. *If* + *should* (Type 1, less likely)

> *If you **should** see Ravi, tell him to call me.* (= if by chance you see him)

This is the source of the inverted form ***Should you** see Ravi, ...*

### 2. *Were to* for unlikely future events

> *If the earth **were to** stop spinning, what would happen?*
> ***Were** the earth **to** stop spinning, ...*

### 3. *Unless* with imperatives and modals

> ***Don't** call me **unless** it is urgent.*
> *You **can't** enter **unless** you have a ticket.*

### 4. *If not* at the end

> *Are you coming? **If not**, I'll go alone.*
> *We will finish today, **if not**, tomorrow.*

### 5. *Only if* and *if only*

> *I'll go **only if** you come with me.* (= this is the only condition)
> ***If only** I had studied harder!* (= I wish I had studied harder: a regret)

*If only* + past perfect is a regret about the past. *If only* + past simple is a wish about the present: ***If only** I **were** taller!*

### 6. *When* in Type 0 and Type 1

> ***When** you heat ice, it melts.* (Type 0: *when* = *if*)
> ***When** I reach Madurai, I'll call you.* (I'm sure I will reach.)

### 7. *Whether ... or not*

> *I'll go **whether** it rains **or not**.* (= even if it rains, and even if it doesn't)

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *If I ~~will go~~ there, I'll call you.* | *If I **go** there, I'll call you.* | No *will* in the *if* clause. |
| *If I ~~would have known~~, I would have helped.* | *If I **had known**, I would have helped.* | Type 3 *if* clause → past perfect. |
| *Unless you ~~don't~~ study, you will fail.* | *Unless you **study**, you will fail.* | *Unless* = *if not*. No double negative. |
| *~~Hadn't I~~ seen him, I would have left.* | ***Had I not** seen him, I would have left.* | Inversion uses the full form *Had I not*. |
| *~~Did I know~~ the answer, I would tell you.* | ***If I knew** the answer, I would tell you.* | Only *had, were, should* can be inverted. |
| *~~Was I~~ you, I would go.* | ***Were I** you, I would go.* | Inversion with *be* uses *were*. |
| *Take a sweater ~~if~~ it gets cold.* (meaning a precaution, take it now) | *Take a sweater **in case** it gets cold.* | *In case* = as a precaution. |
| *I will help you ~~unless~~ you ask me.* (meaning only if you ask) | *I will help you **if** you ask me.* / ***provided** you ask me* | *Unless* would mean "except if you ask", which is the opposite. |
| *If I had taken the job, I ~~would have been~~ rich now.* | *If I had taken the job, I **would be** rich now.* | *Now* shows a present result → mixed conditional, *would* + V1. |
| *You can go ~~provided you will~~ finish the work.* | *You can go **provided you finish** the work.* | *Provided* takes the present simple for the future. |
| *~~But for~~ he helped me, I would have failed.* | ***But for his help**, I would have failed.* | *But for* + noun, not a clause. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Fill in** the verbs in the correct form (including mixed conditionals).
- **Match** the halves.
- **Rewrite** using *unless* (or *if*).
- **Rewrite** with **inversion** (without *if*).
- **Choose** the right condition word (*if, unless, in case, provided*).

### Steps for "Rewrite using *unless*"

1. Find *if ... not* (or *if ... no / never*).
2. Replace *if* with *unless*.
3. Remove the negative (and the *do / does / did* that came with it).
4. Change the verb back to its normal form.
5. Leave the main clause **unchanged**.

### Steps for "Rewrite with inversion"

1. Find the type from the *if* clause.
2. Type 3 (*if ... had*) → ***Had*** + subject + V3.
3. Type 2 (*if ... were*) → ***Were*** + subject. Other verbs → ***Were*** + subject + ***to*** + V1.
4. Type 1 (*if ... should*, or formal *if* + present) → ***Should*** + subject + V1.
5. Negative → *Had / Were / Should* + subject + ***not***.
6. Remove *if*. Keep the main clause and the comma.

### Worked example 1

> *Rewrite using "unless": If you do not take your medicine, you will not get well.*

1. *If you do not take* → *Unless you take*.
2. Main clause unchanged: *you will not get well*.

**Answer:** ***Unless you take your medicine**, you will not get well.*

### Worked example 2

> *Rewrite without "if": If I had known about the strike, I would have stayed at home.*

1. Type 3 (*had known*).
2. *If I had known* → *Had I known*.

**Answer:** ***Had I known** about the strike, I would have stayed at home.*

### Worked example 3

> *Rewrite without "if": If you need any help, please call me.*

1. Type 1 (formal).
2. *If you need* → *Should you need*.

**Answer:** ***Should you need** any help, please call me.*

### Worked example 4

> *Rewrite without "if": If he worked harder, he would get a promotion.*

1. Type 2. The verb is *worked*, not *were*.
2. Use *Were* + subject + *to* + V1: *Were he to work harder*.

**Answer:** ***Were he to work** harder, he would get a promotion.*

### Worked example 5

> *Fill in: If Arun ______ (study) medicine ten years ago, he ______ (be) a doctor now.*

1. *ten years ago* → past condition → *had studied*.
2. *now* → present result → *would be*.
3. This is a mixed conditional.

**Answer:** *If Arun **had studied** medicine ten years ago, he **would be** a doctor now.*

### Worked example 6

> *Choose: Keep this number ______ you need it. (if / in case / unless)*

1. Keep it **now**, as a precaution, because you **may** need it.
2. Precaution → *in case*.

**Answer:** *Keep this number **in case** you need it.*

## Quick check

1. Rewrite using *unless*: *If you don't leave now, you'll miss the train.*
2. Rewrite without *if*: *If I were the captain, I would change the team.*
3. Rewrite without *if*: *If it had not rained, we would have gone out.*
4. *If I ______ (eat) breakfast this morning, I wouldn't be hungry now.*
5. *You can use my laptop ______ you don't install any games.* (in case / as long as)

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***Unless you leave now, you'll miss the train.*** Remove *don't*; keep the main clause.
2. ***Were I the captain, I would change the team.*** Type 2 with *were* → *Were I*.
3. ***Had it not rained, we would have gone out.*** Type 3 negative → *Had it not* (no short form).
4. ***had eaten.*** Past condition (*this morning*) with a present result (*now*) → mixed conditional: past perfect + *would* + V1.
5. ***as long as.*** It is a condition ("only if you don't install games"), not a precaution.

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- ***unless*** = ***if ... not***. Use a positive verb after it. Never *unless ... not*.
- ***provided / as long as*** = only if. ***in case*** = as a precaution. ***otherwise*** = if not. ***even if*** = whether or not.
- **Inversion:** *If I had known* → ***Had I known*** · *If I were you* → ***Were I you*** · *If you need* → ***Should you need***. Negative: ***Had I not*** (no *hadn't*).
- ***Were + to*** for other Type 2 verbs: ***Were he to work** harder...*
- **Mixed:** past condition + present result → *had* + V3, *would* + V1 (*If I had taken the job, I would be rich now*).
- ***But for*** + noun = without.
- **Never** *will* or *would* in the *if* clause.
:::
