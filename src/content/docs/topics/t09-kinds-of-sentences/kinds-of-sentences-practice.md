## Part A: Identify the kind of sentence

**1.** *The children played in the rain.* (simple / compound / complex)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Simple.** One finite verb (*played*).

</details>

**2.** *Kavya sang a song, and her brother played the tabla.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Compound.** Two finite verbs (*sang, played*), joined by the coordinating conjunction *and*. Both parts can stand alone.

</details>

**3.** *We stayed indoors because it was very hot.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Complex.** Two finite verbs (*stayed, was*). *Because it was very hot* cannot stand alone; it is a subordinate clause.

</details>

**4.** *Having finished his breakfast, Ravi left for school.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Simple.** **Trap:** the sentence looks long, but *having finished* is a non-finite verb (a perfect participle). The only finite verb is *left*.

</details>

**5.** *How beautifully she dances!* (by function)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Exclamatory.** It shows strong feeling and ends with an exclamation mark.

</details>

**6.** *Please pass the salt.* (by function)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Imperative.** It is a request. (The subject *you* is understood.)

</details>

## Part B: Identify the subordinate clause and its kind

**7.** *I know **that he is innocent**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Noun clause** (object of *know*). Test: *I know **it**.* ✔

</details>

**8.** *The temple **which we visited yesterday** is very old.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Adjective (relative) clause.** It describes the noun *temple* (which temple?).

</details>

**9.** *Wait here **until the bus arrives**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Adverb clause of time.** It tells **when** (how long) to wait.

</details>

**10.** *She studied hard **so that she might win a scholarship**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Adverb clause of purpose.** It tells **why**, with the aim of something.

</details>

**11.** *This is the village **where my grandfather was born**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Adjective clause.** **Trap:** *where* often starts an adverb clause of place, but here the clause comes after the noun *village* and describes it.

</details>

**12.** *Nobody knows **where he has gone**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Noun clause** (object of *knows*). Test: *Nobody knows **it**.* ✔ Compare with question 11: the same word *where*, but a different job.

</details>

**13.** ***Although it was raining**, the match continued.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Adverb clause of contrast (concession).** It gives a fact that we might expect to stop the match.

</details>

## Part C: Change simple ↔ compound ↔ complex

**14.** *Change into compound and complex: Being poor, he could not buy books.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

- **Compound:** *He was poor, **so** he could not buy books.*
- **Complex:** ***As** (or *Because / Since*) he was poor, he could not buy books.*

The relationship is **reason**.

</details>

**15.** *Change into simple and complex: The thief saw the police and ran away.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

- **Simple:** ***Seeing** the police, the thief ran away.* (or ***On seeing** the police, ...*)
- **Complex:** ***When** the thief saw the police, he ran away.*

The relationship is **time**.

</details>

**16.** *Change into compound and simple: Although she is rich, she is very humble.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

- **Compound:** *She is rich, **but** she is very humble.* (or *She is rich, **yet** ...*)
- **Simple:** ***In spite of** her riches* (or ***Despite** being rich*), *she is very humble.*

The relationship is **contrast**.

</details>

**17.** *Change into complex and simple: Work hard, and you will succeed.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

- **Complex:** ***If** you work hard, you will succeed.*
- **Simple:** ***By working** hard, you will succeed.*

The relationship is **condition** (positive).

</details>

**18.** *Change into complex: Hurry up, or you will miss the train.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***If you do not hurry up**, you will miss the train.* (or ***Unless you hurry up**, you will miss the train.*) The relationship is a negative condition. **Trap:** with *unless*, do not add *not*.

</details>

**19.** *Change into complex: The winner of the race is my cousin.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The boy (or girl / person) **who won the race** is my cousin.* The phrase *the winner of the race* becomes an adjective clause.

</details>

**20.** *Change into simple: He confessed that he was guilty.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*He confessed **his guilt**.* The noun clause becomes a noun phrase.

</details>

**21.** *Change into complex: She saved money to buy a scooter.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*She saved money **so that she could buy** a scooter.* (or *... in order that she might buy ...*) The infinitive of purpose becomes an adverb clause of purpose.

</details>

**22.** *Change into simple: She is not only a good singer but also a good dancer.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Besides being** a good singer, she is a good dancer.* (or ***In addition to being** a good singer, ...*)

</details>

**23.** *Change into compound: After he had finished his homework, he watched TV.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*He finished his homework **and then** watched TV.* (or *He finished his homework, **and** he watched TV.*)

</details>

## Part D: Other transformations

**24.** *Remove "too": The coffee is too hot to drink.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The coffee is **so** hot **that** it **cannot** be drunk.* (or *... so hot that we cannot drink **it***.) *Too ... to* has a negative meaning, so *so ... that* needs *cannot*.

</details>

**25.** *Remove "too": The bag was too heavy for the child to carry.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The bag was **so** heavy **that** the child **could not** carry **it**.* Past tense → *could not*. *Carry* needs an object → *it*.

</details>

**26.** *Change into negative: Only Meena knows the answer.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***None but** Meena knows the answer.* (or *Nobody except Meena knows the answer*.) *Only* + a person → *none but*.

</details>

**27.** *Change into negative: As soon as the bell rang, the students ran out.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***No sooner had** the bell rung **than** the students ran out.* *No sooner* takes the past perfect with inversion (*had the bell rung*) and is followed by *than*.

</details>

**28.** *Change into interrogative: Everyone loves a holiday.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Who does not** love a holiday?* A positive statement becomes a negative question with the same meaning.

</details>

**29.** *Change into assertive: What is the use of crying now?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***It is no use** crying now.* (or *There is no use in crying now*.) The question has an obvious answer: "no use".

</details>

**30.** *Change into assertive: What an exciting match it was!*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*It was **a very exciting** match.* *What a / an* + adjective → *very* + adjective. The exclamation mark becomes a full stop.

</details>

**31.** *Change into exclamatory: It is a very hot day.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***What a hot day it is!*** (or ***How hot the day is!***) *Very* is removed and *What a* / *How* is added.

</details>

## Part E: Combine into one sentence

**32.** *Combine into a simple sentence: Divya went to the market. She wanted to buy vegetables.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Divya went to the market **to buy** vegetables.* The purpose becomes a *to*-infinitive.

</details>

**33.** *Combine into a complex sentence: This is Mr Rajan. He teaches us physics.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*This is Mr Rajan, **who teaches us physics**.* An adjective clause with *who*. (Simple: *This is Mr Rajan, **our physics teacher**.*)

</details>

**34.** *Combine into a simple sentence: The rain stopped. We went out to play.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The rain having stopped**, we went out to play.* **Trap:** the subjects are different (*the rain, we*). A plain participle (*~~Having stopped, we went out~~*) would mean *we* stopped. Use the nominative absolute: noun + participle.

</details>
