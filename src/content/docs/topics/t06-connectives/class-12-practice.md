## Part A: Choose the correct linker

**1.** *I wanted to go out, ______ it was raining heavily.* (and / but / so)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***but.*** The two ideas contrast: I wanted to go out, and the rain was against it.

</details>

**2.** *She was hungry, ______ she made some upma.* (so / yet / or)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***so.*** Making upma is the **result** of being hungry.

</details>

**3.** *Hurry up, ______ you will miss the bus.* (and / or / but)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***or.*** *Or* here means "if not": if you don't hurry, you will miss the bus.

</details>

**4.** *______ the roads were flooded, the schools were closed.* (Although / As / Unless)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***As*** (or *Since / Because*). The flooding is the **reason** for closing the schools.

</details>

**5.** *______ the heavy traffic, we reached the station on time.* (Although / Despite / Because)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Despite*** (or *In spite of*). A noun phrase (*the heavy traffic*) follows, not a clause, so *although* is wrong.

</details>

**6.** *______ he had a fever, he wrote the exam.* (Even though / In spite of / Due to)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Even though.*** A clause (*he had a fever*) follows, and the idea is a strong contrast.

</details>

**7.** *Ravi likes spicy food, ______ his sister prefers sweet dishes.* (whereas / because / so that)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***whereas*** (or *while*). Two facts are compared side by side.

</details>

**8.** *Keep the milk in the fridge ______ it should go bad.* (lest / unless / so)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***lest.*** *Lest* means "so that ... not". It is followed by *should*, without *not*.

</details>

**9.** *Speak clearly ______ everyone can hear you.* (so that / so ... that / such that)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***so that.*** It shows **purpose** (why you should speak clearly).

</details>

**10.** *The exam was ______ difficult ______ many students left questions blank.* (so ... that / such ... that)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***so*** ... ***that.*** An adjective (*difficult*) comes in the middle, with no noun → *so ... that*. It shows **result**.

</details>

**11.** *It was ______ a beautiful morning ______ we decided to walk to school.* (so ... that / such ... that)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***such*** ... ***that.*** *A* + adjective + noun (*a beautiful morning*) → *such ... that*.

</details>

**12.** *You can use my phone ______ you don't make long calls.* (as long as / even if / whereas)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***as long as*** (or *provided that*). It gives a **condition**.

</details>

**13.** *The hotel is near the beach. ______, the rooms are very cheap.* (However / Moreover / Therefore)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Moreover*** (or *Furthermore / In addition*). Both are good points about the hotel → **addition**.

</details>

**14.** *The hotel is near the beach. ______, the rooms are very small.* (However / Moreover / Therefore)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***However.*** **Trap:** compare with question 13. Small rooms are a bad point after a good one → **contrast**.

</details>

**15.** *He did not prepare for the interview. ______, he was not selected.* (Nevertheless / Consequently / Furthermore)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Consequently*** (or *As a result / Therefore*). Not being selected is the **result** of not preparing.

</details>

**16.** *She was very tired. ______, she helped her mother in the kitchen.* (Therefore / Nevertheless / Moreover)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Nevertheless*** (or *However / Even so*). Helping is surprising after being tired → **contrast**.

</details>

## Part B: Combine using the linker given in brackets

**17.** *The shop was crowded. We bought what we needed.* (although)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Although** the shop was crowded, we bought what we needed.* (or *We bought what we needed **although** the shop was crowded.*)

</details>

**18.** *The shop was crowded. We bought what we needed.* (in spite of)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***In spite of the crowd** in the shop, we bought what we needed.* (or ***In spite of the fact that** the shop was crowded, ...*) *In spite of* needs a noun, so the clause changes to *the crowd*.

</details>

**19.** *The guests arrived. The rain started.* (no sooner ... than)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***No sooner had** the guests **arrived than** the rain started.* The first action takes the past perfect with inversion (*had the guests arrived*); the second takes the past simple after *than*.

</details>

**20.** *I had just closed my eyes. The doorbell rang.* (hardly ... when)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Hardly had** I closed my eyes **when** the doorbell rang.* *Hardly* pairs with *when*, not *than*.

</details>

**21.** *Meena can sing. She can also dance.* (not only ... but also)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Meena can **not only** sing **but also** dance.* Each part is followed by a verb (*sing / dance*), so the sentence is balanced.

</details>

**22.** *Arun didn't call me. He didn't send a message.* (neither ... nor)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Arun **neither** called me **nor** sent a message.* *Neither ... nor* is already negative, so *didn't* goes. The verbs become past simple (*called, sent*).

</details>

**23.** *You can come by bus. You can come by train.* (either ... or)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You can come **either** by bus **or** by train.* Each part is followed by a phrase of the same kind (*by bus / by train*).

</details>

**24.** *He worked hard. He wanted to buy a house.* (so that)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*He worked hard **so that** he **could** buy a house.* *So that* + *could* (past) shows purpose.

</details>

**25.** *It was very cold. We lit a fire.* (so ... that)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*It was **so** cold **that** we lit a fire.* *Very* is replaced by *so*.

</details>

**26.** *You must finish your homework. Then you can watch TV.* (provided that)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You can watch TV **provided that** you finish your homework.* The homework is the **condition**. Note the present tense after *provided that*.

</details>

**27.** *It rained heavily. The match was not cancelled.* (however)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*It rained heavily; **however,** the match was not cancelled.* (or *It rained heavily. **However,** the match was not cancelled.*) A linking adverb needs a semicolon or a full stop before it and a comma after it.

</details>

**28.** *He was ill. He went to school.* (despite)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Despite** his illness, he went to school.* (or ***Despite being** ill, ...*) *Despite* + noun or V4, never a clause.

</details>

## Part C: Complete the paragraph

> *Karthik wanted to become a doctor, ______ (29) he studied very hard. ______ (30) his family was poor, he never gave up. He studied at night ______ (31) the street lights were on. ______ (32), he won a scholarship to a medical college in Madurai.*

**29.**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***so.*** Studying hard is the **result** of wanting to become a doctor. A comma comes before *so*, joining two clauses.

</details>

**30.**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Although*** (or *Though / Even though*). Being poor might have stopped him, but it didn't → **contrast**. A clause follows.

</details>

**31.**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***while*** (or *when*). It shows **time**: during the time the lights were on.

</details>

**32.**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Finally*** (or *As a result / In the end*). It begins a new sentence and shows the outcome of all his work.

</details>

## Part D: Find and correct the mistake

**33.** *Although she was tired, but she finished the work.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Although** she was tired, she finished the work.* (or *She was tired, **but** she finished the work.*) Use one linker for one connection. *Although* and *but* together is wrong.

</details>

**34.** *Both my brother as well as my sister live in Chennai.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Both** my brother **and** my sister live in Chennai.* *Both* always pairs with *and*. (*My brother as well as my sister **lives** in Chennai* is also correct, but note the singular verb.)

</details>
