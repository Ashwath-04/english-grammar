## What you will learn

- **Coordinating conjunctions**: *and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet*
- **Correlative conjunctions** (used in pairs): *either ... or, neither ... nor, both ... and, not only ... but also, whether ... or, no sooner ... than, hardly ... when*
- **Subordinating conjunctions**, grouped by meaning:
  - time: *when, while, as soon as, before, after, until, since*
  - reason: *because, since, as*
  - purpose: *so that, in order that, lest*
  - result: *so ... that, such ... that*
  - contrast: *although, though, even though, whereas, while*
  - condition: *if, unless, provided that, as long as*
- **Linking adverbs and phrases**: *however, therefore, moreover, furthermore, nevertheless, consequently, in addition, on the other hand, as a result*
- **Punctuation** with linkers (*; however,*)
- ***Despite / in spite of*** + noun vs ***although*** + clause
- How to answer "Fill in with linkers", "Combine using the linker given" and "Complete the paragraph"

## Why this matters

Linkers are the "glue" of writing. They show how ideas are connected: one idea adds to another, contrasts with it, causes it, or follows it in time. Without them, writing is a list of separate sentences.

The exam asks you to fill in linkers, combine sentences with a given linker, and complete paragraphs. Linkers are also needed in every essay, letter and report you write. This lesson builds on [Kinds of sentences and clauses](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/).

## The idea in simple words

Read these two sentences.

> *It was raining. We went out.*

They are correct, but we don't know how they are connected. Now add a linker:

> *It was raining, **so** we stayed at home.* (result)
> *It was raining, **but** we went out.* (contrast)
> ***Although** it was raining, we went out.* (contrast)
> *It was raining. **However**, we went out.* (contrast, across two sentences)
> ***Because** it was raining, we stayed at home.* (reason)

The linker tells the reader what kind of connection there is. Choosing the right linker means first deciding the **connection**: addition, contrast, reason, result, time, purpose or condition.

## Grammar words you need

**Connective (linker):** any word or phrase that joins words, clauses or sentences and shows how they are related.

**Conjunction:** a word that joins words or clauses inside one sentence: *and, but, because, although*.

**Coordinating conjunction:** joins two parts of **equal** importance: *and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet*.

**Correlative conjunctions:** conjunctions used in **pairs**: *either ... or, both ... and*.

**Subordinating conjunction:** joins a **subordinate clause** to a main clause: *because, when, although, if*.

**Linking adverb (conjunctive adverb):** a word that connects the idea of one sentence or clause to another, but does not grammatically join them: *however, therefore, moreover*.

**Clause:** a group of words with a subject and a finite verb. See [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/).

## The rules

### Rule 1: Coordinating conjunctions join two equal parts. Each has its own meaning.

| Conjunction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***and*** | addition | *Ravi plays the flute **and** his sister sings.* |
| ***but*** | contrast | *The shop was open, **but** there was no one inside.* |
| ***or*** | choice | *You can have tea **or** coffee.* |
| ***nor*** | negative addition | *He did not call, **nor** did he write.* |
| ***for*** | reason (formal) | *She stayed at home, **for** she was unwell.* |
| ***so*** | result | *It was late, **so** we took an auto.* |
| ***yet*** | surprising contrast | *He is very rich, **yet** he lives simply.* |

(An easy way to remember them: **FANBOYS** = **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o.)

Notes:
- After *nor* at the start of a clause, the auxiliary comes before the subject: *nor **did he** write*.
- *For* (meaning "because") is formal and comes only in the **middle**, never at the start.

### Rule 2: Put a comma before a coordinating conjunction that joins two full clauses.

> *The bell rang, **and** the students went home.*
> *I called her, **but** she did not answer.*

When the clauses are very short, the comma can be left out: *She sang **and** he danced.*

No comma is needed when the conjunction joins two words or phrases: *bread **and** butter*, *slowly **but** steadily*.

### Rule 3: Correlative conjunctions come in pairs. Put each part before the same kind of word.

| Pair | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***both ... and*** | the two together | ***Both** Arun **and** Kavya passed.* |
| ***either ... or*** | one or the other | *You can **either** walk **or** take the bus.* |
| ***neither ... nor*** | not one and not the other | *He **neither** smokes **nor** drinks.* |
| ***not only ... but also*** | the first, and the second too | *She is **not only** clever **but also** hard-working.* |
| ***whether ... or*** | it does not matter which | *I will go **whether** it rains **or** not.* |
| ***no sooner ... than*** | immediately after | ***No sooner** had he arrived **than** it began to rain.* |
| ***hardly / scarcely ... when*** | immediately after | ***Hardly** had I sat down **when** the phone rang.* |

**Keep the two parts balanced (parallel).** The word after the first part and the word after the second part should be the same kind of word.

> *She is **not only** clever **but also** kind.* ✔ (adjective / adjective)
> *~~She not only is clever but also kind.~~* ✘
> *He **either** lost the key **or** left it at home.* ✔ (verb / verb)

**Fixed partners.** Do not mix the pairs:

> *~~Both ... as well as~~* ✘ → *both ... and* ✔
> *~~Neither ... or~~* ✘ → *neither ... nor* ✔
> *~~No sooner ... when~~* ✘ → *no sooner ... than* ✔
> *~~Hardly ... than~~* ✘ → *hardly ... when* ✔

For the verb that follows *either ... or* and *neither ... nor*, see [Subject–verb agreement](/topics/t05-subject-verb-agreement/class-12/).

### Rule 4: *No sooner ... than* and *hardly ... when* take the past perfect and inversion.

> *As soon as the guests arrived, the lights went off.*
> → ***No sooner had** the guests **arrived than** the lights went off.*
> → ***Hardly had** the guests **arrived when** the lights went off.*

The first part: *No sooner / Hardly / Scarcely* + ***had*** + subject + V3.
The second part: *than / when* + past simple.

### Rule 5: Use *when, while, as soon as, before, after, until* and *since* to show time.

| Linker | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *when* | at the time that | ***When** I reached home, it was dark.* |
| *while* | during the time that (two actions together) | ***While** Amma was cooking, I set the table.* |
| *as* | at the same moment | ***As** I opened the door, the cat ran out.* |
| *as soon as* | immediately after | *Call me **as soon as** you reach Madurai.* |
| *before* | earlier than | *Brush your teeth **before** you go to bed.* |
| *after* | later than | ***After** the rain stopped, we went out.* |
| *until / till* | up to the time that | *Wait **until** I come back.* |
| *since* | from a past time up to now | *I have known her **since** we were children.* |
| *whenever* | every time that | ***Whenever** it rains, the road floods.* |

After time linkers, use the present tense for the future: *Call me **as soon as** you **reach*** (not *~~will reach~~*). See [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/).

### Rule 6: Use *because, since* and *as* to give a reason.

> *We cancelled the trip **because** it was raining.*
> ***Since** you are busy, I will come tomorrow.*
> ***As** it was late, we took a taxi.*

| Linker | Use |
|---|---|
| *because* | the most direct; gives new information; answers *Why?* (*Why are you late? Because the bus broke down.*) |
| *since, as* | the reason is already known or less important; often at the start |

### Rule 7: Use *so that, in order that* and *lest* to show purpose.

> *She saved money **so that** she **could** buy a laptop.*
> *We left early **in order that** we **might** catch the first bus.* (formal)
> *Walk carefully **lest** you **should** fall.* (= so that you do not fall)

| Linker | Usually followed by |
|---|---|
| *so that* | *can / could / will / would / may / might* |
| *in order that* | *may / might* (formal) |
| *lest* | *should* + V1 (formal; no *not*) |

*Lest* already means "so that ... not". Never add *not*: *~~lest you should not fall~~* ✘

A shorter way to show purpose is a *to*-infinitive: *She saved money **to buy** a laptop.* See [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/).

### Rule 8: Use *so ... that* and *such ... that* to show result.

> *The tea was **so** hot **that** I could not drink it.*
> *He spoke **so** fast **that** nobody understood him.*
> *It was **such** a hot day **that** we stayed indoors.*
> *They are **such** good friends **that** they share everything.*

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| *so* + adjective / adverb + *that* | *so tired that, so quickly that* |
| *such* + (*a / an*) + adjective + noun + *that* | *such a good film that, such kind people that* |

Do not confuse *so that* (purpose) with *so ... that* (result):

> *He worked hard **so that** he could pass.* (purpose: why he worked)
> *He worked **so** hard **that** he passed.* (result: what happened)

### Rule 9: Use *although, though, even though, whereas* and *while* to show contrast.

| Linker | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *although / though* | a fact that we might expect to stop the main idea | ***Although** he was tired, he kept working.* |
| *even though* | stronger contrast | ***Even though** she had a fever, she wrote the exam.* |
| *whereas / while* | two facts compared side by side | *Ravi likes cricket, **whereas** his brother likes football.* |
| *even if* | contrast + condition (it may or may not be true) | *I will go **even if** it rains.* |

*Though* can also come at the **end** of a sentence, as an adverb (in speech): *The film was long. It was good, **though**.*

**Never use *although* and *but* together:**

> *~~Although he is poor, but he is honest.~~* ✘
> ***Although** he is poor, he is honest.* ✔ / *He is poor, **but** he is honest.* ✔

### Rule 10: Use *if, unless, provided (that)* and *as long as* for condition.

> ***If** you study well, you will pass.*
> ***Unless** you study, you will fail.* (= *If you do not study...*)
> *You can go out **provided that** you finish your homework.*
> *You can borrow my bike **as long as** you return it by six.*
> *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* (as a precaution)

*Unless* already means *if ... not*. Don't add another *not*. Full lessons: [Conditional clauses (Class 11)](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-11/) and [Class 12](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-12/).

### Rule 11: Linking adverbs connect ideas across sentences. Choose them by meaning.

| Meaning | Linking adverbs and phrases |
|---|---|
| **Addition** | *moreover, furthermore, in addition, besides, also, what is more* |
| **Contrast** | *however, nevertheless, nonetheless, on the other hand, still, even so, in contrast* |
| **Result** | *therefore, consequently, as a result, hence, thus, accordingly* |
| **Example** | *for example, for instance, such as* |
| **Sequence** | *first, firstly, then, next, after that, finally, lastly* |
| **Summary** | *in short, in brief, to sum up, in conclusion, on the whole* |
| **Truth / emphasis** | *in fact, indeed, actually, of course* |
| **Alternative** | *otherwise, instead, alternatively* |

> *The road was blocked. **However**, we reached on time.*
> *She is a good student. **Moreover**, she is very helpful.*
> *He did not study. **As a result**, he failed.*
> *Plastic harms the earth. **Therefore**, we should use cloth bags.*

**Furthermore** and **moreover** add a **stronger** or more important point. ***On the other hand*** compares two sides of the same topic (often after *On the one hand*).

### Rule 12: A linking adverb needs a full stop or a semicolon before it, and usually a comma after it.

A linking adverb is **not** a conjunction. It cannot join two clauses with only a comma.

> *It was raining; **however,** we went out.* ✔
> *It was raining. **However,** we went out.* ✔
> *~~It was raining, however we went out.~~* ✘ (a comma alone is not enough)

A linking adverb can also come in the middle or at the end of its clause, with commas:

> *We, **however,** went out.*
> *We went out, **however**.*

**Compare** with coordinating conjunctions, which need only a comma:

> *It was raining, **but** we went out.* ✔

### Rule 13: *Despite* and *in spite of* are followed by a noun or a V4. *Although* is followed by a clause.

| Followed by | Linker | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a **noun** or **V4** | *despite* / *in spite of* | ***Despite** the rain, we played.* / ***In spite of** being tired, she kept working.* |
| a **clause** (subject + verb) | *although / though / even though* | ***Although** it was raining, we played.* |
| a **clause**, but starting with *despite* | *despite the fact that* / *in spite of the fact that* | ***Despite the fact that** it was raining, we played.* |

The same pattern works for reason:

| Followed by a noun | Followed by a clause |
|---|---|
| *because of* the rain | *because* it rained |
| *due to / owing to* the strike | *as* there was a strike |
| *during* the film | *while* the film was on |

> *~~Despite it was raining~~* ✘ / *~~Although the rain~~* ✘
> *~~Despite of the rain~~* ✘ (*despite* never takes *of*)

## Special cases

### 1. *Since* and *as*: time or reason

> ***Since** it was late, we left.* (reason)
> *She has lived here **since** 2010.* (time)
> ***As** I was walking home, I met Divya.* (time)
> ***As** you are here, please help me.* (reason)

### 2. *While*: time or contrast

> ***While** I was reading, the lights went off.* (time)
> ***While** Chennai is hot, Ooty is cool.* (contrast)

### 3. *Not only* at the start of a sentence

When *not only* begins the sentence, the auxiliary comes before the subject (inversion):

> ***Not only did** he win the race, **but** he **also** broke the record.*

### 4. *Whether* or *if*

Both can mean "it is not known which". Use ***whether***:
- before *or not*: *I don't know **whether or not** he will come.*
- after a preposition: *We talked **about whether** to go.*
- at the start of a subject clause: ***Whether** he comes is not important.*

### 5. *Even if* and *even though*

> ***Even though** it is raining, I will go.* (It **is** raining.)
> ***Even if** it rains, I will go.* (It may or may not rain.)

### 6. *Because* and *so* together

Never use two linkers for one connection.

> *~~Because he was ill, so he stayed home.~~* ✘
> ***Because** he was ill, he stayed home.* ✔ / *He was ill, **so** he stayed home.* ✔

### 7. *Besides* and *beside*

*Besides* (with *s*) as a linker means "in addition": ***Besides**, the hotel is near the beach.* *Beside* means "next to" and is not a linker. See [Prepositions (Class 11)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-11/).

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Although he is rich, ~~but~~ he is unhappy.* | *Although he is rich, he is unhappy.* | One linker for one connection. |
| *~~Despite~~ it was cold, we went out.* | ***Although** it was cold, we went out.* / ***Despite the cold**, we went out.* | *Despite* + noun, not a clause. |
| *~~In spite~~ the rain, they played.* | ***In spite of** the rain, they played.* | *In spite* always needs *of*. |
| *Both Ravi ~~as well as~~ Arun came.* | ***Both** Ravi **and** Arun came.* | *Both* pairs with *and*. |
| *Neither Meena ~~or~~ Divya knew.* | ***Neither** Meena **nor** Divya knew.* | *Neither* pairs with *nor*. |
| *No sooner had he left ~~when~~ she arrived.* | *No sooner had he left **than** she arrived.* | *No sooner* pairs with *than*. |
| *Hardly had he left ~~than~~ she arrived.* | *Hardly had he left **when** she arrived.* | *Hardly* pairs with *when*. |
| *Study well lest you ~~should not~~ fail.* | *Study well **lest** you **should** fail.* | *Lest* already means "so that ... not". |
| *It was late, ~~however~~ we continued.* | *It was late; **however,** we continued.* | A linking adverb needs a semicolon or full stop before it. |
| *He was ~~so~~ a good player that he was selected.* | *He was **such** a good player that he was selected.* | *Such* + *a* + adjective + noun. |
| *She is not only ~~beautiful but also has~~ talent.* | *She is not only **beautiful** but also **talented**.* | Keep the two parts parallel (adjective / adjective). |
| *Unless you don't hurry, you will be late.* | ***Unless** you hurry, you will be late.* | *Unless* already contains *not*. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Fill in the blanks** with suitable linkers (often from a box).
- **Combine** two sentences using the linker given in brackets.
- **Complete a paragraph** with linkers.

### Steps for "Fill in with a suitable linker"

1. **Read both ideas.** What is the connection?
   - adds more → *and, moreover, in addition*
   - opposite / unexpected → *but, although, however, yet*
   - cause → *because, since, as*
   - effect → *so, therefore, as a result*
   - time → *when, while, after, before, until*
   - aim → *so that, in order to*
   - condition → *if, unless, provided that*
2. **Look at the grammar after the blank.**
   - a noun or V4 → *despite, in spite of, because of*
   - a clause → *although, because, when...*
   - a new sentence (after a full stop) → *However, Therefore, Moreover...*
3. **Check punctuation**: full stop or semicolon before a linking adverb; comma after it.
4. **Check the pairs**: *both ... and, either ... or, neither ... nor, no sooner ... than, hardly ... when*.

### Steps for "Combine using the linker given"

1. **Find where the linker goes**: at the start, or between the clauses.
2. **Change the grammar if needed**: a clause after *despite* → noun; *no sooner* → past perfect + inversion.
3. **Remove extra words** (*but, so, it*) that the linker makes unnecessary.
4. **Add punctuation**: a comma after a subordinate clause that comes first.

### Worked example 1

> *Combine using "although": He practised hard. He lost the match.*

1. Connection: contrast. *Although* + clause.
2. Put *although* before the unexpected-fact clause (*He practised hard*).
3. Comma after the subordinate clause.

**Answer:** ***Although** he practised hard, he lost the match.* (or *He lost the match **although** he practised hard.*)

### Worked example 2

> *Combine using "despite": It was very hot. The children played outside.*

1. *Despite* + noun (not a clause).
2. *It was very hot* → noun phrase: *the great heat* / *the hot weather*.

**Answer:** ***Despite the hot weather**, the children played outside.* (or ***Despite the fact that** it was very hot, ...*)

### Worked example 3

> *Combine using "no sooner ... than": The teacher entered the class. The students stood up.*

1. First action: *The teacher entered*. Use past perfect + inversion: *No sooner had the teacher entered*.
2. Second action: *than* + past simple: *than the students stood up*.

**Answer:** ***No sooner had** the teacher **entered** the class **than** the students stood up.*

### Worked example 4

> *Fill in with a suitable linker: The bus was late. ______, we missed the first period.* (However / As a result / Moreover)

1. Connection: the bus was late → we missed the period. That is a **result**.
2. After a full stop → linking adverb.

**Answer:** *The bus was late. **As a result**, we missed the first period.*

### Worked example 5

> *Complete the paragraph: Plastic is cheap ______ (1) useful. ______ (2), it causes serious harm to the environment. It does not break down easily, ______ (3) it stays in the soil for hundreds of years. ______ (4), we should reduce our use of plastic.*

1. ***and***: adds another good point (cheap + useful).
2. ***However***: contrast with the good points, after a full stop.
3. ***so***: result (it does not break down → it stays in the soil).
4. ***Therefore*** (or ***Hence*** / ***For this reason***): a conclusion drawn from all the reasons.

## Quick check

1. *______ it was a holiday, the office was open.* (Despite / Although)
2. *She studied hard ______ she could get a scholarship.* (so that / so ... that)
3. *Combine using "such ... that": It was a boring film. Many people left early.*
4. *I was tired; ______, I finished the work.* (therefore / nevertheless)
5. Correct the error: *Neither the principal or the teachers were present.*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***Although.*** A clause (*it was a holiday*) follows. *Despite* would need a noun (*Despite the holiday*).
2. ***so that.*** It shows purpose (why she studied hard). *So ... that* would need an adjective or adverb between (*so hard that*).
3. ***It was such a boring film that many people left early.*** *Such* + *a* + adjective + noun + *that*.
4. ***nevertheless.*** Tired → finished the work is an unexpected result (contrast). *Therefore* would mean the tiredness caused the finishing.
5. ***Neither the principal nor the teachers were present.*** *Neither* pairs with *nor*.

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- First decide the **connection**: addition, contrast, reason, result, time, purpose or condition.
- **Coordinating** (*and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet*) join equal clauses, with a comma.
- **Correlatives** come in fixed pairs: *both ... and, either ... or, neither ... nor, not only ... but also, no sooner ... than, hardly ... when*.
- **Subordinating** linkers start a clause: *although, because, when, so that, if, unless*.
- **Linking adverbs** (*however, therefore, moreover*) need a full stop or semicolon before them and a comma after.
- *despite / in spite of / because of* + **noun**. *although / because* + **clause**.
- One linker per connection: never *although ... but*, *because ... so*.
:::
