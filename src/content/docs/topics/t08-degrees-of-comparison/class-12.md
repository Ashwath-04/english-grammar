## What you will learn

- The three degrees: **positive**, **comparative** and **superlative**
- How to form them: *-er / -est*, *more / most*, and the spelling rules
- The irregular forms: *good – better – best*, *bad – worse – worst*, *little – less – least*, *many / much – more – most*, *far – farther / further*, *old – older / elder*, *late – later / latter*
- The patterns: *as ... as*, *not so / as ... as*, *than*, *the* + superlative
- **Latin comparatives** with *to*: *senior to, junior to, superior to, inferior to, prior to, prefer to*
- **Changing the degree** without changing the meaning (the key exam skill), including sentences with *one of the*
- The common errors: *~~more better~~*, *~~elder than~~*, *~~than any city~~*

## Why this matters

We compare things all the time: prices, marks, cricket players, cities. English has a clear system for this, and the exam tests it in two ways.

1. **Fill in** with the right form, or **spot the error** (*~~more taller~~*, *~~senior than~~*).
2. **Change the degree**: rewrite a superlative sentence as a comparative and a positive one, with the same meaning. This is asked almost every year, and it follows a fixed method.

## The idea in simple words

Look at three friends and their heights.

> *Ravi is **tall**.*
> *Arun is **taller than** Ravi.*
> *Karthik is **the tallest** of the three.*

- *tall* describes one person, with no comparison. This is the **positive degree**.
- *taller than* compares **two** people. This is the **comparative degree**.
- *the tallest* compares one person with **all the others** in a group. This is the **superlative degree**.

The same fact can be said in all three degrees:

> *Karthik is **the tallest** boy in the class.* (superlative)
> *Karthik is **taller than any other** boy in the class.* (comparative)
> ***No other** boy in the class is **as tall as** Karthik.* (positive)

All three sentences mean exactly the same thing. Changing between them is called **interchange of degrees**.

## Grammar words you need

**Degree of comparison:** the form of an adjective or adverb that shows how much of a quality something has, compared with others.

**Positive degree:** the plain form: *tall, good, beautiful*. Used alone or with *as ... as*.

**Comparative degree:** compares two persons, things or groups: *taller, better, more beautiful*. Usually followed by *than*.

**Superlative degree:** compares one with all the others in a group of three or more: *tallest, best, most beautiful*. Usually has *the* before it.

**Syllable:** a beat in a word. *tall* (1), *hap-py* (2), *beau-ti-ful* (3).

**Adjective:** a word that describes a noun (*a **tall** boy*). **Adverb:** a word that describes a verb (*he ran **fast***). Both have degrees. See [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/).

## The rules

### Rule 1: Short adjectives (one syllable) add *-er* and *-est*.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| tall | taller | tallest |
| short | shorter | shortest |
| small | smaller | smallest |
| rich | richer | richest |

Spelling changes:

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Ends in *-e*: add *-r*, *-st* | large → larger → largest; wise → wiser → wisest |
| One vowel + one consonant: double the consonant | big → bigger → biggest; hot → hotter → hottest; thin → thinner → thinnest |
| Consonant + *-y*: change *y* to *i* | dry → drier → driest |

### Rule 2: Two-syllable adjectives ending in *-y* change *y* to *i* and add *-er*, *-est*.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| happy | happier | happiest |
| easy | easier | easiest |
| busy | busier | busiest |
| heavy | heavier | heaviest |
| pretty | prettier | prettiest |

### Rule 3: Longer adjectives (most two-syllable words and all three-syllable words) use *more* and *most*.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| beautiful | more beautiful | most beautiful |
| intelligent | more intelligent | most intelligent |
| careful | more careful | most careful |
| famous | more famous | most famous |
| modern | more modern | most modern |

Some two-syllable adjectives can take either form: *clever, narrow, simple, quiet, gentle, polite, common*.

> *cleverer / more clever, simplest / most simple*

### Rule 4: Adverbs ending in *-ly* use *more* and *most*. Short adverbs add *-er*, *-est*.

> *quickly → more quickly → most quickly*
> *carefully → more carefully → most carefully*
> *fast → faster → fastest; hard → harder → hardest; soon → sooner → soonest*
> *early → earlier → earliest* (here *-ly* is part of the word, not an ending)

### Rule 5: Learn the irregular forms.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| good / well | better | best | |
| bad / badly / ill | worse | worst | |
| little (amount) | less | least | with uncountable nouns |
| many / much | more | most | *many* for countable, *much* for uncountable |
| far | farther / further | farthest / furthest | see below |
| old | older / elder | oldest / eldest | see below |
| late | later / latter | latest / last | see below |

**far: farther or further?**
- *farther / farthest*: distance only. *Salem is **farther** from Chennai than Vellore.*
- *further / furthest*: distance **or** "more, additional". *For **further** details, call this number.*

**old: older or elder?**
- *older / oldest*: people and things, with *than*. *My brother is **older than** me.*
- *elder / eldest*: only for **members of a family**, only **before a noun**, and never with *than*. *my **elder** brother*, *her **eldest** daughter*.
- *~~He is elder than me~~* ✘ → *He is **older than** me* ✔

**late: later / latest or latter / last?**
- *later, latest*: **time**. *Come **later**.* *the **latest** news* (the newest).
- *latter, last*: **order** or position. *Of tea and coffee, I prefer the **latter**.* (the second one) *the **last** bench* (at the end).

### Rule 6: Use the positive degree with *as ... as* for equal things, and *not so / not as ... as* for unequal things.

> *Meena is **as tall as** Priya.* (equal)
> *This bag is **as heavy as** that one.*
> *Ravi is **not so tall as** Arun.* (= Arun is taller)
> *The test was **not as difficult as** I thought.*

The adjective between *as ... as* is always in the **positive** form: *~~as taller as~~* ✘.

*Not so ... as* is the traditional form in negatives. *Not as ... as* is also correct and more common today.

### Rule 7: Use the comparative with *than* to compare two persons, things or groups.

> *Chennai is **hotter than** Ooty.*
> *This book is **more interesting than** that one.*
> *Divya sings **better than** her sister.*

With **two** things, use the comparative with *the* and *of the two*:

> *Kavya is **the taller of the two** sisters.* (not *~~the tallest of the two~~*)

### Rule 8: Use *the* + superlative to compare one with all others in a group.

> *Mount Everest is **the highest** mountain **in** the world.*
> *Arun is **the best** player **in** our team.*
> *This is **the most beautiful** temple I have ever seen.*
> *She is **the youngest of** all the students.*

Use ***in*** + a place or group (*in the class, in India*). Use ***of*** + a number or *all* (*of the three, of all*).

### Rule 9: Latin comparatives take *to*, not *than*.

Some adjectives from Latin end in *-ior*. They are already comparative, so they use ***to***.

| Adjective | Example |
|---|---|
| senior to | *Ravi is **senior to** me by two years.* |
| junior to | *She is **junior to** her colleague.* |
| superior to | *This cloth is **superior to** that one.* |
| inferior to | *Cheap phones are often **inferior to** branded ones.* |
| prior to | *Please call me **prior to** your visit.* (= before) |

Two other words also take *to*:

> *I **prefer** tea **to** coffee.* (verb *prefer*)
> *Walking is **preferable to** driving.* (adjective *preferable*)

Do not add *more* to these words: *~~more superior~~* ✘.

### Rule 10: To change a superlative into a comparative and a positive, use *than any other* and *no other ... as ... as*.

This is the **main interchange pattern**.

| Degree | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Superlative | *X is **the** + superlative + noun + in / of the group.* | *Mumbai is **the largest** city in India.* |
| Comparative | *X is + comparative + **than any other** + singular noun + in the group.* | *Mumbai is **larger than any other** city in India.* |
| Positive | ***No other** + singular noun + in the group + is **as / so** + positive + **as** X.* | ***No other** city in India is **as large as** Mumbai.* |

Points to notice:
- *than any **other*** + **singular** noun: *any other city* (not *~~any other cities~~*).
- *No other* + **singular** noun: *No other city* (not *~~No other cities~~*).
- In the positive, X moves to the **end**.

You can also write the comparative with *all other* + plural: *Mumbai is larger than **all other cities** in India.*

**Adverbs work the same way:**

> *Priya runs **the fastest** in the class.*
> *Priya runs **faster than any other** girl in the class.*
> ***No other** girl in the class runs **as fast as** Priya.*

### Rule 11: With *one of the* + superlative, use *than most other* and *very few ... as ... as*.

*One of the* means "among the top few", not "the top one". So we cannot use *any other* or *no other*.

| Degree | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Superlative | *X is **one of the** + superlative + plural noun.* | *Kolkata is **one of the largest** cities in India.* |
| Comparative | *X is + comparative + **than most other** + plural noun.* | *Kolkata is **larger than most other** cities in India.* |
| Positive | ***Very few** + plural noun + are **as / so** + positive + **as** X.* | ***Very few** cities in India are **as large as** Kolkata.* |

Points to notice:
- *one of the* + **plural** noun (*cities*).
- *most other* + **plural** noun.
- *very few* + **plural** noun + **plural** verb (*are*).

### Rule 12: To change a comparative between two things, swap the order and use the opposite adjective, or use *not so ... as*.

> *Comparative:* *Ravi is **taller than** Arun.*
> *Positive:* *Arun is **not so tall as** Ravi.* (or *not as tall as*)
> *Comparative with the opposite word:* *Arun is **shorter than** Ravi.*

> *Comparative:* *Gold is **more precious than** silver.*
> *Positive:* *Silver is **not so precious as** gold.*

When the positive uses *as ... as*, the comparative can use *no ... than* or *not ... than*:

> *Positive:* *Meena is **as clever as** Divya.*
> *Comparative:* *Divya is **not cleverer than** Meena.* (or *Meena is **no less clever than** Divya*)

## Special cases

### 1. Compare things of the same kind

Compare like with like. Use *that of* (singular) or *those of* (plural) to avoid comparing different things.

> *The climate of Ooty is cooler than **that of** Chennai.* (not *~~than Chennai~~*: climate vs city)
> *The streets of Madurai are wider than **those of** this town.*

### 2. *Any* without *other* when the thing is not in the group

Use *any other* only when X is **part of** the group. If X is **not** part of it, use *any* alone.

> *Mumbai is larger than **any other** city in India.* (Mumbai is an Indian city.)
> *Tokyo is larger than **any** city in India.* (Tokyo is not in India.)

### 3. Intensifying a comparative: *much, far, a lot*, not *very*

> *She is **much** taller than her brother.* (not *~~very taller~~*)
> *This phone is **far** better than the old one.*

Use *very* with the positive (*very tall*) and *by far* or *very* with the superlative (*by far the best*, *the very best*).

### 4. Double comparatives

> ***The more** you practise, **the better** you become.*
> *It is getting **hotter and hotter**.* / *The questions became **more and more** difficult.*

### 5. Adjectives that are not normally compared

Some adjectives describe something complete or absolute: *unique, perfect, complete, round, square, dead, ideal, empty, full, excellent, eternal*. Strictly, these have no degrees: *~~more unique~~*.

In everyday English you will see *more perfect* or *fuller*, but in exams avoid comparing these words.

### 6. *Than I* or *than me*

> *Ravi is taller **than I** (am).* (formal: *I* is the subject of the understood *am*)
> *Ravi is taller **than me**.* (common in speech, accepted in most contexts)

Use *than I* in formal writing. Be careful when the meaning changes: *She likes you more than **I** (do)* ≠ *She likes you more than (she likes) **me***.

### 7. *Less* and *least* for the opposite direction

> *This exam was **less difficult than** the last one.*
> *She is **the least interested** student in the class.*

*Less* with countable plural nouns should be *fewer*: *fewer mistakes*. See [Determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/).

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *This is ~~more better~~.* | *This is **better**.* | *Better* is already comparative. Don't add *more*. |
| *He is the ~~most cleverest~~ boy.* | *He is the **cleverest** boy.* / *the **most clever** boy.* | Use one form only, not both. |
| *My brother is ~~elder than~~ me.* | *My brother is **older than** me.* | *Elder* is not used with *than*. |
| *Mumbai is larger ~~than any city~~ in India.* | *Mumbai is larger **than any other city** in India.* | Mumbai is in India. Without *other*, it is compared with itself. |
| *No other ~~cities~~ is as large as Mumbai.* | ***No other city** is as large as Mumbai.* | *No other* + singular noun. |
| *She is ~~senior than~~ me.* | *She is **senior to** me.* | Latin comparatives take *to*. |
| *I prefer tea ~~than~~ coffee.* | *I prefer tea **to** coffee.* | *Prefer* takes *to*. |
| *He is ~~as taller as~~ his father.* | *He is **as tall as** his father.* | Use the positive between *as ... as*. |
| *She is the ~~tallest~~ of the two.* | *She is the **taller** of the two.* | Two people → comparative. |
| *The weather of Ooty is cooler than ~~Chennai~~.* | *... cooler than **that of** Chennai.* | Compare weather with weather. |
| *This is ~~very~~ better.* | *This is **much** better.* | *Very* does not go with comparatives. |
| *Kolkata is one of the ~~largest city~~.* | *Kolkata is one of the **largest cities**.* | *One of the* + plural noun. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Fill in** with the correct degree of the adjective in brackets.
- **Change the degree** of comparison without changing the meaning (*Rewrite in the comparative / positive degree*).
- **Spot the error.**

### Steps for "Change the degree"

1. **Find the degree** in the given sentence (*-est / most* = superlative; *-er / more ... than* = comparative; *as ... as* = positive).
2. **Is there *one of the*?** If yes, use Rule 11 (*most other*, *very few*). If no, use Rule 10 (*any other*, *no other*).
3. **Is it about two things only?** Use Rule 12 (swap, *not so ... as*).
4. **Change the adjective form** (*largest → larger → large*).
5. **Check the noun**: singular after *any other / no other*; plural after *one of the / most other / very few*.
6. **Check the verb**: singular after *no other*; plural after *very few*.
7. **Read both sentences**: they must mean the same.

### Worked example 1

> *Change into comparative and positive: Kavya is the best dancer in the school.*

1. Degree: superlative (*the best*). No *one of the*.
2. Comparative: *best* → *better*; add *than any other* + singular noun.
   *Kavya is **better than any other** dancer in the school.*
3. Positive: *best* → *good*; *No other* + singular noun + *as good as* Kavya.
   ***No other** dancer in the school is **as good as** Kavya.*

**Answer:**
- *Kavya is better than any other dancer in the school.*
- *No other dancer in the school is as good as Kavya.*

### Worked example 2

> *Change into comparative and positive: The Kaveri is one of the longest rivers in South India.*

1. Degree: superlative, with *one of the* → Rule 11.
2. Comparative: *longest* → *longer*; *than most other* + plural noun.
   *The Kaveri is **longer than most other** rivers in South India.*
3. Positive: *Very few* + plural noun + *are as long as* the Kaveri.
   ***Very few** rivers in South India are **as long as** the Kaveri.*

**Answer:**
- *The Kaveri is longer than most other rivers in South India.*
- *Very few rivers in South India are as long as the Kaveri.*

### Worked example 3

> *Change into superlative and comparative: No other metal is as useful as iron.*

1. Degree: positive (*No other ... as ... as*).
2. Superlative: *useful* → *the most useful*; *Iron is the most useful metal.*
3. Comparative: *more useful than any other metal*.

**Answer:**
- *Iron is the most useful metal.*
- *Iron is more useful than any other metal.*

### Worked example 4

> *Change into positive: Gold is costlier than silver.*

1. Degree: comparative between two things → Rule 12.
2. Swap: silver comes first. Use *not so ... as* + positive.

**Answer:** *Silver is **not so costly as** gold.* (or *not as costly as*)

### Worked example 5

> *Spot the error: Ravi is the most tallest boy in the class.*

1. *Tallest* is already superlative.
2. *Most* is extra.

**Answer:** *Ravi is the **tallest** boy in the class.*

## Quick check

1. *My grandfather is the ______ (old) person in our village.*
2. *Arun is ______ (good) at cricket than at football.*
3. *This shirt is superior ______ that one.*
4. Change into comparative: *No other student in the class is as hard-working as Meena.*
5. Change into positive: *Chennai is one of the busiest cities in India.*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***oldest.*** A comparison with everyone in the village → superlative. (*Eldest* is only for members of a family.)
2. ***better.*** *Than* shows a comparison of two things → comparative. *Good → better*.
3. ***to.*** *Superior* is a Latin comparative and takes *to*.
4. ***Meena is more hard-working than any other student in the class.*** Positive with *No other* → comparative with *than any other* + singular noun.
5. ***Very few cities in India are as busy as Chennai.*** *One of the* → *very few* + plural noun + *as ... as*.

</details>

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- Positive (*tall*) · comparative (*taller than*) · superlative (*the tallest*).
- Short words: *-er / -est*. Long words: *more / most*. Never both (*~~more better~~*).
- Irregular: *good–better–best, bad–worse–worst, little–less–least, many/much–more–most*. *Elder / eldest*: family only, no *than*.
- Latin comparatives (*senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior*) and *prefer* take ***to***.
- Interchange: *the largest* ↔ *larger **than any other*** + singular ↔ ***No other*** + singular *... as large as*.
- With *one of the*: *one of the largest* ↔ *larger than **most other*** + plural ↔ ***Very few*** + plural *... as large as*.
:::
