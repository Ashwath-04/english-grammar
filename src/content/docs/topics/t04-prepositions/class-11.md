## What you will learn

- What a preposition is, and what can follow it
- **Prepositions of time:** *at, on, in, for, since, during, by, until, before, after, within*
- **Prepositions of place:** *at, in, on, over, under, above, below, beside, besides, between, among, across, through*
- **Prepositions of movement:** *to, into, onto, towards, along, past, out of*
- The pairs that confuse students: *in / into, on / upon, between / among, beside / besides, since / for, by / with*
- How to answer "Fill in with prepositions" and "Replace the clause with a prepositional phrase"

## Why this matters

Prepositions are short words, but English uses them all the time. The wrong one changes the meaning, or sounds wrong to the listener: *~~on 6 o'clock~~*, *~~in Monday~~*, *~~since two years~~*.

The exam has fill-in-the-blank questions on prepositions every year. They also come in error spotting. The rules for time and place below cover most of these questions.

## The idea in simple words

Look at the cat and the box.

> *The cat is **in** the box.*
> *The cat is **on** the box.*
> *The cat is **under** the box.*
> *The cat is **beside** the box.*
> *The cat jumped **into** the box.*

The words in bold show **where** the cat is in relation to the box. They link the cat to the box. These words are called **prepositions**.

Prepositions also show **time**:

> *The class starts **at** nine, **on** Monday, **in** June.*

## Grammar words you need

**Preposition:** a word that shows how a noun or pronoun is related to another word, usually in place, time or direction. *in, on, at, under, for, since*...

**Object of a preposition:** the noun, pronoun or *-ing* word that comes after a preposition. In *under **the table***, the object is *the table*.

**Prepositional phrase:** a preposition + its object: *in the morning, on the wall, after lunch*.

**Point of time:** an exact moment or starting point: *5 o'clock, Monday, 2019*.

**Period of time:** a length of time: *three hours, two weeks, ten years*.

## The rules

### Rule 1: A preposition is followed by a noun, a pronoun in the object form, or a V4 (*-ing*) form.

> *She sat **near the window**.* (noun)
> *Come **with me**.* (object pronoun: *me*, not *~~I~~*)
> *This gift is **for him**.* (not *~~for he~~*)
> *He is good **at swimming**.* (V4)
> *Thank you **for helping** me.* (not *~~for help~~*)

Object pronouns: *me, you, him, her, it, us, them*.

> *Between **you and me**, the exam was difficult.* (not *~~between you and I~~*)

### Rule 2: Use *at* for exact times, *on* for days and dates, and *in* for longer periods.

| *at* (a point) | *on* (a day) | *in* (a longer period) |
|---|---|---|
| ***at** 6 o'clock, **at** 7.30* | ***on** Monday, **on** Sundays* | ***in** June, **in** summer* |
| ***at** noon, **at** midnight, **at** night* | ***on** 15 August, **on** my birthday* | ***in** 2025, **in** the 1990s* |
| ***at** sunrise, **at** sunset* | ***on** Pongal day, **on** Independence Day* | ***in** the morning, **in** the evening* |
| ***at** the moment, **at** present* | ***on** Monday morning* | ***in** the 21st century* |
| ***at** Deepavali* (the festival period) | ***on** a hot day* | ***in** two weeks* (= after two weeks) |

Note:
- ***in** the morning*, but ***on** Monday morning* (a particular day).
- ***in** the evening*, but ***at** night*.
- British English: ***at** the weekend*. American English: ***on** the weekend*.

**No preposition** before *last, next, this, every, today, tomorrow, yesterday*:

> *I met him **last** week.* (not *~~in last week~~*)
> *We will go **next** Monday.* (not *~~on next Monday~~*)
> *She calls her mother **every** day.*

### Rule 3: Use *for* with a period of time and *since* with a starting point.

| *for* + period | *since* + point |
|---|---|
| ***for** three hours* | ***since** 9 o'clock* |
| ***for** two days* | ***since** Monday* |
| ***for** ten years* | ***since** 2015* |
| ***for** a long time* | ***since** my childhood* |

> *I have been waiting **for** an hour.*
> *I have been waiting **since** six o'clock.*

*Since* is usually used with a **perfect** tense. *For* can be used with any tense:

> *She lived in Delhi **for** two years.* (past simple)
> *We will stay **for** a week.* (future)

More on this in [Tenses (Class 12)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-12/).

### Rule 4: Use *during* to say when something happened within a period. Use *within* for "before the end of".

> *The lights went off **during** the film.* (at some time while the film was on)
> *Nobody spoke **during** the exam.* (all through the exam)
> *Please return the book **within** a week.* (before one week is over)

*During* answers "**when**?". *For* answers "**how long**?".

> *I slept **during** the journey.* (when)
> *I slept **for** two hours.* (how long)
> *~~I slept during two hours.~~* ✘

### Rule 5: Use *by* for "not later than". Use *until* (or *till*) for "up to that time, and then stop".

> *Please submit your project **by** Friday.* (Friday or earlier; one action)
> *The shop is open **until** 9 p.m.* (it stays open up to 9, then it closes)
> *I will wait **till** you come.*

**Test:** *by* goes with an action done **once** (*finish, submit, reach*). *Until* goes with an action or state that **continues** (*wait, stay, be open, work*).

> *I will finish **by** 6.* ✔ / *~~I will finish until 6.~~* ✘
> *I will work **until** 6.* ✔ (I will keep working, then stop.)

### Rule 6: Use *before* for an earlier time and *after* for a later time.

> *Wash your hands **before** meals.*
> *We went for a walk **after** dinner.*
> *Come **before** 8, please.*

(*Before* and *after* can also be conjunctions, followed by a clause: *after we had dinner*.)

### Rule 7: For place, use *at* for a point, *in* for inside a space, and *on* for a surface.

| *at* (a point or a place for an activity) | *in* (inside, or a large area) | *on* (a surface or a line) |
|---|---|---|
| ***at** the bus stop, **at** the door* | ***in** the room, **in** the box* | ***on** the table, **on** the wall* |
| ***at** school, **at** home, **at** work* | ***in** Chennai, **in** India, **in** Asia* | ***on** the floor, **on** the roof* |
| ***at** the station, **at** the airport* | ***in** the garden, **in** the park* | ***on** the second floor* |
| ***at** 12, Anna Salai* (an exact address) | ***in** a car, **in** a taxi* | ***on** a bus, **on** a train, **on** a bike* |
| ***at** the top / bottom of the page* | ***in** the newspaper, **in** a book* | ***on** page 10, **on** the screen* |

Use ***in*** for cities and countries: *She lives **in** Madurai.* Use ***at*** for a smaller, exact place: *She is waiting **at** the Madurai bus stand.*

Vehicles: ***in*** a car or taxi (you sit inside a small space). ***On*** a bus, train, plane, bike, cycle (you can stand or walk on it, or sit on top).

### Rule 8: *Over* and *above* mean "higher than". *Under* and *below* mean "lower than".

| | Higher | Lower |
|---|---|---|
| Directly over / under, often touching or covering | ***over*** *She put a blanket **over** the baby.* | ***under*** *The cat is **under** the bed.* |
| Higher / lower level, not directly | ***above*** *The fan is **above** the table.* | ***below*** *The temperature fell **below** zero.* |

*Above* and *below* are used for **levels and measurements**: ***above** sea level, **below** 18 years of age, marks **above** 90*.

*Over* also means "more than": *There were **over** a hundred people.* And "across, to the other side": *He jumped **over** the wall.*

### Rule 9: *Beside* means "next to". *Besides* means "in addition to".

> *Kavya sat **beside** her mother.* (next to)
> ***Besides** English, she speaks French and Tamil.* (in addition to English)
> *Who was there **besides** you?* (other than you)

One *s* makes all the difference.

### Rule 10: Use *between* for two people or things. Use *among* for more than two, as a group.

> *Divide the sweets **between** the two boys.*
> *Divide the sweets **among** the children.*
> *The house is **between** the school and the temple.*
> *She was happy to be **among** friends.*

*Between* is also used for **more than two** when each one is clearly separate:

> *Trichy lies **between** Chennai, Madurai and Coimbatore.*
> *a treaty **between** three countries*

### Rule 11: Use *across* for "from one side to the other (on a surface)". Use *through* for "in at one end and out at the other (inside something)".

> *We walked **across** the road / **across** the bridge / **across** the field.*
> *The train went **through** the tunnel.*
> *We walked **through** the forest.*
> *Light passes **through** glass.*

*Across* = on a flat surface. *Through* = inside a space with sides (a tunnel, a forest, a crowd, a window).

### Rule 12: Use prepositions of movement to show direction.

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *to* | towards a place, and reaching it | *We went **to** Kanyakumari.* |
| *towards* | in the direction of (may not reach) | *He ran **towards** the gate.* |
| *into* | from outside to inside | *She walked **into** the room.* |
| *out of* | from inside to outside | *He jumped **out of** the bus.* |
| *onto* | moving to a surface | *The cat jumped **onto** the table.* |
| *along* | following a line (road, river) | *We walked **along** the beach.* |
| *past* | going by something | *The bus went **past** our school.* |
| *up / down* | to a higher / lower place | *She climbed **up** the hill.* |
| *from* | the starting point | *He came **from** Salem.* |

### Rule 13: *In* shows position. *Into* shows movement from outside to inside.

> *The children are **in** the classroom.* (position: they are there)
> *The children ran **into** the classroom.* (movement: they went inside)
> *He fell **into** the well.*
> *Put the milk **into** the fridge.*

After *put, throw, jump, fall*, use *into*. After *be, stay, live, sit*, use *in*.

(In everyday speech, *in* is often used with *put* and *throw*: *Put it in the box.* This is accepted. But *into* is better in writing when movement is clear.)

### Rule 14: *On* and *upon* mean the same. *Upon* is more formal. *Onto* shows movement.

> *The book is **on** the table.* (position)
> *The cat jumped **onto** the table.* (movement)
> *Once **upon** a time, there lived a king.* (fixed formal phrase)
> *We depend **upon** / **on** farmers for our food.* (both are correct; *on* is more common)

*Upon* is used in fixed phrases (*once upon a time, upon my word*) and in formal writing.

### Rule 15: Use *by* for the doer of an action or the means of transport. Use *with* for the tool or instrument.

> *The letter was written **by** Priya.* (the doer, in the passive)
> *She wrote the letter **with** a pen.* (the tool)
> *The tree was cut down **by** the workers **with** an axe.*
> *We went **by** bus / **by** train / **by** car.* (means of transport)

*By* + the person or force who did it. *With* + the thing used to do it.

## Special cases

### 1. Verbs that take no preposition

Some verbs are followed directly by their object. Adding a preposition is a common mistake.

> *We **reached** Chennai at noon.* (not *~~reached to Chennai~~*)
> *She **entered** the room.* (not *~~entered into the room~~*)
> *Let's **discuss** the plan.* (not *~~discuss about~~*)
> *He **married** Meena.* (not *~~married with Meena~~*)
> *Please **answer** the question.* (not *~~answer to~~*)
> *He **resembles** his father.* (not *~~resembles to~~*)

*Enter into* is used only for agreements and discussions: *enter into an agreement*.

### 2. *Arrive at* and *arrive in*, never *arrive to*

> *We **arrived at** the station.* (a building or small place)
> *We **arrived in** Mumbai.* (a city or country)

### 3. *Go home*, *come home*: no *to*

> *I went **home** early.* (not *~~went to home~~*)
> *She is **at home**.*

### 4. *In time* and *on time*

> *The train arrived **on time**.* (at the planned time, exactly)
> *We reached the hall **in time** for the speech.* (early enough)

### 5. *On* or *in* the street, *at* or *in* the corner

British English: *He lives **in** Gandhi Street.* American English: *on Gandhi Street.* Both are used in India.

> *There is a shop **at** the corner.* (the meeting point of two roads)
> *Put the chair **in** the corner.* (inside the room, where two walls meet)

### 6. Since and for: never *since* + a period

> *~~I have lived here since three years.~~* ✘ → *for three years* ✔

### 7. *Die of* and *die from*

> *He died **of** a heart attack.* (an illness)
> *She died **from** her injuries.* (a cause like a wound or an accident)

Both are often used the same way in modern English.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *I will meet you ~~on~~ 6 o'clock.* | *I will meet you **at** 6 o'clock.* | Exact times take *at*. |
| *She was born ~~in~~ 5 March.* | *She was born **on** 5 March.* | Dates take *on*. |
| *We will go ~~on~~ next Sunday.* | *We will go **next** Sunday.* | No preposition before *next, last, this, every*. |
| *He has been ill ~~since~~ a week.* | *He has been ill **for** a week.* | *A week* is a period → *for*. |
| *We reached ~~to~~ Madurai at noon.* | *We reached Madurai at noon.* | *Reach* takes no preposition. |
| *Let's discuss ~~about~~ the plan.* | *Let's discuss the plan.* | *Discuss* takes no preposition. |
| *Divide the cake ~~between~~ the five children.* | *Divide the cake **among** the five children.* | More than two, as a group → *among*. |
| *~~Beside~~ maths, he likes science.* | ***Besides** maths, he likes science.* | "In addition to" → *besides*. |
| *She jumped ~~in~~ the pool.* | *She jumped **into** the pool.* | Movement from outside to inside → *into*. |
| *He killed the snake ~~by~~ a stick.* | *He killed the snake **with** a stick.* | The tool → *with*. |
| *Please submit the form ~~until~~ Monday.* | *Please submit the form **by** Monday.* | A single action with a deadline → *by*. |
| *This is between you and ~~I~~.* | *This is between you and **me**.* | After a preposition, use the object pronoun. |
| *I slept ~~during~~ three hours.* | *I slept **for** three hours.* | How long → *for*. *During* + a noun naming the event (*during the film*). |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Fill in the blanks** with suitable prepositions.
- **Choose the right preposition** from two or three options.
- **Replace the clause with a prepositional phrase** (shorter, same meaning).
- **Spot the error.**

### Steps for "Fill in with a preposition"

1. **What does the blank show?** Time, place, movement, the doer, the tool, or something else?
2. **Look at the noun after the blank.**
   - Time: exact time → *at*; day / date → *on*; month / year / part of day → *in*; period → *for*; starting point → *since*; deadline → *by*; continuing up to → *until*.
   - Place: point → *at*; inside / city → *in*; surface → *on*.
   - Movement: going inside → *into*; direction → *towards*; along a line → *along*.
3. **Look at the verb before the blank.** Does it take no preposition (*reach, enter, discuss*) or a fixed one (*arrive at / in*)?
4. **Check the pairs:** *between / among, beside / besides, in / into, by / with, since / for*.
5. **Read the sentence again** aloud in your head.

### Steps for "Replace the clause with a prepositional phrase"

1. Find the clause (it has a subject and a finite verb).
2. Find its meaning: time, reason, description, manner...
3. Change the verb into a noun if needed (*arrived → arrival*, *rose → sunrise*).
4. Put a preposition before it.

| Clause | Prepositional phrase |
|---|---|
| *when the sun rose* | *at sunrise* |
| *after he arrived* | *after his arrival* |
| *the girl who has long hair* | *the girl with long hair* |
| *while we were travelling* | *during the journey* / *on the way* |
| *because he was ill* | *because of his illness* |

### Worked example 1

> *The train leaves ______ 6.15 ______ Monday morning.*

1. First blank: *6.15* is an exact time → ***at***.
2. Second blank: *Monday morning* is a particular day → ***on***.

**Answer:** *The train leaves **at** 6.15 **on** Monday morning.*

### Worked example 2

> *My grandparents have lived in this house ______ 1985.*

1. The blank shows time.
2. *1985* is a starting point, and the tense is present perfect → ***since***.

**Answer:** *My grandparents have lived in this house **since** 1985.*

### Worked example 3

> *The boy dived ______ the river and swam ______ it to the other side.*

1. First blank: movement from outside to inside the water → ***into***.
2. Second blank: from one side to the other side → ***across***.

**Answer:** *The boy dived **into** the river and swam **across** it to the other side.*

### Worked example 4

> *Replace the clause with a prepositional phrase: We left the hall when the meeting ended.*

1. Clause: *when the meeting ended* (subject *the meeting*, verb *ended*).
2. Meaning: time.
3. Change the verb into a noun: *ended* → *the end*.
4. Add a preposition: *at the end of the meeting*. (Or: *after the meeting*.)

**Answer:** *We left the hall **at the end of the meeting**.* (or ***after the meeting***)

### Worked example 5

> *Spot the error: The money was divided between the four brothers.*

1. *Between* is mainly for two.
2. Four brothers, as a group → *among*.

**Answer:** *The money was divided **among** the four brothers.*

## Quick check

1. *We usually go to the temple ______ the evening.*
2. *The cat is hiding ______ the bed.*
3. *______ cricket, Arun plays football and kabaddi.* (Beside / Besides)
4. *You must finish the work ______ 5 p.m.* (by / until)
5. *She cut the vegetables ______ a knife.* (by / with)

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***in.*** Parts of the day (*the morning, the evening*) take *in*.
2. ***under.*** The cat is directly lower than the bed, covered by it.
3. ***Besides.*** It means "in addition to cricket".
4. ***by.*** A deadline for a single action (*finish*) → *by*.
5. ***with.*** A tool → *with*.

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- Time: ***at*** 6 o'clock · ***on*** Monday · ***in*** June / 2025 / the morning. No preposition before *next, last, this, every*.
- ***for*** + period · ***since*** + point · ***during*** + an event (when) · ***by*** = not later than · ***until*** = up to, then stop.
- Place: ***at*** a point · ***in*** a space or city · ***on*** a surface.
- ***into*** = movement inside · ***across*** = from side to side · ***through*** = inside, from end to end.
- ***between*** two · ***among*** many · ***beside*** = next to · ***besides*** = in addition to · ***by*** the doer · ***with*** the tool.
- No preposition after *reach, enter, discuss, marry, answer*.
:::
