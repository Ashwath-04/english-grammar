## Part A: Fill in the dependent preposition

**1.** *Arun is very good ______ chess.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***at.*** *Good* is followed by *at*. (*good in* is a common mistake.)

</details>

**2.** *My grandfather is proud ______ his garden.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***of.*** *Proud* is followed by *of*.

</details>

**3.** *Who is responsible ______ this mess?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***for.*** *Responsible* is followed by *for*.

</details>

**4.** *Kavya is interested ______ classical dance.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***in.*** *Interested* is followed by *in*.

</details>

**5.** *Everything depends ______ the result.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***on*** (or ***upon***). *Depend* is followed by *on*.

</details>

**6.** *This book consists ______ ten chapters.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***of.*** *Consist* is followed by *of*.

</details>

**7.** *The teacher insisted ______ complete silence.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***on.*** *Insist* is followed by *on*.

</details>

**8.** *I prefer idli ______ dosa.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to.*** *Prefer* A *to* B. (*than* is wrong here.)

</details>

**9.** *What is the reason ______ your absence?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***for.*** The noun *reason* is followed by *for*.

</details>

**10.** *Nobody could find a solution ______ this puzzle.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to.*** The noun *solution* is followed by *to*. (*solution for* is a common mistake.)

</details>

**11.** *There has been a sharp increase ______ the price of onions.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***in.*** The noun *increase* is followed by *in* (the thing that goes up). (*an increase **of** 10 rupees* is used for the amount.)

</details>

**12.** *My opinion is different ______ yours.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***from.*** *Different from* is the standard form. (*different to* is also British; *different than* is mainly American.)

</details>

**13.** *He is senior ______ me by two years.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to.*** Latin comparatives like *senior, junior, superior, inferior* take *to*, not *than*.

</details>

**14.** *She has been suffering ______ fever since Monday.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***from.*** *Suffer* is followed by *from*.

</details>

**15.** *I agree ______ you completely.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***with.*** *Agree with* + a person or an opinion.

</details>

**16.** *The principal agreed ______ our proposal for a science fair.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to.*** **Trap:** compare with question 15. *Agree to* + a plan, a request or a proposal.

</details>

**17.** *Amma was angry ______ me for coming home late.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***with.*** *Angry with* + a person. (*angry about* + a thing or situation.)

</details>

## Part B: Choose the correct prepositional phrase

**18.** *______ the weather report, it will rain tomorrow.* (According to / On behalf of)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***According to.*** It means "as the weather report says".

</details>

**19.** *I am writing ______ your advertisement in The Hindu.* (with reference to / in case of)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***with reference to.*** It means "about" and is used in formal letters.

</details>

**20.** *The secretary received the award ______ the club.* (on behalf of / instead of)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***on behalf of.*** She received it as the club's representative. (*Instead of* would mean the club did not get it and she got it in its place.)

</details>

**21.** *______ fire, use the stairs, not the lift.* (In case of / In spite of)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***In case of.*** It means "if there is a fire".

</details>

**22.** *The delay in the flight was ______ bad weather.* (due to / owing to)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***due to.*** **Trap:** after *be* (*was*), the traditional choice is *due to* (= caused by). *Owing to* is used to begin a phrase: *Owing to bad weather, the flight was delayed.*

</details>

## Part C: Replace the clause with a prepositional phrase

**23.** *Although the road was bad, we reached on time.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***In spite of the bad road**, we reached on time.* (or ***Despite the bad road***.) *Although* (contrast) → *in spite of* + noun.

</details>

**24.** *Because he was ill, Ravi could not attend the function.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***On account of his illness**, Ravi could not attend the function.* (or *Because of his illness* / *Owing to his illness*.) *He was ill* becomes the noun phrase *his illness*.

</details>

**25.** *She went to the cinema. She did not go to school.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*She went to the cinema **instead of going** to school.* *Instead of* + V4 for the thing that was not done.

</details>

**26.** *Meena sings, and she also dances.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***In addition to singing**, Meena dances.* (or *Besides singing, Meena dances*.) *In addition to* + V4.

</details>

**27.** *As the rain was heavy, the match was stopped.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Owing to the heavy rain**, the match was stopped.* (or *Because of* / *On account of the heavy rain*.) *As* here gives the reason.

</details>

## Part D: Correct or rewrite

**28.** *Correct the error: Despite of the heat, we played cricket.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Despite the heat**, we played cricket.* (or ***In spite of** the heat*.) *Despite* is never followed by *of*.

</details>

**29.** *Correct the error: My sister is married with an engineer.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*My sister is married **to** an engineer.* *Married* is followed by *to*.

</details>

**30.** *Correct the error: I look forward to see you at the function.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*I look forward to **seeing** you at the function.* In *look forward to*, the word *to* is a preposition, so it is followed by V4.

</details>

**31.** *Rewrite in formal English: Who did you lend the book to?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***To whom** did you lend the book?* In formal English, the preposition moves to the front, and *who* becomes *whom* after a preposition.

</details>

**32.** *Correct the error: This is the room which I study in it.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*This is the room **(which / that) I study in**.* or *This is the room **in which** I study.* *Which* already stands for *the room*, so the extra *it* must go.

</details>
