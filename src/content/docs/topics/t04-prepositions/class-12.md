## What you will learn

- **Prepositional phrases** (groups of words that work as one preposition): *in spite of, on account of, in front of, by means of, in addition to, on behalf of, with reference to, in case of, instead of, according to, owing to, due to*
- **Dependent prepositions**: the fixed prepositions that follow certain words
  - adjective + preposition: *good at, afraid of, proud of, responsible for*
  - verb + preposition: *depend on, consist of, insist on, prefer ... to*
  - noun + preposition: *reason for, interest in, solution to*
- **Prepositions at the end of a clause**: *What are you looking for?*
- How to answer "Fill in", "Choose" and "Replace the clause with a prepositional phrase"

## Why this matters

Many errors in letters and essays come from dependent prepositions: *~~good in maths~~*, *~~depend upon on~~*, *~~prefer tea than coffee~~*. These are not decided by a rule of meaning. They have to be learnt with the word.

Prepositional phrases help you write shorter, more formal sentences. *Although it rained, the match continued* becomes ***In spite of** the rain, the match continued*. This kind of change is a common exam question, and it is also used in [transformation of sentences](/topics/t09-kinds-of-sentences/kinds-of-sentences/).

## The idea in simple words

Look at these two sentences.

> *The match was cancelled **because** it rained.*
> *The match was cancelled **because of** the rain.*

They mean the same. The first uses a **clause** (*it rained*: subject + verb). The second uses a **noun** (*the rain*). *Because of* is a group of two words that works like one preposition. We call it a **prepositional phrase** (or a compound preposition).

Now look at this:

> *Priya is good **at** maths.*

Why *at*? There is no reason of place or time. *Good* always goes with *at*. It is a fixed pair. The *at* "depends on" the word *good*. We call it a **dependent preposition**.

## Grammar words you need

**Prepositional phrase (compound preposition):** a group of two or more words that works as a single preposition: *in spite of, because of, instead of*. It is followed by a noun, pronoun or V4.

(The term *prepositional phrase* also means a preposition + its object, like *in the morning*. This lesson uses it mostly for the groups like *in spite of*.)

**Dependent preposition:** a preposition that always goes with a particular adjective, verb or noun: *afraid **of**, depend **on**, reason **for***.

**Clause:** a group of words with a subject and a finite verb. See [Phrase and clause](/foundation/f5-phrase-clause/).

**Object:** the noun or pronoun after a preposition: *in spite of **the rain***.

## The rules

### Rule 1: A prepositional phrase works as one preposition, and it is followed by a noun, a pronoun or a V4.

> ***In spite of** the rain*, ***instead of** tea*, ***because of** him*, ***in addition to** studying*

It is **never** followed directly by a clause (subject + verb):

> *~~In spite of it rained~~* ✘ → ***In spite of** the rain* ✔ / ***Although** it rained* ✔

### Rule 2: Learn the main prepositional phrases with their meanings.

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *in spite of* / *despite* | although there is / was | ***In spite of** his illness, he attended the exam.* |
| *on account of* | because of (formal) | *The school was closed **on account of** heavy rain.* |
| *because of* | for the reason of | *We were late **because of** the traffic.* |
| *owing to* | because of | ***Owing to** a strike, there were no buses.* |
| *due to* | caused by | *The delay was **due to** fog.* |
| *in front of* | facing, ahead of | *There is a tree **in front of** our house.* |
| *by means of* | using, with the help of | *He crossed the river **by means of** a boat.* |
| *in addition to* | as well as, besides | ***In addition to** English, she teaches French.* |
| *on behalf of* | as the representative of | *I thank you **on behalf of** the school.* |
| *with reference to* | about, concerning (formal letters) | ***With reference to** your letter of 5 May, ...* |
| *in case of* | if there is (an emergency) | ***In case of** fire, break the glass.* |
| *instead of* | in place of | *Let's have idli **instead of** dosa.* |
| *according to* | as stated by | ***According to** the weather report, it will rain.* |

More phrases you will meet:

| Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| *in place of* | *Ravi played **in place of** the injured captain.* |
| *in charge of* | *Mrs Meena is **in charge of** the library.* |
| *for the sake of* | *He gave up his job **for the sake of** his family.* |
| *in favour of* | *Most students voted **in favour of** a trip to Ooty.* |
| *with regard to* | ***With regard to** your request, we are happy to help.* |
| *apart from* | ***Apart from** Arun, everyone came.* (= except) |
| *in view of* | ***In view of** the cyclone warning, schools are closed.* |
| *by way of* | *We went to Ooty **by way of** Coimbatore.* (= via) |
| *at the cost of* | *He finished the work **at the cost of** his health.* |
| *for fear of* | *She did not speak **for fear of** making a mistake.* |
| *with a view to* | *He joined the class **with a view to** improving his English.* (+ V4) |

### Rule 3: *Despite* has no *of*. *In spite of* always has *of*.

> ***Despite** the rain* ✔ / ***In spite of** the rain* ✔
> *~~Despite of the rain~~* ✘ / *~~In spite the rain~~* ✘

### Rule 4: *Due to* traditionally follows *be*. *Owing to* and *because of* begin a phrase that tells why.

The traditional rule (still expected in many exams):

> *The delay **was due to** fog.* ✔ (after *be*: *due to* = caused by)
> ***Owing to** fog, the flight was delayed.* ✔ (tells why the flight was delayed)

In modern English, *due to* is also used like *owing to*: *Due to fog, the flight was delayed.* This is widely accepted today, but *owing to* or *because of* is the safer choice at the start of a sentence.

### Rule 5: Some adjectives are always followed by a particular preposition.

| Preposition | Adjectives |
|---|---|
| ***at*** | good at, bad at, clever at, quick at, surprised at, angry at (a situation) |
| ***of*** | afraid of, proud of, fond of, full of, aware of, capable of, jealous of, tired of, ashamed of, sure of, short of, guilty of |
| ***for*** | responsible for, famous for, late for, ready for, sorry for, grateful for (a thing), eligible for |
| ***in*** | interested in, rich in, weak in, successful in, involved in |
| ***on*** | keen on, dependent on, based on |
| ***to*** | similar to, kind to, polite to, married to, used to, close to, grateful to (a person), junior / senior / superior / inferior to |
| ***with*** | angry with (a person), pleased with, satisfied with, familiar with, busy with, bored with, crowded with |
| ***from*** | different from, absent from, free from, far from, safe from |
| ***about*** | worried about, happy about, sorry about, excited about, careful about |

> *Kavya is good **at** drawing.* (not *~~good in drawing~~*)
> *I am proud **of** you.*
> *He was absent **from** school yesterday.*
> *My father was angry **with** me.* (a person) / *angry **about** the delay* (a thing)

*Different from* is standard in British English. *Different to* is also British. *Different than* is mainly American. Use *different from* in exams.

The Latin comparatives *senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior* take ***to***, not *than*. See [Degrees of comparison](/topics/t08-degrees-of-comparison/class-12/).

### Rule 6: Some verbs are always followed by a particular preposition.

| Preposition | Verbs |
|---|---|
| ***on*** | depend on, rely on, insist on, concentrate on, congratulate (someone) on, comment on, decide on |
| ***of*** | consist of, accuse (someone) of, approve of, think of, dream of, die of (an illness), remind (someone) of |
| ***to*** | belong to, listen to, object to, reply to, refer to, apologise to (a person), agree to (a plan), lead to |
| ***for*** | wait for, apply for, look for, apologise for (a thing), pay for, ask for, blame (someone) for, search for |
| ***with*** | agree with (a person), deal with, compare with, cope with, provide (someone) with, quarrel with |
| ***in*** | believe in, succeed in, participate in, specialise in, result in, take part in |
| ***from*** | suffer from, prevent (someone) from, differ from, protect (someone) from, recover from, borrow from |
| ***at*** | laugh at, look at, aim at, stare at, arrive at |
| ***into*** | divide into, translate into, change into, turn into |
| ***about*** | complain about, worry about, know about, talk about, care about |

> *Success **depends on** hard work.*
> *A week **consists of** seven days.*
> *She **insisted on** paying the bill.* (+ V4)
> *I **agree with** you.* / *The principal **agreed to** our plan.*

### Rule 7: *Prefer* takes *to*, not *than*.

> *I **prefer** tea **to** coffee.* (not *~~prefer tea than coffee~~*)
> *She **prefers** walking **to** cycling.* (V4 + *to* + V4)

With two infinitives, use *rather than*:

> *I **prefer to walk rather than** take the bus.*

*Would rather* also takes *than*: *I **would rather** walk **than** take the bus.*

### Rule 8: Some nouns are always followed by a particular preposition.

| Preposition | Nouns |
|---|---|
| ***for*** | reason for, demand for, need for, respect for, love for, cure for, request for, preference for |
| ***in*** | interest in, increase in, decrease in, rise in, fall in, belief in, change in, confidence in |
| ***to*** | solution to, answer to, key to, reply to, invitation to, damage to, approach to, attention to, exception to |
| ***of*** | cause of, example of, fear of, lack of, knowledge of, advantage of, habit of, possibility of |
| ***on*** | effect on, influence on, attack on, tax on, ban on |
| ***with*** | relationship with, problem with, contact with, connection with |
| ***between*** | difference between, relationship between |

> *What is the **reason for** your absence?*
> *There has been an **increase in** prices.*
> *Nobody could find a **solution to** the problem.* (not *~~solution for~~*)
> *Smoking has a bad **effect on** health.*

A verb and its noun can take **different** prepositions:

> *She is **interested in** art.* / *She has an **interest in** art.* (same)
> *He **replied to** my letter.* / *His **reply to** my letter...* (same)
> *We **discussed** the plan.* / *We had a **discussion about** the plan.* (different)

### Rule 9: In some kinds of sentences, the preposition can come at the end.

This is normal and correct in modern English. It happens in four main places.

**(a) Wh- questions:**

> *What are you looking **for**?*
> *Who did you speak **to**?*
> *Where do you come **from**?*
> *What is this tool used **for**?*

**(b) Relative clauses:**

> *This is the house I live **in**.*
> *She is the friend I told you **about**.*

**(c) Passive sentences:**

> *The child was laughed **at**.*
> *This problem must be dealt **with**.*

**(d) After an infinitive:**

> *I need a chair to sit **on**.*
> *She has no one to talk **to**.*

In **formal** English, the preposition can move to the front, before *whom* or *which*:

> ***To whom** did you speak?* (formal) = *Who did you speak to?* (normal)
> *the house **in which** I live* (formal) = *the house I live in* (normal)

After a preposition, use *whom* and *which*, never *who* or *that*: *~~to who~~*, *~~in that~~* ✘.

The old school rule "never end a sentence with a preposition" is not followed in modern English. Both orders are correct, but the end position sounds more natural in speech.

## Special cases

### 1. Phrasal verbs: the preposition changes the meaning

Some verbs + prepositions (or adverbs) make a new meaning:

> *look **after*** (take care of) · *look **for*** (search) · *look **into*** (investigate) · *look **up to*** (respect)
> *give **up*** (stop) · *put **off*** (postpone) · *carry **on*** (continue) · *turn **down*** (refuse)

*She **looks after** her grandmother.* / *Please **look for** my keys.*

### 2. *Agree with*, *agree to*, *agree on*

> *I **agree with** Ravi.* (a person, or an opinion)
> *The manager **agreed to** our request.* (a plan or a proposal)
> *We **agreed on** the date.* (a decision we all made)

### 3. *Angry with* and *angry about*

> *Amma was **angry with** me.* (a person)
> *She was **angry about** the broken window.* (a thing or situation)

### 4. *Accuse of* but *charge with*

> *They **accused** him **of** theft.*
> *The police **charged** him **with** theft.*

### 5. *Worth* + V4

> *This book is **worth reading**.* (not *~~worth to read~~*)

### 6. *Look forward to* + V4

> *I **look forward to meeting** you.* (not *~~to meet you~~*)

Here *to* is a preposition, so it is followed by V4. The same is true for *be used to, object to, with a view to*.

### 7. *In case of* and *in case*

> ***In case of** fire, call 101.* (+ noun: if there is a fire)
> *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* (+ clause: because it may rain)

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *She is good ~~in~~ maths.* | *She is good **at** maths.* | *Good* takes *at*. |
| *I prefer tea ~~than~~ coffee.* | *I prefer tea **to** coffee.* | *Prefer* takes *to*. |
| *~~Despite of~~ the rain, we went out.* | ***Despite** the rain* / ***In spite of** the rain* | *Despite* has no *of*. |
| *~~In spite of~~ he was ill, he came.* | ***Although** he was ill* / ***In spite of** his illness* | A prepositional phrase takes a noun, not a clause. |
| *We have found a solution ~~for~~ the problem.* | *... a solution **to** the problem.* | *Solution* takes *to*. |
| *He is junior ~~than~~ me.* | *He is junior **to** me.* | Latin comparatives take *to*. |
| *The house consists ~~in~~ four rooms.* | *The house consists **of** four rooms.* | *Consist* takes *of*. |
| *She is married ~~with~~ a doctor.* | *She is married **to** a doctor.* | *Married* takes *to*. |
| *He was absent ~~for~~ the class.* | *He was absent **from** the class.* | *Absent* takes *from*. |
| *Who did you give it ~~to him~~?* | *Who did you give it **to**?* | The question word is the object of *to*. Don't add a pronoun. |
| *I look forward to ~~meet~~ you.* | *I look forward to **meeting** you.* | *To* is a preposition here, so V4 follows. |
| *He died ~~by~~ cancer.* | *He died **of** cancer.* | An illness takes *die of*. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Fill in the blanks** with suitable prepositions (often dependent prepositions).
- **Choose the right preposition** from options.
- **Replace the clause (or underlined words) with a prepositional phrase.**
- **Spot the error.**

### Steps for "Fill in with a dependent preposition"

1. **Find the word right before the blank**: is it an adjective, a verb or a noun?
2. **Recall the fixed partner** (use the tables above). Say the pair aloud: *proud of, depend on, reason for*.
3. **Check if the meaning changes** the preposition: *agree with* (a person) vs *agree to* (a plan); *angry with* vs *angry about*.
4. **Check what comes after**: a noun, a pronoun (object form) or a V4.

### Steps for "Replace the clause with a prepositional phrase"

1. **Find the joining word** of the clause (*although, because, when, if, instead*) and its meaning.
2. **Choose the prepositional phrase** with the same meaning:

| Clause word | Prepositional phrase |
|---|---|
| *although / though* | *in spite of / despite* |
| *because / as / since* (reason) | *because of / on account of / owing to* |
| *if there is* (emergency) | *in case of* |
| *instead of doing* / *rather than* | *instead of* + V4 |
| *as ... says* | *according to* |
| *and also* | *in addition to* |

3. **Change the clause into a noun or V4**: *he was ill* → *his illness*; *it rained* → *the rain*; *she worked hard* → *her hard work*.
4. **Check the pronoun**: *he* → *his*, *they* → *their*.
5. **Write the new sentence** and read it again.

### Worked example 1

> *Replace the clause: Although he was poor, he was honest.*

1. Joining word: *although* → contrast.
2. Phrase: *in spite of* (or *despite*).
3. *He was poor* → noun: *his poverty*.

**Answer:** ***In spite of his poverty**, he was honest.* (or ***Despite his poverty***)

### Worked example 2

> *Replace the clause: The train was late because there was fog.*

1. Joining word: *because* → reason.
2. Phrase: *because of* / *on account of* / *owing to*.
3. *There was fog* → noun: *the fog*.

**Answer:** *The train was late **because of the fog**.* (or *on account of the fog* / *owing to the fog*; *The delay of the train was **due to** the fog* also works.)

### Worked example 3

> *Fill in: Meena is very fond ______ music, and she is interested ______ learning the flute.*

1. First blank: after the adjective *fond* → ***of***.
2. Second blank: after the adjective *interested* → ***in***. It is followed by V4 (*learning*), which is correct after a preposition.

**Answer:** *Meena is very fond **of** music, and she is interested **in** learning the flute.*

### Worked example 4

> *Rewrite using "instead of": He watched TV. He did not study.*

1. *Instead of* + V4 for the thing **not** done.
2. *did not study* → *studying*.

**Answer:** *He watched TV **instead of studying**.*

### Worked example 5

> *Spot the error: She prefers coffee than tea.*

1. *Prefer* takes *to*, not *than*.

**Answer:** *She prefers coffee **to** tea.*

## Quick check

1. *The success of the event depends ______ the weather.*
2. *Karthik was congratulated ______ his excellent results.*
3. *Replace the clause: As it was a holiday, the office was closed.*
4. *What are you afraid ______?*
5. Correct the error: *Despite of his hard work, he failed.*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***on.*** *Depend* takes *on*.
2. ***on.*** *Congratulate* (someone) takes *on*.
3. ***On account of the holiday, the office was closed.*** (or *Because of the holiday* / *Owing to the holiday*.) *As* here shows reason.
4. ***of.*** *Afraid* takes *of*. In a wh- question, the preposition goes to the end.
5. ***Despite his hard work, he failed.*** (or *In spite of his hard work*.) *Despite* has no *of*.

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- Prepositional phrases (*in spite of, because of, instead of, according to*) + **noun / pronoun / V4**, never a clause.
- *Despite* (no *of*) = *in spite of*. *Due to* after *be*; *owing to / because of* to begin a phrase.
- Learn dependent prepositions with the word: *good **at**, proud **of**, responsible **for**, interested **in**, depend **on**, consist **of**, insist **on**, prefer ... **to**, reason **for**, solution **to***.
- A preposition can end a question, a relative clause, a passive or an infinitive: *What are you looking for?*
- *to* + V4 after *look forward to, be used to, object to, with a view to*.
:::
