## What you will learn

- The difference between **time** (present, past, future) and **aspect** (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous)
- All **12 tenses**: the formula, the positive, negative and question forms, when to use each one, and its **signal words**
- Other ways to talk about the future: *be going to*, the present continuous, the present simple
- **Stative verbs** (*know, believe, belong, own*), which are not normally used in the continuous form
- How to answer "Fill in with the correct tense form", "Identify the tense", "Correct the error" and cloze passages

## Why this matters

Every sentence you speak or write has a tense. The tense tells the listener **when** something happens and **how** we see it: as a habit, as something going on, or as something finished.

In the exam, tenses come up as their own exercise (fill in the blanks, identify the tense, correct the error). They are also the base for [Active and passive voice](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-11/), [Reported speech](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-11/) and [Conditional clauses](/topics/t13-conditionals/class-11/). A student who is sure of tenses finds those topics much easier.

## The idea in simple words

Look at these sentences about Priya and her music class.

> *Priya **practises** the veena every day.* (a habit)
> *Priya **is practising** the veena now.* (going on at this moment)
> *Priya **has practised** for the concert.* (finished, and she is ready now)
> *Priya **has been practising** since six o'clock.* (started at six, still going on)

All four are about the **present**. But each one shows the action in a different way. This "way of seeing" the action is called **aspect**.

English has three **times** and four **aspects**:

| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | writes | is writing | has written | has been writing |
| **Past** | wrote | was writing | had written | had been writing |
| **Future** | will write | will be writing | will have written | will have been writing |

Three times × four aspects = **12 tenses**.

## Grammar words you need

**Tense:** the form of the verb that shows time and aspect. *She **wrote**.* (past simple)

**Time:** when the action happens: present, past or future.

**Aspect:** how we see the action.
- **Simple:** as a fact, a habit, or a complete event.
- **Continuous (progressive):** as going on, in progress, not finished. Always uses *be* + V4.
- **Perfect:** as finished before a certain point. Always uses *have* + V3.
- **Perfect continuous:** as going on for a period of time up to a certain point. Always uses *have been* + V4.

**V1 to V5:** the five verb forms (*write, wrote, written, writing, writes*). See [Verb forms](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/).

**Auxiliary (helping) verb:** *be, have, do, will*, which help the main verb make tenses. See [Auxiliary verbs](/foundation/f3-auxiliaries/).

**Signal words:** words in a sentence that point to a tense, such as *yesterday* (past simple), *now* (present continuous) or *since* (present perfect).

## The rules

The examples use the subject *she* and the verb *write*. The full chart is on the [Tense table](/reference/tense-table/) page.

### Rule 1: Every tense is time + aspect. Find both before you choose a verb form.

Ask two questions:

1. **When?** Present, past or future?
2. **How?** A fact or habit? Going on? Finished? Going on for a period?

> *Look! It **is raining**.* → present + going on → **present continuous**
> *We **had left** before the rain started.* → past + finished earlier → **past perfect**

### Rule 2: Use the present simple for habits, facts and things that are always true.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they **write** | I / you / we / they **do not write** | **Do** I / you / we / they **write**? |
| he / she / it **writes** | he / she / it **does not write** | **Does** he / she / it **write**? |

**Formula:** V1 (with *I, you, we, they*) / V5 (with *he, she, it*)

**Uses:**

> *Ravi **goes** to the gym every morning.* (habit)
> *Water **boils** at 100 °C.* (scientific fact)
> *The sun **rises** in the east.* (universal truth)
> *My uncle **lives** in Trichy.* (permanent situation)
> *The Chennai Express **leaves** at 6.30.* (timetable, even for the future)

**Signal words:** *always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never, every day, every week, on Sundays, once a month*

### Rule 3: Use the present continuous for actions going on now, and for temporary situations.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| I **am writing** | I **am not writing** | **Am** I **writing**? |
| he / she / it **is writing** | he / she / it **is not writing** | **Is** she **writing**? |
| we / you / they **are writing** | we / you / they **are not writing** | **Are** they **writing**? |

**Formula:** *am / is / are* + V4

**Uses:**

> *Kavya **is reading** in her room now.* (going on at this moment)
> *My brother **is staying** with us this week.* (temporary)
> *Prices **are rising** every month.* (a situation that is changing)
> *We **are meeting** the principal tomorrow.* (a fixed plan for the future: see Rule 14)

**Signal words:** *now, right now, at the moment, at present, currently, today, this week, these days, Look!, Listen!*

### Rule 4: Use the present perfect for a past action connected to now.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they **have written** | **have not written** | **Have** they **written**? |
| he / she / it **has written** | **has not written** | **Has** she **written**? |

**Formula:** *has / have* + V3

The action happened in the past, but we are thinking about **now**: its result, our experience up to now, or a period that is still going on.

> *I **have lost** my pen.* (result now: I don't have it)
> *Arun **has visited** Ooty three times.* (experience in his life up to now)
> *She **has just finished** her exam.* (very recent)
> *We **have lived** in Salem since 2015.* (started in the past, still true)
> ***Have** you ever **eaten** jackfruit halwa?* (experience: *ever*)

The present perfect does **not** go with a finished past time like *yesterday* or *last year*. *~~I have seen him yesterday~~* is wrong. See Rule 6.

**Signal words:** *just, already, yet, ever, never, so far, recently, lately, up to now, since, for, this week / this year* (when it is not over)

### Rule 5: Use the present perfect continuous for an action that started in the past and is still going on, to show how long.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they **have been writing** | **have not been writing** | **Have** they **been writing**? |
| he / she / it **has been writing** | **has not been writing** | **Has** she **been writing**? |

**Formula:** *has / have been* + V4

> *Meena **has been waiting** for the bus since 8 o'clock.* (she is still waiting)
> *They **have been building** the bridge for two years.*
> *It **has been raining** all morning.*

It can also show a recent activity that explains something you can see now:

> *Your eyes are red. **Have** you **been crying**?*

**Signal words:** *for, since, all day, all morning, how long, lately*

**Since and for:** *since* + a **point** of time (*since Monday, since 2019, since morning*). *for* + a **period** of time (*for three days, for two years, for an hour*).

### Rule 6: Use the past simple for an action finished at a definite time in the past.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| she **wrote** (all subjects) | she **did not write** | **Did** she **write**? |

**Formula:** V2 (positive). *did not* + V1 (negative). *Did* + subject + V1 (question).

> *We **went** to Madurai last week.*
> *Gandhiji **was born** in 1869.*
> *She **got** up, **brushed** her teeth and **went** for a walk.* (a series of past actions)
> *When I was small, I **played** in the street every evening.* (a past habit)

In the negative and question, the main verb goes back to **V1**: *She did not **write**.* *Did she **write**?*

**Signal words:** *yesterday, last night, last week, last year, ago, in 2010, on Monday, when I was young*

### Rule 7: Use the past continuous for an action that was going on at a time in the past.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| I / he / she / it **was writing** | **was not writing** | **Was** she **writing**? |
| we / you / they **were writing** | **were not writing** | **Were** they **writing**? |

**Formula:** *was / were* + V4

> *At 9 o'clock last night, I **was studying**.* (in progress at a past time)
> *We **were having** dinner when the lights went off.* (interrupted by a short action)
> ***While** Ravi **was cooking**, his sister **was cleaning** the house.* (two actions going on together)
> *The birds **were singing** and the sun **was shining**.* (background in a story)

**The interruption pattern:** the long action is past continuous; the short action that breaks it is past simple.

> *I **was crossing** the road **when** I **saw** an accident.*

**Signal words:** *while, when, as, at 6 pm yesterday, at that time, all evening*

### Rule 8: Use the past perfect for the earlier of two past actions.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| she **had written** (all subjects) | **had not written** | **Had** she **written**? |

**Formula:** *had* + V3

When we talk about two things in the past, the past perfect shows the one that happened **first**.

> *When we reached the station, the train **had** already **left**.*
> (1st: the train left. 2nd: we reached.)
> *She **had finished** her homework before her friends came.*
> *I **had** never **seen** the sea before I went to Chennai.*

If the order is already clear from *before* or *after*, the past simple is also accepted: *She finished her homework before her friends came.* But in exams, use the past perfect for the earlier action when the question expects it.

**Signal words:** *before, after, already, by the time, when, until, never... before*

### Rule 9: Use the past perfect continuous for an action that went on for some time before another past action.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| she **had been writing** (all subjects) | **had not been writing** | **Had** she **been writing**? |

**Formula:** *had been* + V4

> *Karthik **had been playing** for two hours when it started to rain.*
> *They **had been living** in Coimbatore for ten years before they moved to Chennai.*
> *Her eyes were tired because she **had been reading** all night.*

**Signal words:** *for, since, how long*, together with a past time (*when, before, by the time*)

### Rule 10: Use the future simple for predictions, promises, offers and decisions made at the moment of speaking.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| she **will write** (all subjects) | **will not (won't) write** | **Will** she **write**? |
| I / we **shall write** (formal British) | **shall not (shan't) write** | **Shall** I / we **write**? |

**Formula:** *will / shall* + V1

> *I think India **will win** the match.* (prediction)
> *I **will help** you with your project.* (promise)
> *The phone is ringing. I'**ll answer** it.* (decision made now)
> ***Shall** I **open** the window?* (offer)
> ***Shall** we **go** to the beach?* (suggestion)

**Will and shall:** *will* is correct with every subject. *Shall* with *I* and *we* is formal and found mostly in British English. Today *shall* is used mainly in questions to offer or suggest: *Shall I...? Shall we...?*

**Signal words:** *tomorrow, next week, next year, soon, in 2030, I think, probably, perhaps*

### Rule 11: Use the future continuous for an action that will be going on at a time in the future.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| she **will be writing** | **will not be writing** | **Will** she **be writing**? |

**Formula:** *will / shall be* + V4

> *At this time tomorrow, we **will be travelling** to Kanyakumari.*
> *Don't call at 7. I **will be having** dinner.*
> *Divya **will be waiting** for you at the gate.*

It is also a polite way to ask about someone's plans:

> ***Will** you **be using** the computer this evening?*

**Signal words:** *at this time tomorrow, at 10 am tomorrow, this time next week, all day tomorrow*

### Rule 12: Use the future perfect for an action that will be finished before a time in the future.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| she **will have written** | **will not have written** | **Will** she **have written**? |

**Formula:** *will / shall have* + V3

> *By next March, Arun **will have finished** his course.*
> *The guests **will have left** by the time we reach home.*
> *I **will have saved** ten thousand rupees by Deepavali.*

**Signal words:** *by tomorrow, by next week, by 2030, by the time, before*

### Rule 13: Use the future perfect continuous for an action that will have gone on for a period up to a time in the future.

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| she **will have been writing** | **will not have been writing** | **Will** she **have been writing**? |

**Formula:** *will / shall have been* + V4

> *By next June, my father **will have been working** in this bank for twenty years.*
> *By 6 o'clock, Meena **will have been studying** for five hours.*

This tense is rare in speech. It needs two things: a future point (*by next June*) and a period (*for twenty years*).

**Signal words:** *by... for..., by the time... for...*

### Rule 14: Use *be going to*, the present continuous or the present simple for some kinds of future.

*Will* is not the only way to talk about the future.

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *am / is / are going to* + V1 | a plan or intention decided before now | *I **am going to buy** a new cycle next month.* |
| *am / is / are going to* + V1 | a prediction based on something we can see now | *Look at those dark clouds. It **is going to rain**.* |
| present continuous | a fixed arrangement (time and place are settled) | *We **are leaving** for Ooty on Friday.* |
| present simple | a timetable or official programme | *The exam **starts** on 3 March.* *The bus **leaves** at 7.* |
| *am / is / are about to* + V1 | something that will happen very soon | *Hurry! The film **is about to start**.* |

Compare *will* and *going to*:

> *A: We have no milk. B: Oh, **I'll buy** some.* (decided now → *will*)
> *A: Why are you taking the bag? B: **I'm going to buy** some milk.* (decided before → *going to*)

### Rule 15: Stative verbs are not normally used in the continuous tenses.

Some verbs show a **state** (a condition that stays the same), not an action. They are called **stative verbs**. We usually use them in the simple form, even when we mean "now".

> *I **know** the answer.* (not *~~I am knowing the answer~~*)
> *This bag **belongs** to Kavya.* (not *~~is belonging~~*)
> *Ravi **owns** two cows.* (not *~~is owning~~*)
> *She **believes** in hard work.*

| Group | Stative verbs |
|---|---|
| Thinking | know, believe, understand, remember, forget, mean, suppose, realise |
| Feeling | like, love, hate, want, need, prefer, wish |
| Having | have (= own), own, possess, belong, contain, consist |
| Senses | see, hear, smell, taste (when they mean "notice" or "have a quality") |
| Being | be, seem, appear, look (= seem), resemble, cost, weigh (= have weight) |

See the special cases below for verbs that can be both.

## Special cases

### 1. Verbs that can be stative or action verbs

Some verbs change meaning. In one meaning they are stative (no *-ing*); in another they are actions (*-ing* is fine).

| Stative meaning (simple only) | Action meaning (continuous possible) |
|---|---|
| *I **think** you are right.* (= believe) | *I **am thinking** about my project.* (= considering) |
| *He **has** a car.* (= owns) | *He **is having** lunch.* (= eating) |
| *I **see** what you mean.* (= understand) | *I **am seeing** the doctor at 5.* (= meeting) |
| *The soup **tastes** good.* (= has a taste) | *Amma **is tasting** the soup.* (= trying it) |
| *The flower **smells** sweet.* | *She **is smelling** the flower.* |
| *He **is** rude.* (his nature) | *He **is being** rude today.* (behaving rudely now) |
| *The baby **weighs** four kilos.* | *The shopkeeper **is weighing** the rice.* |

### 2. Present simple, not *will*, after *when, if, before, until, as soon as*

In a clause about the future that begins with a time or condition word, use the **present simple**, not *will*.

> *I will call you **when** I **reach** Chennai.* (not *~~when I will reach~~*)
> ***If** it **rains**, we will stay at home.* (not *~~If it will rain~~*)
> *Wait here **until** I **come** back.*

Also use the present perfect, not the future perfect: *I will leave **as soon as** I **have finished**.*

### 3. *Always* with the present continuous

*Always* usually goes with the present simple. But *always* + present continuous shows a habit that annoys the speaker:

> *He **is always losing** his keys!*

### 4. Present perfect or past simple with *this morning / today*

If the period is **still going on**, use the present perfect. If it is **over**, use the past simple.

> *I **have drunk** three cups of tea today.* (today is not over)
> *I **drank** three cups of tea this morning.* (said in the evening: the morning is over)

### 5. *Been* and *gone*

> *Ravi **has gone** to Delhi.* (He is in Delhi now, or on his way.)
> *Ravi **has been** to Delhi.* (He went there and came back. It is his experience.)

### 6. *Used to* and *would* for past habits

> *I **used to** live in Salem.* (a past state or habit, not true now)
> *Every summer, we **would** visit our grandparents.* (a repeated past action)

*Would* is used for repeated actions only, not for states. *~~I would live in Salem~~* (for a past state) is wrong. See [Modal auxiliaries (Class 11)](/topics/t02-modal-auxiliaries/class-11/).

### 7. The past continuous is not used for a short, complete action

> *She **opened** the door.* (short, complete → past simple)
> *She **was opening** the door when the phone rang.* (in progress, interrupted → past continuous)

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *I ~~am knowing~~ him well.* | *I **know** him well.* | *Know* is a stative verb. It is not used in the continuous. |
| *She ~~did not went~~ to school.* | *She **did not go** to school.* | After *did*, use V1. |
| *I ~~have seen~~ him yesterday.* | *I **saw** him yesterday.* | *Yesterday* is a finished past time. Use the past simple. |
| *I am living here ~~since~~ five years.* | *I **have been living** here **for** five years.* | *Five years* is a period, so use *for*. An action from the past up to now needs the present perfect (continuous). |
| *He ~~go~~ to school every day.* | *He **goes** to school every day.* | Present simple with *he* needs V5. |
| *If it ~~will rain~~, we will stay home.* | *If it **rains**, we will stay home.* | After *if* (and *when*), use the present simple for the future. |
| *When I reached, the bus ~~left~~ already.* | *When I reached, the bus **had** already **left**.* | The bus left first. The earlier past action takes the past perfect. |
| *Did you ~~finished~~ your work?* | ***Did** you **finish** your work?* / ***Have** you **finished** your work?* | After *did*, use V1. Or use the present perfect, with *have* + V3. |
| *She ~~is having~~ two brothers.* | *She **has** two brothers.* | *Have* meaning "own / be related to" is stative. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Fill in the blanks** with the correct form of the verb in brackets.
- **Identify the tense** of the underlined verb.
- **Spot and correct the error.**
- **Cloze passage:** a paragraph with blanks, where each verb must fit the time of the story.

### Steps for "Fill in with the correct tense form"

1. **Find the signal words** (*yesterday, now, since, by next year, every day, when, while*).
2. **Decide the time:** present, past or future.
3. **Decide the aspect:** habit or fact (simple)? going on (continuous)? finished before a point (perfect)? going on for a period up to a point (perfect continuous)?
4. **Find the subject** and choose the right helping verb (*is / are, has / have, was / were*).
5. **Choose the verb form** (V1, V2, V3, V4, V5).
6. **Check:** is the verb stative? Is the blank after *if / when*? Is there a second past action?

### Steps for "Identify the tense"

1. **Find the full verb phrase** (all the helping verbs + the main verb).
2. **Look at the first helping verb** to find the **time**: *am / is / are / has / have / do / does* → present; *was / were / had / did* → past; *will / shall* → future. No helping verb: V1/V5 → present, V2 → past.
3. **Look at the rest** to find the **aspect**: *be* + V4 → continuous; *have* + V3 → perfect; *have been* + V4 → perfect continuous; nothing else → simple.
4. **Name it:** time + aspect.

### Worked example 1

> *Meena ______ (live) in Madurai since 2018.*

1. Signal word: *since 2018*.
2. Time: started in the past, still true now → present.
3. Aspect: a period up to now → perfect. (*Live* can also take the perfect continuous.)
4. Subject: *Meena* → *has*.
5. Form: *has* + V3 → *has lived*.

**Answer:** *Meena **has lived** in Madurai since 2018.* (*has been living* is also correct.)

### Worked example 2

> *When the teacher entered, the students ______ (talk) loudly.*

1. Signal word: *when* + a past action (*entered*).
2. Time: past.
3. Aspect: the talking was going on when the teacher entered → continuous.
4. Subject: *the students* (plural) → *were*.
5. Form: *were* + V4 → *were talking*.

**Answer:** *When the teacher entered, the students **were talking** loudly.*

### Worked example 3

> *By the time the doctor came, the patient ______ (die).*

1. Signal words: *by the time* + a past action (*came*).
2. Time: past.
3. Aspect: the death happened **before** the doctor came → perfect.
4. Subject: *the patient* → *had* (all subjects).
5. Form: *had* + V3 → *had died*.

**Answer:** *By the time the doctor came, the patient **had died**.*

### Worked example 4

> *Identify the tense: The workers **will have been working** for ten hours by evening.*

1. Verb phrase: *will have been working*.
2. First helping verb: *will* → future.
3. Rest: *have been* + V4 → perfect continuous.

**Answer:** **Future perfect continuous.**

### Worked example 5 (cloze)

> *Last Sunday, Karthik ______ (1. wake) up early. While he ______ (2. drink) his coffee, his friend ______ (3. call). They ______ (4. plan) a trip to Ooty a week before. At eight, they ______ (5. leave) for the bus stand.*

1. ***woke***: a finished action in a past story → past simple.
2. ***was drinking***: going on when something else happened → past continuous.
3. ***called***: the short action that interrupts → past simple.
4. ***had planned***: done *a week before* that Sunday → the earlier past action → past perfect.
5. ***left***: the next finished action in the story → past simple.

## Quick check

1. *Look! The baby ______ (sleep).*
2. *We ______ (visit) the zoo last Saturday.*
3. *She ______ (not finish) her project yet.*
4. *I will phone you as soon as I ______ (get) the results.*
5. Identify the tense: *They **had been waiting** for an hour.*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***is sleeping.*** *Look!* shows an action going on now → present continuous. *The baby* → *is*.
2. ***visited.*** *Last Saturday* is a finished past time → past simple (V2).
3. ***has not finished.*** *Yet* goes with the present perfect in negatives and questions. *She* → *has*.
4. ***get.*** After *as soon as* (a time word), use the present simple for the future, not *will get*.
5. **Past perfect continuous.** *Had* (past) + *been* + V4 (perfect continuous).

</details>

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- Tense = **time** (present / past / future) + **aspect** (simple / continuous / perfect / perfect continuous).
- Continuous = *be* + V4. Perfect = *have* + V3. Perfect continuous = *have been* + V4.
- Present perfect: connected to now; never with *yesterday, ago, last week*.
- Past perfect: the **earlier** of two past actions.
- After *if, when, as soon as, until*: present simple for the future.
- *since* + point of time; *for* + period of time.
- Stative verbs (*know, believe, own, belong, like*): no continuous form.
- Future also with *going to* (plans, evidence), present continuous (arrangements), present simple (timetables).
:::
