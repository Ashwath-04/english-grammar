## What you will learn

- What **active** and **passive** voice are, and **when and why** we use the passive
- The rule for changing active into passive: **object → subject**, ***be* (same tense) + V3**, ***by* + agent** (often left out)
- The passive in **every tense** where it is possible, with a table
- Why the perfect continuous tenses and the future continuous are **not normally used** in the passive
- **Pronoun changes**: *I → me, he → him, they → them*
- How to **change the voice**, **fill in passive forms**, and **rewrite a process** in the passive

## Why this matters

The passive is everywhere in formal English: news reports (*Ten people **were injured***), science (*The solution **is heated***), notices (*Photography **is not allowed***) and instructions.

In the exam, you will change sentences from active to passive (and back), fill in passive forms, and rewrite a process (how tea is made, how paper is recycled) in the passive. The passive depends on two earlier lessons: [Tenses](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/) and [Verb forms](/foundation/f2-verb-forms/) (especially V3).

## The idea in simple words

Look at these two sentences.

> *Ravi **broke** the window.*
> *The window **was broken** by Ravi.*

They describe the same event. The difference is **which one we talk about first**.

- In the first sentence, we start with **Ravi**, the person who did the action. This is the **active voice**.
- In the second, we start with **the window**, the thing that the action happened to. This is the **passive voice**.

Now look at this:

> *The window **was broken**.*

We don't know (or don't care) who broke it. The passive lets us talk about an action **without naming the doer**. This is the main reason we use it.

## Grammar words you need

**Voice:** the form of the verb that shows whether the subject **does** the action (active) or **receives** it (passive).

**Active voice:** the subject does the action. *The cat **ate** the fish.*

**Passive voice:** the subject receives the action. *The fish **was eaten** by the cat.*

**Agent (doer):** the person or thing that does the action. In the passive, it comes after ***by***: *by the cat*.

**Object:** the person or thing that receives the action in an active sentence. *The cat ate **the fish**.* See [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/).

**Transitive verb:** a verb that takes an object (*eat something, write something*). Only transitive verbs have a passive.

**Intransitive verb:** a verb with no object (*sleep, arrive, go*). It has no passive.

**V3 (past participle):** *written, eaten, broken, done, made*. Every passive verb uses V3.

## The rules

### Rule 1: Use the passive when the doer is unknown, obvious or unimportant, or when the action is more important than the doer.

| Reason | Example |
|---|---|
| The doer is **unknown** | *My bicycle **was stolen** last night.* (We don't know who stole it.) |
| The doer is **obvious** | *The thief **was arrested**.* (by the police, of course) |
| The doer is **unimportant** | *Rice **is grown** in Thanjavur.* (We care about the rice, not the farmers' names.) |
| We want to focus on the **receiver** | *The Taj Mahal **was built** by Shah Jahan.* (The topic is the Taj Mahal.) |
| **Formal, scientific or official** writing | *The mixture **is heated** to 100 °C.* / *Visitors **are requested** not to touch the exhibits.* |
| To sound **polite** or avoid blame | *A mistake **has been made**.* (instead of *You made a mistake.*) |

In everyday speech, the active is more common and more direct. Use the passive when you have a reason.

### Rule 2: Only verbs that take an object can be made passive.

> *Arun **wrote** a letter.* → *A letter was written by Arun.* ✔ (*a letter* is the object)
> *The baby **slept**.* → no passive ✘ (*slept* has no object)
> *They **arrived** late.* → no passive ✘

Before you change any sentence, find the object. **No object, no passive.**

### Rule 3: To change active to passive, follow three steps.

> *Active:* ***The teacher*** *corrects* ***the papers****.*

1. **The object becomes the subject.** *The papers* → new subject.
2. **The verb becomes *be* + V3**, with *be* in the **same tense** as the active verb. *Corrects* is present simple → *are* + *corrected*. (*Are* because *the papers* is plural.)
3. **The old subject goes after *by*** (or is left out). *The teacher* → *by the teacher*.

> *Passive:* ***The papers*** *are corrected* ***by the teacher****.*

| Active | → | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Subject (*the teacher*) | → | *by* + agent (*by the teacher*) |
| Verb (*corrects*) | → | *be* (same tense) + V3 (*are corrected*) |
| Object (*the papers*) | → | Subject (*the papers*) |

### Rule 4: Keep the tense the same. Change only the form of *be*.

The tense never changes when you change the voice. A past simple active becomes a past simple passive.

| Tense | Active | Passive formula | Passive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present simple | *She **writes** a letter.* | *am / is / are* + V3 | *A letter **is written** by her.* |
| Present continuous | *She **is writing** a letter.* | *am / is / are **being*** + V3 | *A letter **is being written** by her.* |
| Present perfect | *She **has written** a letter.* | *has / have **been*** + V3 | *A letter **has been written** by her.* |
| Past simple | *She **wrote** a letter.* | *was / were* + V3 | *A letter **was written** by her.* |
| Past continuous | *She **was writing** a letter.* | *was / were **being*** + V3 | *A letter **was being written** by her.* |
| Past perfect | *She **had written** a letter.* | *had **been*** + V3 | *A letter **had been written** by her.* |
| Future simple | *She **will write** a letter.* | *will / shall **be*** + V3 | *A letter **will be written** by her.* |
| Future perfect | *She **will have written** a letter.* | *will / shall **have been*** + V3 | *A letter **will have been written** by her.* |
| *be going to* | *She **is going to write** a letter.* | *am / is / are going to **be*** + V3 | *A letter **is going to be written** by her.* |

**A quick way to remember:** take the active verb, put ***be*** in the same form, and add V3.
- *writes* (simple) → ***is** written*
- *is writ**ing*** (continuous) → *is **being** written*
- *has writ**ten*** (perfect) → *has **been** written*
- *will write* → *will **be** written*

See the full chart on the [Passive voice table](/reference/passive-voice-table/).

### Rule 5: The perfect continuous tenses and the future continuous are not normally used in the passive.

These four tenses have no common passive form:

- present perfect continuous: *She has been writing a letter.*
- past perfect continuous: *She had been writing a letter.*
- future continuous: *She will be writing a letter.*
- future perfect continuous: *She will have been writing a letter.*

Their passive forms (*~~has been being written~~*, *~~will be being written~~*) are very awkward and almost never used. If an exam gives one of these, the usual answer is: **"The passive is not normally used for this tense."** (Some teachers accept a nearby tense, such as the present perfect: *A letter **has been written***, but this changes the meaning slightly.)

### Rule 6: Change the pronouns between subject form and object form.

The **subject** of the active sentence goes after *by*, so it changes to the **object form**. The **object** of the active sentence becomes the subject, so it changes to the **subject form**.

| Subject form | Object form |
|---|---|
| I | me |
| we | us |
| you | you |
| he | him |
| she | her |
| it | it |
| they | them |
| who | whom |

> *Active:* ***I** helped **him**.*
> *Passive:* ***He** was helped by **me**.*

> *Active:* ***They** invited **us**.*
> *Passive:* ***We** were invited by **them**.*

### Rule 7: The verb agrees with the new subject.

After the change, *be* must match the **new** subject in number.

> *Active:* *The boy **plays** two games.*
> *Passive:* *Two games **are** played by the boy.* (plural subject → *are*)

> *Active:* *The children **eat** a cake.*
> *Passive:* *A cake **is** eaten by the children.* (singular subject → *is*)

See [Subject–verb agreement](/topics/t05-subject-verb-agreement/class-12/).

### Rule 8: Leave out *by* + agent when it is unknown, obvious or unimportant.

These subjects are usually **left out** in the passive: *someone, somebody, people, they* (meaning people in general), *one, nobody* (changes to a negative).

> *Active:* *Someone has stolen my purse.*
> *Passive:* *My purse **has been stolen**.* (not *~~by someone~~*)

> *Active:* *People speak Tamil in Sri Lanka.*
> *Passive:* *Tamil **is spoken** in Sri Lanka.*

> *Active:* *The police caught the thief.*
> *Passive:* *The thief **was caught**.* (or *by the police*; both are correct)

Keep *by* + agent when the doer is **important** or **new information**:

> *"Gitanjali" was written **by Tagore**.*

### Rule 9: In negative sentences, *not* goes after the first helping verb.

> *Active:* *They did not invite me.*
> *Passive:* *I **was not** invited (by them).*

> *Active:* *She has not finished the work.*
> *Passive:* *The work **has not been** finished (by her).*

Notice: *did not invite* (past simple, negative) becomes *was not invited*. The *did* disappears, because *was* is now the helping verb.

### Rule 10: To describe a process, use the present simple passive and sequence words.

A **process** (how something is made or done) is usually written in the passive, because the steps matter more than the people.

> *How tea is made:*
> ***First**, the tea leaves **are plucked**. **Then** they **are dried** in the sun. **After that**, they **are rolled** and **cut**. **Finally**, the tea **is packed** and **sent** to the market.*

Use the **present simple passive** (*is / are* + V3) for a general process, and the **past simple passive** (*was / were* + V3) for a process that happened once (such as a science experiment you did).

Sequence words: *first, firstly, then, next, after that, later, finally, at last*.

## Special cases

### 1. Phrasal verbs and verbs with prepositions keep the small word

> *Active:* *The nurse **looked after** the baby.*
> *Passive:* *The baby **was looked after** by the nurse.* (not *~~was looked by~~*)

> *Active:* *Everyone **laughed at** him.*
> *Passive:* *He **was laughed at** (by everyone).*

### 2. Some verbs with objects still have no passive

Some stative verbs do not normally go into the passive, even with an object: *have* (= own), *resemble, fit, suit, lack, contain* (in some uses).

> *She **has** a car.* → *~~A car is had by her.~~* ✘
> *Ravi **resembles** his father.* → *~~His father is resembled by Ravi.~~* ✘

### 3. *Be born* is always passive

> *I **was born** in Madurai.* (not *~~I born~~*)

There is no active form in normal use.

### 4. Passive to active

To change passive to active, reverse the steps: the agent after *by* becomes the subject, the subject becomes the object, and the verb goes back to the active form in the same tense.

> *Passive:* *The match was won by Chennai.*
> *Active:* *Chennai **won** the match.*

If there is **no agent**, add a suitable subject: *someone, people, they, the police...*

> *Passive:* *The road has been repaired.*
> *Active:* ***They** have repaired the road.* (or *Someone has repaired the road.*)

### 5. *Get* passive (informal)

In speech, *get* + V3 is sometimes used instead of *be* + V3, especially for accidents or changes:

> *He **got hurt** in the match.* / *They **got married** last year.*

In exams, use *be*.

### 6. *By* or *with*

*By* is used for the doer. *With* is used for the tool or material used by the doer:

> *The tree was cut down **by** the workers **with** an axe.*

See [Prepositions (Class 11)](/topics/t04-prepositions/class-11/).

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *The letter ~~was write~~ by Priya.* | *The letter **was written** by Priya.* | The passive needs V3, not V1. |
| *The letter ~~was wrote~~ by Priya.* | *The letter **was written** by Priya.* | V3 (*written*), not V2 (*wrote*). |
| *They built the house last year.* → *The house ~~is built~~ last year.* | *The house **was built** last year.* | Keep the tense: past simple active → *was / were* + V3. |
| *The work ~~has done~~.* | *The work **has been done**.* | Perfect passive: *has / have **been*** + V3. |
| *The song is being sung by ~~she~~.* | *The song is being sung by **her**.* | After *by*, use the object form. |
| *~~Him~~ was helped by them.* | ***He** was helped by them.* | The new subject takes the subject form. |
| *Many mangoes ~~was~~ eaten by the children.* | *Many mangoes **were** eaten by the children.* | The verb agrees with the new plural subject. |
| *My pen has been stolen ~~by someone~~.* | *My pen has been stolen.* | Leave out *by someone*. It adds nothing. |
| *~~The bus was arrived late.~~* | *The bus **arrived** late.* | *Arrive* has no object, so it has no passive. |
| *The baby was looked ~~by~~ the nurse.* | *The baby was looked **after** by the nurse.* | Keep the small word of the phrasal verb. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Change the voice** (active → passive, or passive → active).
- **Fill in the blanks** with the passive form of the verb in brackets.
- **Rewrite a process** (a paragraph of instructions or steps) in the passive.

### Steps for "Change into passive"

1. **Find the subject, the verb and the object** of the active sentence.
2. **Check:** is there an object? If not, there is no passive.
3. **Find the tense** of the active verb.
4. **Make the object the new subject** (change a pronoun to the subject form: *him → he*).
5. **Write *be* in the same tense**, matching the new subject (*is / are, was / were, has / have been, will be...*).
6. **Add V3** of the main verb.
7. **Add *by* + the old subject** in the object form (*I → me*), or leave it out if it is *someone, people, they*.
8. **Keep the rest** (time, place) at the end.

### Worked example 1

> *Kavya is painting a picture.*

1. Subject: *Kavya*. Verb: *is painting*. Object: *a picture*.
2. Object? Yes.
3. Tense: present continuous.
4. New subject: *A picture*.
5. *Be*: present continuous → *is being* (singular).
6. V3: *painted*.
7. *by Kavya*.

**Answer:** *A picture **is being painted** by Kavya.*

### Worked example 2

> *They have not repaired the road yet.*

1. Subject: *They*. Verb: *have not repaired*. Object: *the road*.
2. Tense: present perfect (negative).
3. New subject: *The road* (singular) → *has not been*.
4. V3: *repaired*.
5. *They* (people in general) → leave out.

**Answer:** *The road **has not been repaired** yet.*

### Worked example 3

> *Did the boys break the window?*

1. Subject: *the boys*. Verb: *did ... break* (past simple question). Object: *the window*.
2. New subject: *the window* (singular) → past simple passive: *was ... broken*.
3. Question: put *was* before the subject.
4. *by the boys*.

**Answer:** ***Was** the window **broken** by the boys?* (More question types are in [Active and passive voice (Class 12)](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-12/).)

### Worked example 4

> *Change into active: The thieves were caught by the villagers.*

1. Agent: *the villagers* → new subject.
2. Subject: *the thieves* → new object.
3. Verb: *were caught* (past simple passive) → active past simple: *caught*.

**Answer:** *The villagers **caught** the thieves.*

### Worked example 5 (process)

> *Rewrite in the passive: First, we wash the rice. Then we soak it for an hour. Next, we grind it into a batter. Finally, we leave the batter overnight.*

1. The doer *we* is not important → leave it out.
2. A general process → present simple passive (*is / are* + V3).
3. Keep the sequence words.

**Answer:** *First, the rice **is washed**. Then it **is soaked** for an hour. Next, it **is ground** into a batter. Finally, the batter **is left** overnight.*

(*Grind – ground – ground*.)

## Quick check

Change into passive.

1. *The farmer grows sugarcane.*
2. *Somebody has taken my umbrella.*
3. *Arun will invite you to the party.*
4. *The workers were building a new bridge.*
5. *I had finished the work before noon.*

1. ***Sugarcane is grown by the farmer.*** Present simple → *is* + V3.
2. ***My umbrella has been taken.*** Present perfect → *has been* + V3. *By somebody* is left out.
3. ***You will be invited to the party by Arun.*** Future simple → *will be* + V3.
4. ***A new bridge was being built by the workers.*** Past continuous → *was being* + V3.
5. ***The work had been finished by me before noon.*** Past perfect → *had been* + V3. *I → me*.

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- **Passive = *be* (same tense) + V3.** Object → subject. Subject → *by* + agent.
- Use the passive when the doer is unknown, obvious or unimportant, or in formal and scientific writing.
- **No object, no passive** (*arrive, sleep, go*).
- Continuous → *being* + V3. Perfect → *been* + V3. Future → *will be* + V3.
- Perfect continuous and future continuous: no normal passive.
- Pronouns: *I → me, he → him, she → her, we → us, they → them* after *by*.
- Leave out *by someone / by people / by them* (general).
- Processes: present simple passive + *first, then, next, finally*.
:::
