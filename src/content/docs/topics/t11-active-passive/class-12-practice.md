## Part A: Modals

**1.** *We must protect the environment.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The environment must be protected (by us).*** Modal + *be* + V3. The modal *must* stays the same.

</details>

**2.** *You can send the form online.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The form can be sent online (by you).*** *Can* + *be* + V3. *Send – sent – sent*.

</details>

**3.** *They should have repaired the road before the rains.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The road should have been repaired before the rains.*** Modal + *have* + V3 → modal + *have been* + V3. *They* (general) is left out.

</details>

**4.** *Someone might have taken your bag by mistake.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Your bag might have been taken by mistake.*** *Might have been* + V3. *By someone* is left out.

</details>

**5.** *You ought to respect your teachers.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Your teachers ought to be respected (by you).*** *Ought to* + *be* + V3.

</details>

## Part B: Questions

**6.** *Did Arun paint this picture?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Was this picture painted by Arun?*** Past simple question → *was* + subject + V3. *Did* disappears.

</details>

**7.** *Does your mother make sweets at Deepavali?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Are sweets made by your mother at Deepavali?*** Present simple → *are* + V3, because the new subject (*sweets*) is plural. *Does* disappears.

</details>

**8.** *Have you completed the assignment?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Has the assignment been completed by you?*** Present perfect → *has been* + V3. The new subject is singular → *has*.

</details>

**9.** *Will they postpone the exam?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Will the exam be postponed?*** Future → *will* + subject + *be* + V3. *They* (general) is left out.

</details>

**10.** *Who wrote "Ponniyin Selvan"?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***By whom was "Ponniyin Selvan" written?*** *Who* is the subject (the doer) → *By whom*. Past simple → *was* + V3.

</details>

**11.** *Who is making that noise?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***By whom is that noise being made?*** Present continuous → *is being* + V3.

</details>

**12.** *Why did the police arrest him?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Why was he arrested (by the police)?*** *Why* is not the subject, so it stays at the front. *Him → he*.

</details>

**13.** *Whom did you meet at the station?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Who was met by you at the station?*** **Trap:** *whom* was the object. In the passive it becomes the subject, so it changes to *who*.

</details>

**14.** *What did Kavya cook for lunch?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***What was cooked by Kavya for lunch?*** *What* was the object; now it is the subject. Past simple → *was* + V3.

</details>

## Part C: Commands and requests

**15.** *Close the gate.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Let the gate be closed.*** A command with an object → *Let* + object + *be* + V3.

</details>

**16.** *Don't waste water.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Let water not be wasted.*** (or *Let not water be wasted*, formal.) Negative command → *Let ... not be* + V3. (*You are requested not to waste water* is also accepted.)

</details>

**17.** *Please lend me your notes.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***You are requested to lend me your notes.*** *Please* shows a request → *You are requested to* + V1.

</details>

**18.** *Get out of the room at once!*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***You are ordered to get out of the room at once.*** A strong command with no object → *You are ordered to*.

</details>

**19.** *Take this medicine twice a day.* (a doctor speaking)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***You are advised to take this medicine twice a day.*** (or *Let this medicine be taken twice a day*.) Advice for the listener's good → *advised*.

</details>

**20.** *Let us plant some trees.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Let some trees be planted (by us).*** *Let us* + object → *Let* + object + *be* + V3.

</details>

## Part D: Two objects

**21.** *The manager offered Ravi a job.* (two ways)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

- ***Ravi was offered a job by the manager.*** (person as subject: more common)
- ***A job was offered to Ravi by the manager.*** (thing as subject: add *to*)

</details>

**22.** *Mr Kumar teaches us chemistry.* (two ways)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

- ***We are taught chemistry by Mr Kumar.*** (*us → we*)
- ***Chemistry is taught to us by Mr Kumar.***

</details>

**23.** *My aunt bought me a new dress.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***A new dress was bought for me by my aunt.*** With *buy*, use ***for*** before the person. (*I was bought a new dress* is possible but sounds unnatural.)

</details>

## Part E: Agents other than *by*

**24.** *Everyone knows this story.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***This story is known to everyone.*** *Known* (= familiar) takes *to*, not *by*.

</details>

**25.** *Smoke filled the kitchen.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The kitchen was filled with smoke.*** *Filled* takes *with*.

</details>

**26.** *Her rude reply surprised us.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***We were surprised at (or by) her rude reply.*** *Surprised* takes *at* or *by*.

</details>

## Part F: The impersonal passive

**27.** *People say that honesty is the best policy.* (two ways)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

- ***It is said that honesty is the best policy.***
- ***Honesty is said to be the best policy.***

</details>

**28.** *People believe that the king hid his treasure in the temple.* (two ways)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

- ***It is believed that the king hid his treasure in the temple.***
- ***The king is believed to have hidden his treasure in the temple.***

**Trap:** the hiding happened **before** the believing, so form (b) needs the perfect infinitive (*to have* + V3).

</details>

**29.** *They reported that the minister was ill.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***It was reported that the minister was ill.*** (or ***The minister was reported to be ill.***) The reporting verb is past → *was reported*.

</details>

## Part G: Infinitives, gerunds and verbs with no passive

**30.** *I want someone to clean my room.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***I want my room to be cleaned.*** Infinitive passive: *to be* + V3.

</details>

**31.** *She doesn't like people teasing her.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***She doesn't like being teased.*** Gerund passive: *being* + V3.

</details>

**32.** *The teacher made the students repeat the poem.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The students were made to repeat the poem (by the teacher).*** *Make* in the passive takes *to* before the infinitive.

</details>

**33.** *The sun rises in the east.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**No passive is possible.** **Trap:** *rise* is intransitive (it has no object). The sentence can only be active.

</details>
