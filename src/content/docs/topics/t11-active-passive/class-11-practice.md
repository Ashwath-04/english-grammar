## Part A: Change into the passive voice

**1.** *Meena sings a song.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***A song is sung by Meena.*** Present simple → *is* + V3. *Sing – sang – sung*.

</details>

**2.** *The farmers grow rice in Thanjavur.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Rice is grown in Thanjavur (by the farmers).*** Present simple → *is* + V3. The agent can be left out because it is obvious.

</details>

**3.** *Ravi is writing a story.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***A story is being written by Ravi.*** Present continuous → *is being* + V3.

</details>

**4.** *The children have planted twenty trees.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Twenty trees have been planted by the children.*** Present perfect → *have been* + V3. The new subject is plural → *have*.

</details>

**5.** *My brother repaired the fan.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The fan was repaired by my brother.*** Past simple → *was* + V3.

</details>

**6.** *The police were questioning the suspects.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The suspects were being questioned by the police.*** Past continuous → *were being* + V3.

</details>

**7.** *The guests had eaten all the sweets.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***All the sweets had been eaten by the guests.*** Past perfect → *had been* + V3. *Eat – ate – eaten*.

</details>

**8.** *The principal will announce the results tomorrow.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The results will be announced (by the principal) tomorrow.*** Future simple → *will be* + V3. The time word stays at the end.

</details>

**9.** *By June, they will have completed the new library.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***By June, the new library will have been completed.*** Future perfect → *will have been* + V3. *They* (general) is left out.

</details>

**10.** *I helped her.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***She was helped by me.*** **Trap:** both pronouns change. *Her* (object) → *she* (subject). *I* (subject) → *by me* (object form).

</details>

**11.** *They invited us to the wedding.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***We were invited to the wedding (by them).*** *Us → we*, and *we* takes *were*.

</details>

**12.** *Someone has stolen my bicycle.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***My bicycle has been stolen.*** *By someone* adds nothing, so it is left out.

</details>

**13.** *She did not invite Arun.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Arun was not invited by her.*** Past simple negative → *was not* + V3. The *did* disappears because *was* is now the helping verb.

</details>

**14.** *The nurse looks after the patients.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The patients are looked after by the nurse.*** The phrasal verb keeps its small word (*after*). *Looked after* + *by*.

</details>

**15.** *The students are going to clean the classroom.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The classroom is going to be cleaned by the students.*** *Be going to* → *is going to be* + V3.

</details>

**16.** *She has been writing letters all morning.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**The passive is not normally used** for the present perfect continuous. **Trap:** *Letters have been being written* is technically possible but almost never used. (A nearby form, *Letters have been written*, changes the meaning: it says the letters are finished.)

</details>

## Part B: Change into the active voice

**17.** *The match was won by Chennai.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Chennai won the match.*** The agent (*Chennai*) becomes the subject. *Was won* (past simple) → *won*.

</details>

**18.** *The letters are delivered by the postman every morning.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The postman delivers the letters every morning.*** Present simple; *the postman* is singular → *delivers*.

</details>

**19.** *Our house has been painted.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Someone has painted our house.*** (or *They have painted our house* / *The painters have painted our house*.) **Trap:** there is no agent, so we must add a suitable subject.

</details>

**20.** *The thief was being chased by the dogs.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The dogs were chasing the thief.*** Past continuous passive → past continuous active. *The dogs* is plural → *were*.

</details>

## Part C: Fill in with the passive form of the verb

**21.** *English ______ (speak) in many countries.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is spoken.*** A general fact → present simple passive. *Speak – spoke – spoken*.

</details>

**22.** *The Brihadeeswarar Temple ______ (build) by Raja Raja Chola.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***was built.*** A finished past event → past simple passive.

</details>

**23.** *The new bridge ______ (open) next month.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***will be opened.*** Future → *will be* + V3.

</details>

**24.** *Look! The road ______ (repair).*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is being repaired.*** *Look!* shows an action going on now → present continuous passive.

</details>

**25.** *All the tickets ______ already ______ (sell).*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***have*** ... ***been sold.*** *Already* goes with the present perfect → *have been* + V3. *Sell – sold – sold*.

</details>

## Part D: Rewrite the process in the passive

> *How a mango drink is made: First, we wash the mangoes. Then we peel them and cut them into pieces. Next, we blend the pieces with water and sugar. Finally, we pour the drink into glasses and serve it cold.*

**26.** *First, we wash the mangoes.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***First, the mangoes are washed.*** A general process → present simple passive. *We* is not important, so it is left out.

</details>

**27.** *Then we peel them and cut them into pieces.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Then they are peeled and cut into pieces.*** *Them → they*. The second *are* can be left out when two verbs share it. *Cut – cut – cut*.

</details>

**28.** *Next, we blend the pieces with water and sugar.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Next, the pieces are blended with water and sugar.*** *With* stays, because water and sugar are the things used, not the doer.

</details>

**29.** *Finally, we pour the drink into glasses and serve it cold.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Finally, the drink is poured into glasses and served cold.*** One subject (*the drink*), two passive verbs sharing *is*.

</details>

## Part E: Find and correct the mistake

**30.** *The cake was ate by the children.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The cake was **eaten** by the children.* The passive needs V3 (*eaten*), not V2 (*ate*).

</details>

**31.** *The work has done.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The work **has been done**.* The perfect passive needs *been*: *has been* + V3.

</details>

**32.** *The train was arrived late.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The train **arrived** late.* **Trap:** *arrive* has no object, so it cannot be passive. The active sentence is the only correct form.

</details>
