## What you will learn

- When to use ***a*** and when to use ***an***: by the first **sound**, not the first letter (*an hour, a university, an MLA, a one-rupee coin*)
- Every main use of ***the***: things already mentioned, unique things, superlatives, rivers, seas, mountain ranges, newspapers, musical instruments, *the poor*, *the USA* and more
- When to use **no article** (the zero article): meals, languages, games, most countries and cities, general ideas, and *go to school* vs *go to the school*
- How to answer "Fill in with articles" and "Spot the error"

## Why this matters

*A, an* and *the* are among the most common words in English. They appear in almost every sentence. Mistakes with them are easy to make and easy to spot, so examiners love them.

In the exam, articles come as "Fill in the blanks with suitable articles" and in error-spotting questions. The rules also lead into [Determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/).

## The idea in simple words

Read this short story.

> *Yesterday Ravi saw **a** dog in **the** street. **The** dog was hungry, so he gave it **a** biscuit.*

- ***a** dog*: we hear about this dog for the first time. It is "one dog, any dog". We use ***a***.
- ***the** dog*: now we know which dog. It is the dog we already talked about. We use ***the***.
- ***the** street*: Ravi and the listener both know which street (the one where it happened).
- ***a** biscuit*: one biscuit, not a particular one.

So ***a / an*** means "one, not a particular one". ***The*** means "the one we both know".

Now look at this:

> ***Dogs** are loyal animals.*

There is no article before *dogs*. We are talking about dogs **in general**, all dogs. This is the **zero article**.

## Grammar words you need

**Article:** the words *a, an* and *the*. They come before a noun.

**Indefinite article:** *a* and *an*. They mean "one", or "any one". They do not point to a particular thing.

**Definite article:** *the*. It points to a particular thing that the listener knows.

**Zero article:** using no article at all before a noun: *Rice is grown in Thanjavur.*

**Countable noun:** a noun that can be counted: *one book, two books*.

**Uncountable noun:** a noun that cannot be counted: *water, milk, advice, information, furniture*. It has no plural and does not take *a / an*.

**Determiner:** a word that comes before a noun to show which one or how much. Articles are determiners. So are *this, my, some, every*. See [Parts of speech](/foundation/f1-parts-of-speech/).

**Vowel sound:** the sounds of *a, e, i, o, u* as in *apple, egg, ink, orange, umbrella*.

## The rules

### Rule 1: Use *a* or *an* before a singular countable noun when it is not a particular one, or when you mention it for the first time.

> *I bought **a** new pen.*
> *There is **an** owl in the tree.*
> *Karthik wants to be **an** engineer.*

*A / an* is **never** used with:
- **plural nouns**: *~~a books~~* → *some books* or *books*
- **uncountable nouns**: *~~an advice~~* → *some advice* or *a piece of advice*

### Rule 2: Choose *a* or *an* by the first sound, not the first letter.

- ***an*** before a **vowel sound**: *an apple, an egg, an idli, an orange, an umbrella*
- ***a*** before a **consonant sound**: *a book, a mango, a pen, a table*

The **letter** can trick you. Always listen to the **sound**.

| Starts with a vowel letter but a consonant sound → *a* | Starts with a consonant letter but a vowel sound → *an* |
|---|---|
| *a **u**niversity* (sounds "you") | *an **h**our* (*h* is silent: "our") |
| *a **u**niform*, *a **u**seful book* | *an **h**onest man*, *an **h**onour*, *an **h**eir* |
| *a **E**uropean* (sounds "you") | *an **M**LA* (sounds "em") |
| *a **o**ne-rupee coin* (sounds "won") | *an **M**BA*, *an **N**CC cadet*, *an **S**MS* |
| *a **o**nce-in-a-lifetime chance* | *an **F**IR*, *an **X**-ray*, *an **L**KG class* |
| *a **U**-turn*, *a **U**N officer* | *an **R**TO officer* (sounds "ar") |

For short forms (abbreviations) read aloud letter by letter, look at how the **first letter** sounds: *F* sounds "ef", so *an FIR*. *U* sounds "you", so *a UN officer*.

Words with a sounded *h* take *a*: *a house, a hotel, a hospital, a historic day*. (Older British English sometimes wrote *an hotel* or *an historic*. Today, *a* is standard.)

### Rule 3: Use *a / an* to mean "one", for jobs, for rates, and in exclamations.

> *I need **a** hundred rupees.* (= one hundred)
> *My mother is **a** teacher.* (a job: always needs *a / an*)
> *Petrol costs 100 rupees **a** litre.* (= per litre)
> *Take this tablet twice **a** day.*
> *What **a** beautiful kolam!* (exclamation with a singular countable noun)
> *It is such **a** hot day.* / *half **an** hour* / *quite **a** good film*

### Rule 4: Use *the* when the listener knows which one you mean.

The listener can know in three ways.

**(a) It was mentioned before:**

> *I saw **a** film last night. **The** film was about a farmer.*

**(b) A phrase or clause tells us which one:**

> ***The** book on the table is mine.*
> ***The** girl who won the prize is my cousin.*
> ***The** water in this well is sweet.*

**(c) It is clear from the situation:**

> *Please close **the** door.* (the door of this room)
> *Where is **the** principal?* (the principal of our school)

### Rule 5: Use *the* with things that are the only one of their kind.

> ***the** sun, **the** moon, **the** earth, **the** sky, **the** world, **the** universe, **the** equator, **the** internet*

> ***The** sun rises in the east.*

The directions also take *the*: ***the** east, **the** north, **the** south, **the** west*. (But not after *towards* + a direction used as an adverb: *We drove **north**.*)

### Rule 6: Use *the* with superlatives, ordinal numbers, and *only, same, next, last* (in a series).

> *Mount Everest is **the** highest mountain in the world.* (superlative)
> *Ravi lives on **the** second floor.* (ordinal number: first, second, third...)
> *Today is **the** first day of the exam.*
> *She is **the** only girl in the team.*
> *We study in **the** same class.*
> *This is **the** last bus to Madurai.*

### Rule 7: Use *the* with rivers, seas, oceans, canals, deserts, mountain ranges and groups of islands.

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Rivers | **the** Kaveri, **the** Ganga, **the** Vaigai, **the** Nile |
| Seas and oceans | **the** Arabian Sea, **the** Indian Ocean, **the** Bay of Bengal |
| Canals | **the** Suez Canal, **the** Buckingham Canal |
| Deserts | **the** Thar, **the** Sahara |
| Mountain ranges | **the** Himalayas, **the** Western Ghats, **the** Alps, **the** Nilgiris |
| Groups of islands | **the** Andaman and Nicobar Islands, **the** Maldives, **the** Lakshadweep Islands |

But **single** mountains and islands usually take **no article**: *Mount Everest, Mount Abu, Sri Lanka, Rameswaram*. Lakes whose name begins with *Lake* also take no article: *Lake Victoria*.

Be careful with *first* in races and exams: *Priya came **first** in the race* has no article, because *first* here works as an adverb (it tells how she came).

### Rule 8: Use *the* with country names that are plural or that contain words like *Republic, Kingdom, States, Union*.

> ***the** USA (**the** United States), **the** UK (**the** United Kingdom), **the** UAE, **the** Netherlands, **the** Philippines, **the** Republic of India*

Most other countries take **no article**: *India, Japan, France, Sri Lanka*. (See Rule 13.)

### Rule 9: Use *the* with newspapers, holy books, famous buildings, ships and named trains.

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Newspapers | ***The** Hindu, **The** Times of India, **The** Indian Express* |
| Holy books | ***the** Gita, **the** Bible, **the** Quran, **the** Ramayana, **the** Thirukkural* |
| Famous buildings and monuments | ***the** Taj Mahal, **the** Red Fort, **the** Gateway of India, **the** Meenakshi Temple* |
| Ships and named trains | ***the** Titanic, **the** Vaigai Express, **the** Pallavan Express* |

### Rule 10: Use *the* with musical instruments. Use no article with games.

> *Kavya plays **the** veena.* / *Arun plays **the** guitar.* / *She is learning **the** violin.*
> *Ravi plays **cricket**.* / *We play **kabaddi**.* / *They love **chess**.* (no article)

(American English often drops *the* with instruments: *She plays piano*. In exams, use the British form with *the*.)

### Rule 11: Use *the* + adjective for a group of people, and *the* + singular noun for a whole class.

**The + adjective = all the people of that kind.** The verb is **plural**.

> ***The** poor **need** our help.* (= poor people)
> ***The** rich, **the** old, **the** young, **the** sick, **the** blind, **the** unemployed*

**Nationalities** ending in *-sh, -ch, -ese* take *the*: ***the** English, **the** French, **the** Japanese, **the** Chinese*.

**The + singular countable noun** can mean the whole class or species:

> ***The** tiger is our national animal.* (= tigers as a species)
> ***The** computer has changed our lives.* (= computers in general)

### Rule 12: Use no article with meals, languages, games and school subjects in general.

> *We have **breakfast** at 7.* (not *~~the breakfast~~*)
> *She speaks **Tamil**, **English** and **Hindi**.*
> *They play **football** every evening.*
> ***Mathematics** is my favourite subject.*

But use an article when the noun is made particular, or has an adjective before it:

> ***The** lunch at the wedding was delicious.* (that particular lunch)
> *We had **a** heavy dinner.* (an adjective before a meal → *a*)
> ***The** Tamil language is very old.* (with the word *language*)

### Rule 13: Use no article with names of people, most countries, states, cities, continents, days, months and festivals.

> ***Priya** lives in **Coimbatore**.*
> ***India** is in **Asia**.*
> ***Tamil Nadu**, **Kerala**, **Chennai**, **Madurai***
> *on **Monday**, in **January**, at **Pongal**, for **Deepavali***

### Rule 14: Use no article with uncountable and abstract nouns, and plural nouns, when they mean something in general.

> ***Honesty** is the best policy.* (not *~~The honesty~~*)
> ***Milk** is good for children.*
> ***Gold** is a precious metal.*
> ***Mangoes** are sweet in summer.* (mangoes in general)
> ***Children** like sweets.*

When the meaning is **particular**, use *the*:

> ***The** honesty of this boy surprised everyone.* (his honesty)
> ***The** milk in this jug is sour.* (this milk)
> ***The** mangoes from Salem are famous.* (these particular mangoes)

### Rule 15: Use no article with *school, college, church, hospital, prison, bed* when we mean their main purpose. Use *the* when we mean the building.

| Main purpose (no article) | The building / place (*the*) |
|---|---|
| *Meena goes to **school** by bus.* (to study) | *Her father went to **the school** to meet the teacher.* (to visit the building) |
| *He was taken to **hospital**.* (as a patient) | *We went to **the hospital** to see him.* (as visitors) |
| *The thief was sent to **prison**.* (as a prisoner) | *The minister visited **the prison**.* |
| *It's late. Go to **bed**.* (to sleep) | *Don't sit on **the bed** with your shoes on.* (the piece of furniture) |
| *They go to **church** on Sundays.* (to pray) | *The church is very old.* |

(American English says *go to **the** hospital* even as a patient. British and Indian English use *go to hospital*.)

## Special cases

### 1. Fixed phrases with no article

Many common phrases have no article: *at home, at night, at noon, at midnight, by bus, by train, by car, on foot, go to bed, go to work, go home, at sea, in time, on time, by chance, by mistake, in fact*.

> *We travelled **by train**.* (but: *We were **on the** train*)
> *I walk to college **on foot**.*

Compare: ***in the** morning, **in the** evening, **in the** afternoon*, but ***at** night* (no article).

### 2. *The* with names that contain *of*

> ***the** Bay of Bengal, **the** University of Madras, **the** Cape of Good Hope, **the** Prime Minister of India*

### 3. A proper noun used as a common noun

When a famous name is used to mean "a person like that", it takes *the* or *a*.

> *Kalidasa is called **the** Shakespeare of India.*
> *He is **a** Sachin in the making.* (a great cricketer like Sachin)

### 4. Titles and positions

A title before a name has no article: ***Doctor** Ravi, **Professor** Meena, **Captain** Arun*. A position used alone usually takes *the*: ***the** President, **the** Chief Minister*.

After *became, was elected, was appointed*, a unique position often has no article: *He was elected **captain** of the team.* (*the captain* is also correct.)

### 5. Diseases

Most diseases take no article: *cancer, diabetes, malaria, typhoid*. Some common ones take *a* or *the*: ***a** cold, **a** headache, **a** fever, **the** flu*.

### 6. *The* with comparatives in double patterns

> ***The** more you read, **the** more you learn.*
> ***The** sooner, **the** better.*

### 7. *Few* and *a few*, *little* and *a little*

Adding *a* changes the meaning: *a few* = some; *few* = almost none. This is explained in [Determiners (Class 12)](/topics/t03-articles-determiners/class-12/).

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *I waited for ~~a~~ hour.* | *I waited for **an** hour.* | *Hour* begins with a vowel sound ("our"). The *h* is silent. |
| *She studies at ~~an~~ university.* | *She studies at **a** university.* | *University* begins with a consonant sound ("you"). |
| *He is ~~a~~ MLA.* | *He is **an** MLA.* | *M* is read as "em", a vowel sound. |
| *~~The~~ honesty is the best policy.* | ***Honesty** is the best policy.* | An abstract noun used in general takes no article. |
| *Kaveri is a long river.* | ***The** Kaveri is a long river.* | Rivers take *the*. |
| *He climbed ~~the~~ Mount Everest.* | *He climbed **Mount Everest**.* | A single mountain takes no article. Ranges (*the Himalayas*) take *the*. |
| *She plays ~~veena~~.* | *She plays **the** veena.* | Musical instruments take *the*. |
| *They play ~~the~~ cricket.* | *They play **cricket**.* | Games take no article. |
| *Can you give me ~~an~~ advice?* | *Can you give me **some** advice / **a piece of** advice?* | *Advice* is uncountable. It cannot take *a / an*. |
| *My father is ~~doctor~~.* | *My father is **a** doctor.* | A job after *be* needs *a / an*. |
| *Sun rises in ~~east~~.* | ***The** sun rises in **the** east.* | Unique things and directions take *the*. |
| *We have ~~the~~ lunch at one.* | *We have **lunch** at one.* | Meals take no article in general. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Fill in the blanks** with *a, an* or *the* (or put ✗ / *no article* where none is needed).
- **Spot the error** in a sentence that has a wrong or missing article.
- **Fill in a passage** with articles.

### Steps for "Fill in with articles"

1. **Look at the noun after the blank.** Is it singular, plural or uncountable? A name?
2. **Is it a particular one the listener knows?** (mentioned before, made clear by a phrase, unique, superlative, ordinal) → ***the***.
3. **Is it a name?** Check the lists: rivers, seas, ranges, newspapers, holy books → ***the***. People, most countries, cities, single mountains → **no article**.
4. **Is it general?** Plural or uncountable in general, meals, languages, games → **no article**.
5. **Is it one / any / a job?** Singular countable → ***a*** or ***an***. Choose by the **sound** of the next word (not always the noun: *an **old** man*).

### Worked example 1

> *My uncle is ______ honest man.*

1. Noun: *man* (singular, countable). The word right after the blank is *honest*.
2. Not a particular man; it describes my uncle → *a / an*.
3. *Honest* begins with a silent *h* → vowel sound ("on-est").

**Answer:** *My uncle is **an** honest man.*

### Worked example 2

> *______ Ganga is ______ longest river in India.*

1. First blank: *Ganga* is a river → *the*.
2. Second blank: *longest* is a superlative → *the*.

**Answer:** ***The** Ganga is **the** longest river in India.*

### Worked example 3

> *My sister goes to ______ college by ______ bus. Yesterday my father went to ______ college to pay her fees.*

1. First blank: she goes to study (main purpose) → **no article**.
2. Second blank: *by bus* is a fixed phrase → **no article**.
3. Third blank: my father went to the building (not to study) → ***the***.

**Answer:** *My sister goes to **college** by **bus**. Yesterday my father went to **the** college to pay her fees.*

### Worked example 4

> *I bought ______ umbrella and ______ uniform. ______ umbrella is red.*

1. *Umbrella*: first mention, singular. Sound "um" → vowel → ***an***.
2. *Uniform*: first mention, singular. Sound "you" → consonant → ***a***.
3. *Umbrella* again: now we know which one → ***the***.

**Answer:** *I bought **an** umbrella and **a** uniform. **The** umbrella is red.*

### Worked example 5

> *Spot the error: The rich should help poor.*

1. *Poor* is an adjective used for a group of people.
2. Adjective used as a noun for a group → needs ***the***.

**Answer:** *The rich should help **the poor**.*

## Quick check

1. *She is ______ European.*
2. *Arun reads ______ Hindu every morning.*
3. *______ Japanese are hard-working people.*
4. *We had ______ dinner at eight.*
5. *Kavya is ______ only child of her parents.*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***a.*** *European* begins with the sound "you", a consonant sound.
2. ***The.*** Newspapers take *the*. (Write it with a capital: *The Hindu*.)
3. ***The.*** *The* + a nationality ending in *-ese* means all the people of that country.
4. **No article.** Meals in general take no article. (*a delicious dinner* would need *a*, because of the adjective.)
5. ***the.*** *Only* takes *the*: there is only one such child.

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- ***a / an*** = one, any, first mention, a job. Choose by **sound**: *an hour, an MLA, a university, a one-rupee coin*.
- ***the*** = the listener knows which one: mentioned before, made clear, unique (*the sun*), superlative, ordinal, *only, same*.
- ***the*** with rivers, seas, oceans, ranges, island groups, deserts, newspapers, holy books, instruments, *the USA*, *the poor*.
- **No article** with names of people, most countries and cities, single mountains, meals, languages, games, subjects, and general plural, uncountable and abstract nouns.
- *go to school* (to study) / *go to **the** school* (the building).
:::
