## Part A: Choose the correct determiner

**1.** *______ students came to the meeting, so it was cancelled.* (Few / A few)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Few.*** The meeting was cancelled, so almost nobody came. *Few* = almost none (negative).

</details>

**2.** *I still have ______ rupees left. Let's buy some snacks.* (few / a few)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***a few.*** There is enough to buy snacks, so the meaning is positive. *A few* = some.

</details>

**3.** *There is ______ water in the bottle. You can drink it.* (little / a little)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***a little.*** *Water* is uncountable, and there is enough to drink → *a little* (= some).

</details>

**4.** *He showed ______ interest in the project, so we left him out.* (little / a little)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***little.*** He was left out, so he showed almost no interest. *Little* = hardly any (negative).

</details>

**5.** *Ravi spent ______ money he had on a gift for his mother.* (little / the little)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***the little.*** **Trap:** he had a small amount and spent **all** of it. *The little* = all of the small amount. *Little* alone would mean "almost no money", which does not fit *spent*.

</details>

**6.** *______ friends she has are very loyal to her.* (Few / The few)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The few.*** She has a small number of friends, and **all** of them are loyal. *The few* is followed by a clause (*she has*) that says which friends.

</details>

**7.** *How ______ chapters have you finished?* (much / many)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***many.*** *Chapters* is a plural countable noun → *many*.

</details>

**8.** *We don't have ______ time. Hurry up!* (much / many)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***much.*** *Time* is uncountable → *much*.

</details>

**9.** *She wore a gold bangle on ______ wrist.* (each / every)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***each.*** A person has **two** wrists. *Every* is not used for two things.

</details>

**10.** *Almost ______ house in our street has a kolam at the door during Pongal.* (each / every)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***every.*** **Trap:** *almost* and *nearly* can go with *every*, but not with *each*.

</details>

**11.** *You can sit on ______ of the two chairs.* (either / any)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***either.*** There are **two** chairs. *Either* = one or the other of two. (*Any* is used for three or more.)

</details>

**12.** *______ of the three answers is correct.* (Neither / None)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***None.*** There are **three** answers. *Neither* is only for two. For more than two, use *none*.

</details>

**13.** *______ my parents are teachers.* (Both / All)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Both.*** A person has two parents. *Both* is used for two.

</details>

**14.** *Would you like ______ sweets?* (some / any)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***some.*** **Trap:** it is a question, but it is an **offer**. Offers and requests use *some*.

</details>

**15.** *Is there ______ milk in the fridge?* (some / any)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***any.*** A normal question (not an offer) → *any*.

</details>

**16.** *There were ______ accidents on this road this year than last year.* (less / fewer)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***fewer.*** *Accidents* is countable → *fewer*. *Less* is for uncountable nouns.

</details>

## Part B: Fill in with a suitable determiner

**17.** *______ dog wagged ______ tail.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The*** ... ***its.*** *The dog* (a particular dog). *Its* is the possessive determiner (no apostrophe).

</details>

**18.** *I have told you ______ times not to touch the switch.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***several*** (or ***many***). *Times* is plural countable. *Several* = more than two, but not a very large number.

</details>

**19.** *Do we have ______ chairs for all the guests?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***enough.*** *Enough* before a noun = as many as we need.

</details>

**20.** *______ the students in my class have passed.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***All.*** *All* can come before *the*. The verb *have* is plural, so the subject is plural. (*Both* would need exactly two students.)

</details>

**21.** *Read ______ first two chapters before the test.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***the.*** Ordinal numbers (*first*) take *the*. Notice the order: *the first two*, not *the two first*.

</details>

**22.** *______ student has to wear the ID card.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Every*** (or ***Each***). The noun *student* is singular and the verb *has* is singular. *All* would need *students*.

</details>

**23.** *There is ______ sugar in the tin. We need to buy some.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***no.*** *We need to buy some* shows there is none. *No* = not any.

</details>

## Part C: Find and correct the mistake

**24.** *There are much people at the beach today.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*There are **many** people at the beach today.* *People* is a plural countable noun → *many*.

</details>

**25.** *Every of the girls received a gift.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Every one of** the girls* (or ***Each of** the girls*) *received a gift.* *Every* cannot be followed directly by *of*.

</details>

**26.** *Each of the players have a new bat.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Each of the players **has** a new bat.* *Each* takes a singular verb, even when it is followed by *of* + a plural noun.

</details>

**27.** *Both the four boys were punished.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***All four** boys* (or ***All the four** boys*) *were punished.* *Both* is used for two only.

</details>

**28.** *The two first rows are for parents.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***The first two** rows are for parents.* The ordinal number comes before the cardinal number.

</details>

**29.** *I have very less friends in this city.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*I have very **few** friends in this city.* *Friends* is countable → *few*. *Less* is for uncountable nouns.

</details>

**30.** *These kind of mangoes are expensive.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***This kind of mango is*** expensive, or ***These kinds of mangoes are*** expensive. The demonstrative must match *kind / kinds* in number.

</details>

**31.** *Neither of the shops were not open.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Neither of the shops **was** open.* *Neither* is already negative, so remove *not*. In formal English, *neither of* takes a singular verb (*were* is common in speech).

</details>
