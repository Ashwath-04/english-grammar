## Part A: Identify the non-finite verb and its type

**1.** *Reading novels is my favourite pastime.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Reading***: **gerund.** It is the subject of the sentence (it works as a noun). The finite verb is *is*.

</details>

**2.** *The children watched the setting sun.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***setting***: **present participle.** It describes the noun *sun* (the sun that is setting).

</details>

**3.** *She went to the library to borrow a book.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to borrow***: **to-infinitive** (of purpose). It tells **why** she went.

</details>

**4.** *We found a wounded bird in the garden.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***wounded***: **past participle.** It describes the bird, with a passive meaning (the bird had been wounded).

</details>

**5.** *Having locked the door, Amma left for the temple.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Having locked***: **perfect participle.** The locking was complete before she left.

</details>

**6.** *I bought new running shoes.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***running***: **gerund.** **Trap:** the shoes are not running. They are shoes **for** running. That makes it a gerund (a noun used in front of another noun), not a participle.

</details>

**7.** *Look at that running boy!*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***running***: **present participle.** The boy **is** running. It describes the boy. Compare with question 6.

</details>

**8.** *The manager made the workers wait outside.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***wait***: **bare infinitive.** After *make* (active), the infinitive has no *to*.

</details>

## Part B: Fill in with the correct form of the verb

**9.** *Kavya enjoys ______ (dance) in the rain.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***dancing.*** *Enjoy* is followed by a gerund.

</details>

**10.** *My father promised ______ (buy) me a bicycle.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to buy.*** *Promise* is followed by a *to*-infinitive.

</details>

**11.** *Would you mind ______ (lend) me your pen?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***lending.*** *Mind* is followed by a gerund.

</details>

**12.** *The thief denied ______ (steal) the purse.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***stealing*** (or ***having stolen***). *Deny* is followed by a gerund.

</details>

**13.** *He is very good at ______ (draw) cartoons.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***drawing.*** After a preposition (*at*), use a gerund.

</details>

**14.** *I am looking forward to ______ (see) you in Madurai.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***seeing.*** **Trap:** *to* here is a preposition, not part of an infinitive. So the verb takes the gerund form.

</details>

**15.** *Please let me ______ (explain).*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***explain.*** *Let* is followed by a bare infinitive.

</details>

**16.** *The students were made ______ (stand) in the sun.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to stand.*** **Trap:** *make* takes a bare infinitive in the active (*made them stand*), but *to* in the passive (*were made to stand*).

</details>

**17.** *Don't forget ______ (switch) off the fan when you leave.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to switch.*** *Forget* + *to*-infinitive = forget something you must do.

</details>

**18.** *I will never forget ______ (meet) my favourite cricketer.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***meeting.*** *Forget* + gerund = forget (or not forget) a past experience. The meeting already happened.

</details>

**19.** *On the way to Ooty, we stopped ______ (have) tea.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***to have.*** *Stop* + *to*-infinitive = stop (the journey) in order to do something. They stopped so that they could have tea.

</details>

**20.** *My grandfather stopped ______ (smoke) ten years ago.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***smoking.*** *Stop* + gerund = end an activity or habit. Compare with question 19.

</details>

**21.** *The fan is not working. Try ______ (press) the switch again.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***pressing.*** *Try* + gerund = do something as a test, to see if it works.

</details>

**22.** *I regret ______ (tell) him my secret. Now everyone knows.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***telling*** (or ***having told***). *Regret* + gerund = be sorry about a past action.

</details>

## Part C: Combine using a participle

**23.** *Arun heard the news. He was very happy.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Hearing the news**, Arun was very happy.* (or ***Having heard the news***.) Same subject; the first action becomes a participle clause.

</details>

**24.** *She completed her degree. She joined a bank.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Having completed her degree**, she joined a bank.* The degree had to be complete before she joined the bank → perfect participle.

</details>

**25.** *The letter was written in haste. It had many mistakes.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Written in haste**, the letter had many mistakes.* The letter received the action (passive) → past participle.

</details>

**26.** *Priya was tired after the long journey. She went to bed early.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Being tired after the long journey**, Priya went to bed early.* (or ***Tired after the long journey**, ...*.) The reason becomes a participle clause.

</details>

**27.** *The farmer saw the clouds. He hurried home.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Seeing the clouds**, the farmer hurried home.* The two actions are close together → present participle.

</details>

**28.** *Ravi had been warned about the traffic. He left home early.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Having been warned about the traffic**, Ravi left home early.* A completed passive action → *having been* + V3.

</details>

## Part D: Find and correct the mistake

**29.** *He suggested to go for a picnic.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*He suggested **going** for a picnic.* (or *He suggested **that we should go** for a picnic*.) *Suggest* is not followed by a *to*-infinitive.

</details>

**30.** *She is interesting in learning Hindi.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*She is **interested** in learning Hindi.* The person who has the feeling → *-ed* adjective.

</details>

**31.** *My teacher made me to read the poem again.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*My teacher made me **read** the poem again.* *Make* (active) + object + bare infinitive.

</details>

**32.** *Running to catch the bus, my bag fell down.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Running to catch the bus, **I dropped my bag**.* (or *As I was running to catch the bus, my bag fell down*.) This is a dangling participle: the bag was not running. The subject of the main clause must be the person who was running.

</details>

**33.** *We decided going to Kodaikanal for the holidays.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*We decided **to go** to Kodaikanal for the holidays.* *Decide* is followed by a *to*-infinitive.

</details>
