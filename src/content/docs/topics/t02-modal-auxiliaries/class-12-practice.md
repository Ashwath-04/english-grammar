## Part A: Fill in with the correct modal form

**1.** *The streets are wet. It ______ (rain) last night.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***must have rained.*** A strong conclusion about the past from evidence (wet streets) → *must have* + V3.

</details>

**2.** *Ravi was in Delhi on Monday. He ______ (attend) the meeting in Chennai that day.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***can't have attended*** (or ***couldn't have attended***). The speaker is sure it did **not** happen: he was in another city. Negative past deduction → *can't have* + V3.

</details>

**3.** *I can't find my umbrella. I ______ (leave) it on the bus.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***may have left*** (or ***might have left*** / ***could have left***). The speaker is not sure what happened → *may / might have* + V3.

</details>

**4.** *You ______ (tell) me about the test! I didn't prepare at all.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***should have told*** (or ***ought to have told***). The right action (telling me) was not done → *should have* + V3.

</details>

**5.** *We ______ (take) a taxi. The station was only five minutes away.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***needn't have taken.*** They took a taxi, but it was not necessary → *needn't have* + V3.

</details>

**6.** *Why did you climb that wall? You ______ (fall).*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***could have fallen*** (or ***might have fallen***). It was possible, but it did not happen.

</details>

**7.** *Kavya has been working since 6 a.m. She ______ (be) tired.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***must be.*** A strong conclusion about the **present** → *must* + V1. **Trap:** don't use *must have been*; she is tired now.

</details>

**8.** *That ______ (be) Arun at the door. He's in Salem this week.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***can't be.*** The speaker is sure it is **not** Arun → *can't* + V1. (*mustn't be* is not used for negative deduction in British English.)

</details>

**9.** *Don't ring the bell. The baby ______ (sleep).*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***may be sleeping*** (or ***might be sleeping***; ***must be sleeping*** if the speaker is sure). An action that is possibly going on now → modal + *be* + V4.

</details>

**10.** *You ______ wear a helmet on a two-wheeler. It's the law.* (must / have to)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***have to.*** The need comes from outside (the law), so *have to* is the better choice. (*must* is also accepted in most exams.)

</details>

**11.** *I ______ remember to call Grandma tonight. It's her birthday.* (must / have to)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***must.*** The speaker has decided this personally. *Must* shows the speaker's own feeling of need.

</details>

**12.** *You ______ pay now. You can pay at the end of the month.* (mustn't / don't have to)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***don't have to.*** **Trap:** paying now is not forbidden; it is only not necessary. *Mustn't* would mean "you are not allowed to pay now".

</details>

**13.** *You ______ park here. It's the hospital entrance.* (mustn't / don't have to)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***mustn't.*** Parking at a hospital entrance is not allowed → prohibition.

</details>

**14.** *The bus ______ arrive at 9, but it came at 10.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***was supposed to.*** What was expected (the timetable) did not happen. *Was supposed to* often shows this.

</details>

**15.** *______ I finish the whole chapter today?* (Need / Needs)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Need.*** In a question, *need* can be a modal: no *-s*, and V1 without *to* after the subject. (*Do I need to finish...?* is also correct.)

</details>

**16.** *How ______ you speak to your teacher like that!*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***dare.*** *How dare you...!* is a fixed expression that shows anger. Here *dare* is a modal: no *to* after it.

</details>

## Part B: Rewrite using a modal

**17.** *I'm sure Divya has reached home by now.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Divya **must have reached** home by now.* *I'm sure* + past → *must have* + V3.

</details>

**18.** *It is impossible that he wrote this essay himself.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*He **can't have written** this essay himself.* (or *couldn't have written*.) Sure it did not happen → *can't have* + V3.

</details>

**19.** *Perhaps the shop was closed.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The shop **may have been** closed.* (or *might have been* / *could have been*.) *Perhaps* + past → *may have* + V3. *Was* → *have been*.

</details>

**20.** *It was a mistake for us not to bring a map.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*We **should have brought** a map.* (or *ought to have brought*.) The right action was not done → *should have* + V3.

</details>

**21.** *It was not necessary for you to wait for me, but you did.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You **needn't have waited** for me.* Done, but not necessary → *needn't have* + V3.

</details>

**22.** *I'm sure she is at the library.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*She **must be** at the library.* *I'm sure* + present → *must* + V1.

</details>

**23.** *You had the chance to win, but you didn't try.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You **could have won**.* Possible in the past, but it did not happen → *could have* + V3.

</details>

## Part C: Identify the function of the modal

**24.** *You **shouldn't have eaten** so much.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Criticism of a past action.** You did eat a lot, and it was wrong.

</details>

**25.** *She **must have missed** the train.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Deduction about the past** (the speaker is almost sure). It is not obligation.

</details>

**26.** *I **needn't have bought** so much rice.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**An unnecessary past action.** I bought the rice, but it was not necessary.

</details>

## Part D: Find and correct the mistake

**27.** *He must has forgotten the address.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*He must **have** forgotten the address.* After a modal, always *have*, never *has*.

</details>

**28.** *You should have came earlier.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You should have **come** earlier.* Modal + *have* + **V3**. *Come – came – come*.

</details>

**29.** *I am used to get up at five.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*I am used to **getting** up at five.* *Be used to* (= accustomed to) is followed by V4. (If the meaning is a past habit, write *I **used to get** up at five*.)

</details>

**30.** *My father need to rest after his operation.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*My father **needs** to rest after his operation.* In a positive sentence, *need* is an ordinary verb: it takes *-s* and *to*.

</details>

**31.** *She mustn't have seen us. It was very dark.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*She **can't have** seen us.* (or *couldn't have seen*.) The negative of past deduction is *can't have*, not *mustn't have*.

</details>

**32.** *Yesterday I must go to the doctor.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Yesterday I **had to** go to the doctor.* *Must* has no past form for obligation. Use *had to*. (*must have gone* would mean "I'm sure someone went", which is a different meaning.)

</details>
