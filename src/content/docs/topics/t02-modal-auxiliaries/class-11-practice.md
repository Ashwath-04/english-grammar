## Part A: Fill in with a suitable modal

**1.** *Arun ______ swim across the river when he was ten.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***could*** (or ***was able to***). A general ability in the past.

</details>

**2.** *The exam was very difficult, but Priya ______ answer all the questions.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***was able to*** (or ***managed to***). **Trap:** this is one particular success in the past, not a general ability. For a single past success, use *was able to*, not *could*.

</details>

**3.** *______ I come in, madam?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***May*** (or ***Could*** / ***Can***). Asking for permission. *May* is the most formal and the best choice when speaking to a teacher.

</details>

**4.** *Take your raincoat. It ______ rain this evening.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***may*** (or ***might*** / ***could***). Possibility: the speaker is not sure it will rain.

</details>

**5.** *All students ______ wear their ID cards inside the campus.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***must*** (or ***have to***). A rule → obligation.

</details>

**6.** *You ______ smoke at a petrol bunk. It is very dangerous.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***must not*** (*mustn't*). It is not allowed → prohibition.

</details>

**7.** *You ______ buy a new dictionary. You can use my old one.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***need not*** (or ***don't have to*** / ***don't need to***). **Trap:** it is not forbidden to buy one; it is only not necessary. So *must not* is wrong here.

</details>

**8.** *You look pale. You ______ see a doctor.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***should*** (or ***ought to*** / ***had better***). Advice. *Had better* adds a warning: something bad may happen if you don't.

</details>

**9.** *______ you please close the window? It's cold.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Would*** (or ***Could*** / ***Will*** / ***Can***). A request. *Would* and *could* are the most polite.

</details>

**10.** *______ we go to Marina Beach this Sunday?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Shall.*** A suggestion. *Shall we...?* is the usual way to suggest doing something together.

</details>

**11.** *I ______ live in Trichy when I was in primary school.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***used to.*** **Trap:** *live* here is a past **state**, not a repeated action. *Would* cannot be used for past states. Only *used to* works.

</details>

**12.** *Last year, my mother ______ go to the hospital every week for treatment.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***had to.*** Obligation in the past. *Must* has no past form, so use *had to*.

</details>

**13.** *After six months of training, you ______ drive a car confidently.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***will be able to.*** A future ability that does not exist yet. *Will* and *can* cannot go together, so use *will be able to*.

</details>

**14.** *You ______ respect your elders.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***ought to*** (or ***should***). A moral duty. *Ought to* is often used for duties like this.

</details>

## Part B: Identify the function of the modal

**15.** *Kavya **can** solve this sum in a minute.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ability.** *Can* shows that she has the skill.

</details>

**16.** *You **may** take one sweet each.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Permission.** The speaker is allowing the listeners to take a sweet.

</details>

**17.** *Ravi **might** be at the temple now.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Possibility.** The speaker is not sure where Ravi is.

</details>

**18.** *You **mustn't** touch those wires.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Prohibition.** It is not allowed (and dangerous).

</details>

**19.** ***Could** you lend me your notes?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Request** (a polite request). It is not past ability, because it asks someone to do something now.

</details>

**20.** *I **will** finish this project on time, whatever happens.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Determination.** *Whatever happens* shows a strong decision.

</details>

**21.** *Every Sunday, Grandpa **would** take us to the market.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Past habit.** A repeated action in the past (*every Sunday*).

</details>

**22.** *You **may not** leave the hall before 11 a.m.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Prohibition (refusal of permission).** **Trap:** *may not* can also show possibility (*He may not come* = perhaps he won't come). Here it is a rule in an exam hall, so it means "you are not allowed".

</details>

## Part C: Rewrite using a modal

**23.** *It is possible that the train is late.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The train **may be** late.* (or *might be* / *could be*.) *It is possible that* → possibility → *may*. *Is* becomes V1 *be*.

</details>

**24.** *It is compulsory for all drivers to wear seat belts.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*All drivers **must wear** seat belts.* (or *have to wear*.) *Compulsory* → obligation → *must*.

</details>

**25.** *I advise you to rest for a few days.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You **should rest** for a few days.* (or *ought to rest* / *had better rest*.) *I advise you* → advice → *should*.

</details>

**26.** *It is not necessary for you to come early.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You **need not come** early.* (or *don't have to come* / *don't need to come*.) *Not necessary* → lack of necessity.

</details>

**27.** *Ravi knows how to play the mridangam.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Ravi **can play** the mridangam.* *Knows how to* → ability → *can*.

</details>

**28.** *Please allow me to go home early.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***May I go** home early?* (or *Could I* / *Can I*.) Asking for permission → *may*. The sentence becomes a question.

</details>

**29.** *Smoking is not allowed here.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You **must not smoke** here.* (or *You **cannot smoke** here*.) *Not allowed* → prohibition.

</details>

## Part D: Find and correct the mistake

**30.** *He can able to lift that heavy box.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*He **can lift** that heavy box.* (or *He **is able to** lift...*). Use either *can* or *be able to*, not both together.

</details>

**31.** *You need not to worry about the fees.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You **need not worry** about the fees.* (or *You **don't need to** worry...*). The modal *need not* is followed by V1 without *to*.

</details>

**32.** *You had better to leave now.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You had better **leave** now.* *Had better* is followed by V1, without *to*.

</details>

**33.** *Did you used to play kabaddi in school?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Did you **use to** play kabaddi in school?* After *did*, the verb goes back to V1: *use to*, not *used to*.

</details>
