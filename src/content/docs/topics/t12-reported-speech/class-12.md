## What you will learn

- How to report **mixed sentences** (a statement + a question + a command in one speech)
- How to turn a **dialogue** (a conversation) into a smooth **reported paragraph**
- How to choose a **precise reporting verb**: *suggested, apologised, promised, refused, admitted, warned, congratulated, thanked* and more, each with its correct pattern
- How to report ***Let's ...*** (*suggested / proposed that we should ...*)
- How to change **indirect speech back into direct speech**

## Why this matters

Real speech is rarely one neat sentence. People mix statements, questions and requests: *"I'm sorry I'm late. Has the class started? Please let me in."* Class 12 exam questions are like this.

Reporting a dialogue also tests writing skill. A good answer does not repeat *said ... said ... said*. It uses verbs like *apologised, asked, requested, replied, agreed*, which show the speaker's purpose clearly.

## The idea in simple words

Look at this short speech:

> *Ravi said to the teacher, "I'm sorry I'm late. May I come in?"*

It has two parts: an **apology** (a statement) and a **question** (asking for permission). Each part needs its own reporting verb.

> *Ravi **apologised** to the teacher for being late **and asked** if he might come in.*

Notice:
- *I'm sorry* became the verb ***apologised***. We report the **purpose** of the words, not each word.
- The two parts are joined with ***and***, and each has its own verb (*apologised*, *asked*).

## Grammar words you need

**Mixed sentence:** a piece of direct speech that contains more than one kind of sentence (for example, a statement and a question).

**Dialogue:** a conversation between two or more people, written as direct speech.

**Precise reporting verb:** a verb that shows the speaker's purpose: *suggested, apologised, promised, refused, warned, admitted...*

**Gerund (V4):** the *-ing* form used as a noun: *apologised for **being** late*. See [Non-finite verbs](/topics/t07-non-finite-verbs/class-12/).

**To-infinitive:** *to* + V1: *promised **to come***.

## The rules

### Rule 1: In a mixed sentence, report each part with its own reporting verb, and join the parts with *and*.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *Arun said, "I am tired. Can you help me?"* | *Arun **said that** he was tired **and asked** me **if** I could help him.* |
| *The teacher said, "Sit down. Why are you late?"* | *The teacher **told** me **to sit** down **and asked why** I was late.* |
| *She said, "Hurray! We have won. Let's celebrate."* | *She **exclaimed with joy that** they had won **and suggested that** they **should** celebrate.* |
| *He said to me, "Where were you? I waited for an hour."* | *He **asked** me **where** I had been **and said** (or ***added***) **that** he had waited for an hour.* |
| *Amma said, "It's late. Go to bed."* | *Amma **said that** it was late **and told** me **to go** to bed.* |

Useful joining words:

| Join with | When |
|---|---|
| *and* + new reporting verb | most mixed sentences |
| *and added that* | a second statement after the first |
| *but* | the second part contrasts with the first |
| *and then* | the parts happen in order |

Do not repeat the same reporting verb: *~~He said that he was tired and said that ...~~*. Use *added*, *explained* or *went on to say*.

### Rule 2: Report the purpose, not every word.

Greetings, *yes / no*, *please*, *sorry*, *thank you* and names of people being addressed are turned into the reporting verb, or left out.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *"Thank you for the gift," she said to me.* | *She **thanked** me **for** the gift.* |
| *"I'm sorry I broke the glass," said Ravi.* | *Ravi **apologised for** breaking the glass.* |
| *"Congratulations on your success!" said Grandpa.* | *Grandpa **congratulated** me **on** my success.* |
| *"Yes, I will help you," he said.* | *He **agreed to** help me.* |
| *"No, I won't go," she said.* | *She **refused to** go.* |
| *"Sir, may I go out?" said the boy.* | *The boy **respectfully asked** the teacher **if** he might go out.* |

Words like *sir, madam* can become *respectfully*. *Friends* or a name becomes the object of the reporting verb.

### Rule 3: Choose a precise reporting verb, and use its correct pattern.

Each verb is followed by its own pattern. Using the wrong pattern is the most common error.

| Reporting verb | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***suggested*** | *that* + subject + *should* + V1 / + V4 | *He suggested **that we should go** by train.* / *He suggested **going** by train.* |
| ***apologised*** | (*to* + person) + *for* + V4 / noun | *She apologised **for being** late.* |
| ***promised*** | *to* + V1 / *that* + *would* | *He promised **to return** the book.* |
| ***refused*** | *to* + V1 | *The boy refused **to eat** his lunch.* |
| ***agreed*** | *to* + V1 / *that* | *She agreed **to help** us.* |
| ***offered*** | *to* + V1 | *Arun offered **to carry** my bag.* |
| ***threatened*** | *to* + V1 | *The man threatened **to call** the police.* |
| ***admitted*** | V4 / *that* | *He admitted **stealing** the money.* / *admitted **that** he had stolen it* |
| ***denied*** | V4 / *that* | *She denied **breaking** the vase.* |
| ***warned*** | person + (*not*) *to* + V1 / *that* | *He warned me **not to touch** the wire.* |
| ***advised*** | person + (*not*) *to* + V1 | *The doctor advised me **to rest**.* |
| ***reminded*** | person + *to* + V1 / *that* | *She reminded me **to lock** the door.* |
| ***invited*** | person + *to* + V1 / *to* + noun | *They invited us **to stay** for dinner.* |
| ***congratulated*** | person + *on* + noun / V4 | *He congratulated me **on winning** the prize.* |
| ***thanked*** | person + *for* + noun / V4 | *She thanked us **for helping** her.* |
| ***accused*** | person + *of* + V4 | *He accused me **of lying**.* |
| ***insisted*** | *on* + V4 / *that* | *She insisted **on paying** the bill.* |
| ***complained*** | *that* / *about* + noun | *He complained **that** the food was cold.* |
| ***boasted*** | *that* / *about* + V4 | *He boasted **that** he was the best player.* |
| ***informed / assured*** | person + *that* | *She informed us **that** the bus was late.* |
| ***explained*** | *that* / (*to* + person) + wh- | *He explained **that** the shop was closed.* |
| ***replied / answered*** | *that* | *She replied **that** she was fine.* |

Common pattern errors:

> *~~He suggested me to go.~~* ✘ → *He suggested **that I should go**.* ✔ / *He suggested **(my) going**.* ✔
> *~~She apologised to be late.~~* ✘ → *She apologised **for being** late.* ✔
> *~~He promised me coming.~~* ✘ → *He promised **to come**.* ✔
> *~~They congratulated me for my success.~~* → *congratulated me **on** my success* ✔

### Rule 4: Report *Let's* with *suggested* or *proposed that ... should*.

*Let's* (= *let us*) makes a **suggestion** for the speaker and the listener together.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *He said, "Let's go to the beach."* | *He **suggested that they should go** to the beach.* / *He **suggested going** to the beach.* |
| *She said to me, "Let's have lunch together."* | *She **suggested to me that we should have** lunch together.* / *She **proposed that we should have** lunch together.* |
| *Ravi said, "Let's not waste time."* | *Ravi **suggested that they should not waste** time.* |

Choose the pronoun (*we* or *they*) by who is reporting:
- If the reporter was part of the group → ***we***: *He suggested that **we** should go* (I was there too).
- If not → ***they***: *He suggested to his friends that **they** should go*.

**Let me / Let him** (asking permission, not a suggestion) is reported differently:

> *"Let me go home early," said the clerk.* → *The clerk **requested to be allowed to** go home early.* / *... **asked if he could** go home early.*

### Rule 5: Report questions with *Shall I / Shall we / Will you* by their purpose.

| Direct | Purpose | Indirect |
|---|---|---|
| *"Shall I open the window?" he said.* | an **offer** | *He **offered to open** the window.* |
| *"Shall we start?" she said.* | a **suggestion** | *She **suggested that they should** start.* |
| *"Will you pass the salt?" he said to me.* | a **request** | *He **requested** (or *asked*) **me to pass** the salt.* |
| *"Shall I wait for you?" she asked.* | asking for **advice / instructions** | *She **asked if she should** wait for me.* |

### Rule 6: To report a dialogue, write a connected paragraph with varied verbs and linkers.

Follow these steps:

1. **Name the speakers** clearly at the start (*Ravi asked Priya ...*). Use pronouns after that, but only if they are clear.
2. **Find the purpose** of each speech: question, answer, request, offer, apology, agreement, refusal.
3. **Choose a precise reporting verb** for each one.
4. **Join** the sentences with linkers: *and, but, so, then, when, after that*.
5. **Change tenses, pronouns, time and place** as usual.
6. **Leave out** small words like *Oh, Well, OK* (or show their meaning: *agreed*).

**Example dialogue:**

> *Customer: "How much does this shirt cost?"*
> *Shopkeeper: "It costs five hundred rupees."*
> *Customer: "That's too expensive. Can you give me a discount?"*
> *Shopkeeper: "Sorry, the price is fixed."*

**Reported paragraph:**

> *The customer **asked** the shopkeeper how much that shirt cost. The shopkeeper **replied that** it cost five hundred rupees. The customer **said that** it was too expensive **and asked** if he could give him a discount. The shopkeeper **apologised and said that** the price was fixed.*

(*Cost* stays *cost* because the past and present forms are the same: *cost – cost – cost*.)

### Rule 7: To change indirect into direct speech, reverse every change.

1. **Reporting verb:** *told* + person → *said to* + person. *Asked* → *said* (or *asked*). *Ordered / requested* → *said to*.
2. **Quotation marks:** put the reported words inside " ". Add a comma before them.
3. **Sentence type:** restore the question order and the question mark; restore the command (*Sit down.*); restore *Please* for *requested*; restore the interjection (*Hurray!*, *Alas!*) for *exclaimed with joy / sorrow*.
4. **Tense:** move one step forward again (*was → is, had seen → saw / have seen, would → will, could → can*).
5. **Pronouns:** the speaker → *I / we*; the listener → *you*.
6. **Time and place:** *then → now, that day → today, the next day → tomorrow, there → here, that → this*.

| Indirect | Direct |
|---|---|
| *She told me that she was busy that day.* | *She said to me, "I am busy today."* |
| *He asked me if I had finished my work.* | *He said to me, "Have you finished your work?"* |
| *The teacher ordered the boys not to run.* | *The teacher said to the boys, "Don't run."* |
| *She requested me to help her.* | *She said to me, "Please help me."* |
| *He exclaimed with joy that he had passed.* | *He said, "Hurray! I have passed."* |
| *He suggested that they should go to the cinema.* | *He said, "Let's go to the cinema."* |
| *She apologised for being late.* | *She said, "I'm sorry I'm late."* |

When the original tense is not clear (*had seen* could come from *saw* or *have seen*), choose the one that fits the time words.

## Special cases

### 1. Questions within statements

> *He said, "I don't know where she lives."*
> → *He said that he didn't know where she lived.* (The question inside is already in statement order.)

### 2. Exclamations with a question form

> *"Isn't it a beautiful day!" she said.* → *She **exclaimed that** it was a beautiful day.*

It looks like a question, but it is an exclamation. Report the meaning.

### 3. Reporting *must*, *should* and *ought to*

> *"You should apologise," he said to me.* → *He **advised** me **to apologise**.* (or *He said that I should apologise.*)
> *"You must finish this today," said the manager.* → *The manager said that I **had to** finish it that day.* / *The manager **told** me **to finish** it that day.*

### 4. Vocatives and question tags

Leave out question tags; report the meaning:

> *"You're coming, aren't you?" he said.* → *He **asked** me **if** I was coming.* (or *He wanted to confirm that I was coming.*)

### 5. Reporting in the present tense

When a dialogue is reported "live" (for example, a teacher reading a play aloud), the reporting verb can be present: *Ravi **asks** Priya if she **is** ready.* The tenses then do not change. Most exam answers use the past.

### 6. *Said* with an adverb for tone

To show how something was said, add an adverb or a verb of manner: *shouted, whispered, cried, angrily said, politely asked, sadly replied*.

> *"Get out!" he shouted.* → *He **angrily ordered** me **to get out**.*

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *He said that he was tired ~~and he said that~~ could I help him.* | *He said that he was tired **and asked** if I could help him.* | Each part needs its **own** reporting verb, and a reported question uses statement order with *if*. |
| *He suggested ~~me to go~~.* | *He suggested **that I should go**.* / *suggested **going*** | *Suggest* is not followed by person + *to*. |
| *She apologised ~~to be~~ late.* | *She apologised **for being** late.* | *Apologise* takes *for* + V4. |
| *They congratulated him ~~for~~ his victory.* | *They congratulated him **on** his victory.* | *Congratulate* takes *on*. |
| *He refused ~~that he would~~ come.* | *He refused **to come**.* | *Refuse* takes a *to*-infinitive. |
| *He promised ~~me coming~~.* | *He promised **to come**.* / *He promised me that he would come.* | *Promise* takes *to* + V1 or a *that* clause. |
| *She suggested ~~let's~~ go out.* | *She suggested **that we should go** out.* | *Let's* is not kept in reported speech. |
| *He thanked me ~~to help~~ him.* | *He thanked me **for helping** him.* | *Thank* takes *for* + V4. |
| *The boy said sorry ~~that~~ he broke the glass.* | *The boy **apologised for breaking** the glass.* | Report the purpose with a precise verb. |
| *She asked ~~shall I~~ open the door.* | *She **offered to open** the door.* | *Shall I ...?* (an offer) → *offered to*. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Convert** direct speech (often mixed sentences) into indirect speech.
- **Report a dialogue** as a paragraph.
- **Fill in** with suitable reporting verbs.
- **Convert** indirect speech into direct speech.

### Steps for a mixed sentence

1. **Split** the speech into its parts (statement / question / command / exclamation / wish).
2. **Choose a reporting verb** for each part (Rule 3).
3. **Report each part** with the Class 11 changes (tense, pronoun, time, word order).
4. **Join** the parts with *and*, *and added that*, *but* or *and then*.
5. **Read it** to check it flows and does not repeat the same verb.

### Worked example 1

> *The teacher said to Kavya, "You have done well. Keep it up."*

1. Parts: statement (*You have done well*) + command / encouragement (*Keep it up*).
2. Verbs: *told* (statement) + *told / encouraged* (command).
3. *You have done well* → *she had done well*. *Keep it up* → *to keep it up*.
4. Join with *and*.

**Answer:** *The teacher **told** Kavya **that** she had done well **and encouraged** her **to keep** it up.*

### Worked example 2

> *Arun said, "Let's go for a movie tonight. I will buy the tickets."*

1. Parts: suggestion (*Let's ...*) + promise / offer (*I will buy ...*).
2. Verbs: *suggested that ... should* + *offered to*.
3. *tonight → that night*.

**Answer:** *Arun **suggested that they should go** for a movie **that night and offered to buy** the tickets.* (If the reporter was in the group: *suggested that **we** should go*.)

### Worked example 3

> *Meena said to her mother, "I'm sorry, Amma. I forgot to buy the milk. Shall I go now?"*

1. Parts: apology + statement + offer.
2. Verbs: *apologised* + (*explained / said*) + *offered*.
3. *I forgot → she had forgotten*. *Shall I go now?* (offer) → *offered to go then*.

**Answer:** *Meena **apologised to** her mother **and explained that** she **had forgotten** to buy the milk. She **offered to go then**.* (or *... and offered to go at once.*)

### Worked example 4 (dialogue)

> *Ravi: "Priya, can you lend me your bicycle?"*
> *Priya: "Yes, of course. But please bring it back by five."*
> *Ravi: "I promise I will."*

1. Ravi's speech: a request → *asked Priya if she could lend him her bicycle*.
2. Priya's speech: agreement + request → *agreed and requested him to bring it back by five*.
3. Ravi's speech: promise → *promised that he would*.

**Answer:** *Ravi **asked** Priya **if she could lend him her** bicycle. Priya **readily agreed, but requested** him **to bring** it back by five. Ravi **promised that he would**.*

### Worked example 5 (indirect → direct)

> *The old man asked the boy where he lived and told him to go home quickly.*

1. Two parts: a wh- question + a command.
2. Question: *where he lived* → *"Where do you live?"* (*lived → live*, add *do*, question order).
3. Command: *told him to go home quickly* → *"Go home quickly."*
4. Pronouns: *he* (the boy, the listener) → *you*.

**Answer:** *The old man said to the boy, "**Where do you live? Go home quickly.**"*

## Quick check

1. *"Thank you for coming," said the host to the guests.*
2. *"I didn't break the window," said the boy.* (use *denied*)
3. *"Let's play chess," said Karthik to his sister.*
4. *"Will you please close the door?" said the teacher to Ravi.*
5. Change into direct speech: *She apologised for being late and asked if the class had started.*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***The host thanked the guests for coming.*** *Thank you* → *thanked* + person + *for* + V4.
2. ***The boy denied breaking (or having broken) the window.*** (or *The boy denied that he had broken the window.*) *Deny* + V4 or *that* clause.
3. ***Karthik suggested to his sister that they should play chess.*** (or *Karthik suggested playing chess.*) *Let's* → *suggested that ... should*.
4. ***The teacher requested Ravi to close the door.*** *Will you please ...?* is a request → *requested* + person + *to*.
5. ***She said, "I'm sorry I'm late. Has the class started?"*** *Apologised for being late* → *I'm sorry I'm late*. *Asked if the class had started* → *Has the class started?*

</details>

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- **Mixed sentences:** one reporting verb per part; join with *and / and added that / but*.
- **Report the purpose:** *Thank you* → *thanked for*; *Sorry* → *apologised for*; *Yes, I will* → *agreed to*; *No* → *refused to*.
- **Learn the patterns:** *suggested that ... should / + V4* · *apologised for + V4* · *promised / refused / offered / agreed + to* · *admitted / denied + V4* · *congratulated on* · *thanked for* · *accused of* · *warned (not) to*.
- ***Let's*** → *suggested / proposed that we / they should*.
- *Shall I ...?* → *offered to*. *Shall we ...?* → *suggested*. *Will you ...?* → *requested*.
- **Indirect → direct:** reverse every change, restore quotation marks, question order, *please* and interjections.
:::
