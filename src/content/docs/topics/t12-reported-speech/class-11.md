## What you will learn

- The difference between **direct** and **indirect (reported) speech**, and the names of the parts: **reporting verb** and **reported speech**
- How to report **statements**: *said → said that*; *said to + person → told + person + that*
- The **tense changes** when the reporting verb is in the past (*is → was, will → would, can → could...*)
- When the tense does **not** change
- How to change **pronouns**, and **words of time and place** (*now → then, tomorrow → the next day, here → there*)
- How to report **questions** (*asked if / whether*, wh- words), **commands and requests** (*told / ordered / requested ... to*), and **exclamations and wishes**

## Why this matters

We report what other people say all the time: *Amma said that dinner was ready.* *The teacher told us to bring our notebooks.* In stories and news reports, almost all speech is reported.

In the exam, you will change direct speech into indirect (and the reverse), report short dialogues, and fill in reporting verbs. The tense changes depend on what you learnt in [Tenses (Class 11)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-11/) and the sequence of tenses in [Tenses (Class 12)](/topics/t01-tenses/class-12/).

## The idea in simple words

Yesterday, Ravi said to you: *"I am tired."*

Today you tell a friend what Ravi said. You could repeat his exact words:

> *Ravi said, "I am tired."*

This is **direct speech**. The exact words are inside quotation marks (" ").

Or you could report the meaning in your own words:

> *Ravi said that **he was** tired.*

This is **indirect speech** (also called **reported speech**). There are no quotation marks. Some words have changed:

- *I* became *he*, because you are talking about Ravi.
- *am* became *was*, because Ravi said it in the past.

All of reported speech is about making changes like these, in a fixed and logical way.

## Grammar words you need

**Direct speech:** the exact words of the speaker, inside quotation marks. *She said, **"I like mangoes."***

**Indirect speech (reported speech):** the speaker's words reported without quotation marks, with changes. *She said **that she liked mangoes**.*

**Reporting verb:** the verb that introduces the speech: ***said**, **told**, **asked**, **ordered***.

**Reported speech (reported clause):** the words that are reported. In *She said, "I like mangoes,"* the reported speech is *I like mangoes*.

**Backshift:** moving the tense one step back into the past (*is → was*, *has → had*).

**Narration:** another name for direct and indirect speech in older grammar books.

## The rules

### Rule 1: Report a statement with *said that* or *told + person + that*.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *Meena **said**, "I am busy."* | *Meena **said that** she was busy.* |
| *Meena **said to me**, "I am busy."* | *Meena **told me that** she was busy.* |

- *said* (no listener) → ***said that***
- *said to* + listener → ***told*** + listener + ***that***

> *~~She said me that...~~* ✘ / *~~She told that...~~* ✘
> *She **said that**...* ✔ / *She **told me that**...* ✔ / *She **said to me that**...* ✔ (correct, but less common)

*That* can be left out in informal English: *She said she was busy.* In exams, include it.

**Punctuation:** remove the comma and the quotation marks. The full stop goes at the end.

### Rule 2: When the reporting verb is in the past, move the tense one step back.

| Direct | Indirect | Example (direct → indirect) |
|---|---|---|
| present simple | past simple | *"I **live** in Salem."* → *he **lived** in Salem* |
| present continuous | past continuous | *"I **am reading**."* → *she **was reading*** |
| present perfect | past perfect | *"I **have finished**."* → *he **had finished*** |
| present perfect continuous | past perfect continuous | *"I **have been waiting**."* → *she **had been waiting*** |
| past simple | past perfect | *"I **saw** the film."* → *he **had seen** the film* |
| past continuous | past perfect continuous | *"I **was sleeping**."* → *she **had been sleeping*** |
| past perfect | no change | *"I **had left**."* → *he **had left*** |
| *will* | *would* | *"I **will** come."* → *she **would** come* |
| *shall* | *should* (or *would*) | *"I **shall** go."* → *he **should / would** go* |
| *can* | *could* | *"I **can** swim."* → *he **could** swim* |
| *may* | *might* | *"I **may** be late."* → *she **might** be late* |
| *must* | *had to* (or *must*) | *"I **must** leave."* → *he **had to** leave* |
| *could, would, should, might, ought to* | no change | *"You **should** rest."* → *I **should** rest* |

The past simple can stay as it is when the order of events is clear: *He said, "I **saw** her yesterday."* → *He said that he **had seen** her the day before.* (*he saw her* is also accepted in many textbooks.)

*Must* stays *must* when it is a deduction, a permanent rule, or advice: *"You must be tired."* → *He said that I **must** be tired.*

### Rule 3: The tense does not change when the reporting verb is in the present or future.

> *Kavya **says**, "I am happy."* → *Kavya **says** that she **is** happy.*
> *He **will say**, "I am ready."* → *He **will say** that he **is** ready.*
> *The teacher **has said**, "The test is on Monday."* → *The teacher **has said** that the test **is** on Monday.*

### Rule 4: The tense does not change for universal truths and facts that are still true.

> *The teacher said, "The earth **goes** round the sun."*
> → *The teacher said that the earth **goes** round the sun.* (a universal truth)

> *She said, "Water **boils** at 100 °C."* → *She said that water **boils** at 100 °C.*

For **habits and facts that are still true**, the change is optional:

> *He said, "I **go** for a walk every morning."*
> → *He said that he **goes** / **went** for a walk every morning.* (both correct; *goes* shows it is still true)

### Rule 5: Change the pronouns to match who is speaking and who is listening.

Think about **who** each pronoun refers to. Then use the right pronoun for the person reporting.

| In the direct speech | Changes to |
|---|---|
| **First person** (*I, me, my, we, us, our*) | the person of the **subject** of the reporting verb (the speaker) |
| **Second person** (*you, your*) | the person of the **object** of the reporting verb (the listener) |
| **Third person** (*he, she, it, they*) | **no change** |

> *Ravi said to Priya, "**I** will help **you**."*
> → *Ravi told Priya that **he** would help **her**.*
> (*I* = Ravi → *he*; *you* = Priya → *her*)

> *You said to me, "**I** like **your** dress."*
> → *You told me that **you** liked **my** dress.*
> (*I* = you → *you*; *your* = me → *my*)

> *Arun said, "**They** are late."* → *Arun said that **they** were late.* (third person: no change)

### Rule 6: Change words of time and place when the report is made at a different time or place.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *now* | *then* |
| *today* | *that day* |
| *tonight* | *that night* |
| *this morning / week* | *that morning / week* |
| *tomorrow* | *the next day* / *the following day* |
| *yesterday* | *the previous day* / *the day before* |
| *next week / month / year* | *the following week* / *the next week* |
| *last week / month / year* | *the previous week* / *the week before* |
| *ago* | *before* / *earlier* |
| *here* | *there* |
| *this* | *that* |
| *these* | *those* |
| *come* | *go* (sometimes, when the place changes) |

> *She said, "I will come **tomorrow**."* → *She said that she would come **the next day**.*
> *He said, "I met him two days **ago**."* → *He said that he had met him two days **before**.*
> *Arun said, "I like **this** place."* → *Arun said that he liked **that** place.*

These changes follow **logic**. If you report something on the same day, in the same place, you can keep *today* and *here*: *(this morning) Amma said that she would be home **today**.* In exams, make the changes unless the question shows otherwise.

### Rule 7: Report a yes/no question with *asked* + *if / whether*, and use statement order.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *He said to me, "**Are you** ready?"* | *He **asked** me **if** I **was** ready.* |
| *She said, "**Do you** like tea?"* | *She **asked whether** I **liked** tea.* |
| *Ravi said to Kavya, "**Have you** seen my book?"* | *Ravi **asked** Kavya **if** she **had seen** his book.* |

Changes:
1. *said / said to* → ***asked*** (or *enquired, wanted to know*).
2. Add ***if*** or ***whether*** (not *that*).
3. Use **statement order**: subject + verb (*I was ready*, not *~~was I ready~~*).
4. Remove *do / does / did*: *"Do you like tea?"* → *if I **liked** tea*.
5. Change tense, pronouns, time and place as usual.
6. End with a **full stop**, not a question mark.

### Rule 8: Report a wh- question with *asked* + the question word, and use statement order.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *She said to me, "**Where do you live**?"* | *She asked me **where I lived**.* |
| *He said, "**What is** your name?"* | *He asked **what my name was**.* |
| *The teacher said, "**Why are you** late?"* | *The teacher asked **why I was** late.* |
| *Meena said, "**Who has taken** my pen?"* | *Meena asked **who had taken** her pen.* |

- Keep the question word (*where, what, why, who...*). Do **not** add *if* or *that*.
- Use statement order. Remove *do / does / did*.
- When the question word is the subject (*who has taken*), the order is already subject + verb.

> *~~She asked me where did I live.~~* ✘ → *She asked me where **I lived**.* ✔

### Rule 9: Report commands and requests with *told / ordered / requested / advised / begged* + person + *to*-infinitive.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *The teacher said to us, "**Sit down**."* | *The teacher **told** us **to sit** down.* |
| *The officer said to the soldiers, "**Fire**!"* | *The officer **ordered** the soldiers **to fire**.* |
| *She said to me, "**Please help** me."* | *She **requested** me **to help** her.* |
| *The doctor said to him, "**Drink** plenty of water."* | *The doctor **advised** him **to drink** plenty of water.* |
| *The beggar said, "**Please give** me some food."* | *The beggar **begged** (us) **to give** him some food.* |

**Negative commands** use ***not to***:

> *Amma said to me, "**Don't** go out."* → *Amma **told** me **not to go** out.*
> *He said to them, "**Don't** make a noise."* → *He **ordered** them **not to make** a noise.*

**Choose the reporting verb by the meaning:**

| Meaning | Reporting verb |
|---|---|
| normal instruction | *told* |
| strong command (from authority) | *ordered, commanded* |
| polite request (*please, kindly*) | *requested* (remove *please*) |
| advice | *advised* |
| humble or desperate request | *begged, implored* |
| strong encouragement | *urged* |
| warning | *warned* |

### Rule 10: Report exclamations with *exclaimed (with joy / sorrow / surprise) that*, and wishes with *wished* or *prayed*.

**Exclamations:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *He said, "**Hurray!** We have won."* | *He **exclaimed with joy that** they had won.* |
| *She said, "**Alas!** I have lost my purse."* | *She **exclaimed with sorrow that** she had lost her purse.* |
| *They said, "**What a** beautiful sight!"* | *They **exclaimed that** it was **a very** beautiful sight.* |
| *Ravi said, "**How** clever she is!"* | *Ravi **exclaimed that** she was **very** clever.* |
| *She said, "**Wow!** That's a lovely saree."* | *She **exclaimed with wonder that** it was a lovely saree.* |

- Remove the interjection (*Hurray, Alas, Wow*) and show its feeling: *with joy, with sorrow, with surprise, with wonder*.
- Change *What a ...!* and *How ...!* into a statement with ***very***.

**Wishes, greetings and prayers:**

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *He said to me, "**Good morning**."* | *He **wished** me **good morning**.* |
| *She said, "**Happy birthday**, Arun!"* | *She **wished** Arun **a happy birthday**.* |
| *Grandma said, "**May God bless you**!"* | *Grandma **prayed that God might bless** me.* |
| *They said to us, "**Thank you**."* | *They **thanked** us.* |
| *He said to his friends, "**Good night**."* | *He **bade** his friends **good night**.* (or *wished them good night*) |
| *She said to him, "**Congratulations!**"* | *She **congratulated** him.* |

## Special cases

### 1. *Yes* and *No* answers

Change *yes* and *no* into a short clause with the helping verb.

> *I said to him, "Are you coming?" He said, "**Yes**."*
> → *I asked him if he was coming. He **replied that he was**.*

> *She said, "Did you see the match?" I said, "**No**."*
> → *She asked if I had seen the match. I **replied that I hadn't**.*

### 2. Addressing someone by name

A name used to call someone becomes the object of the reporting verb.

> *"Kavya, close the door," said Amma.*
> → *Amma **told Kavya** to close the door.*

### 3. *Must* in reported speech

> *"I must study," said Ravi.* → *Ravi said that he **had to** study.* (necessity → *had to*)
> *"You must be hungry," she said.* → *She said that I **must** be hungry.* (deduction → no change)
> *"You must not smoke," said the doctor.* → *The doctor said that he **must not** smoke.* / *The doctor told him **not to smoke**.*

### 4. *Let's* in reported speech

> *He said, "**Let's** go to the beach."* → *He **suggested that we should go** to the beach.* (or *He suggested going to the beach.*)

More on this in [Reported speech (Class 12)](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-12/).

### 5. Other reporting verbs for statements

Instead of *said*, you can use a verb that shows the meaning more exactly: *replied, answered, added, explained, complained, admitted, promised, informed (+ person), assured (+ person)*.

> *"The shop is closed on Sundays," he explained.* → *He **explained that** the shop was closed on Sundays.*

### 6. When *said* comes after the speech

> *"I am hungry," said the child.* → *The child said that he / she was hungry.*

The position of *said* in the direct speech does not change the rules.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *He ~~said me~~ that he was tired.* | *He **told me** that he was tired.* | *Say* cannot take a person directly. Use *told* + person. |
| *She ~~told that~~ she was busy.* | *She **said that** she was busy.* / *She **told me that**...* | *Told* needs a person after it. |
| *He said that he ~~will~~ come.* | *He said that he **would** come.* | Past reporting verb → *will* becomes *would*. |
| *She asked me where ~~did I live~~.* | *She asked me where **I lived**.* | Reported questions use statement order, without *did*. |
| *He asked me ~~that~~ if I was ready.* | *He asked me **if** I was ready.* | No *that* with reported questions. |
| *He asked me if I was ready?* | *He asked me if I was ready.* | A reported question ends with a full stop, not a question mark. |
| *She told me ~~don't~~ go.* | *She told me **not to go**.* | Negative command → *not to* + V1. |
| *He requested me ~~please~~ to help.* | *He requested me to help him.* | *Requested* already shows politeness. Remove *please*. |
| *The teacher said that the sun ~~rose~~ in the east.* | *... the sun **rises** in the east.* | Universal truths keep the present tense. |
| *She said she would come ~~tomorrow~~.* (reported a week later) | *She said she would come **the next day**.* | Time words change when the time of reporting is different. |
| *He told to me to wait.* | *He told **me** to wait.* | *Tell* takes the person directly, with no *to*. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Convert** direct speech into indirect speech (and indirect into direct).
- **Report a dialogue** (a short conversation) in indirect speech.
- **Fill in** with suitable reporting verbs.

### Steps for "Change into indirect speech"

1. **Identify the kind of sentence** inside the quotation marks: statement, yes/no question, wh- question, command / request, exclamation or wish.
2. **Choose the reporting verb and the joining word:**

| Kind | Reporting verb | Joining word |
|---|---|---|
| statement | *said / told* + person | *that* |
| yes/no question | *asked* | *if / whether* |
| wh- question | *asked* | the question word |
| command / request | *told, ordered, requested, advised...* + person | *to* / *not to* + V1 |
| exclamation | *exclaimed (with joy / sorrow...)* | *that* |
| wish / greeting | *wished, prayed, thanked, bade* | — / *that* |

3. **Change the pronouns** (Rule 5).
4. **Change the tense** if the reporting verb is past (Rule 2), unless it is a universal truth.
5. **Change time and place words** (Rule 6).
6. **Fix the word order** for questions (statement order) and **the punctuation** (no quotation marks; full stop at the end).

### Worked example 1 (statement)

> *Karthik said to me, "I have lost my watch here."*

1. Kind: statement.
2. *said to me* → *told me* + *that*.
3. Pronouns: *I* = Karthik → *he*; *my* → *his*.
4. Tense: *have lost* (present perfect) → *had lost* (past perfect).
5. Place: *here* → *there*.

**Answer:** *Karthik **told me that he had lost his** watch **there**.*

### Worked example 2 (yes/no question)

> *Priya said to Arun, "Will you come to my birthday party tomorrow?"*

1. Kind: yes/no question.
2. *said to Arun* → *asked Arun* + *if / whether*.
3. Pronouns: *you* = Arun → *he*; *my* = Priya's → *her*.
4. Tense: *will* → *would*. Statement order: *he would come*.
5. Time: *tomorrow* → *the next day*.

**Answer:** *Priya **asked** Arun **if he would come** to **her** birthday party **the next day**.*

### Worked example 3 (wh- question)

> *The stranger said to me, "Where is the post office?"*

1. Kind: wh- question.
2. *said to me* → *asked me* + *where*.
3. Statement order: *the post office was*.
4. Tense: *is* → *was*.

**Answer:** *The stranger **asked** me **where the post office was**.*

### Worked example 4 (command)

> *The teacher said to the students, "Don't talk in the library."*

1. Kind: negative command (from a teacher).
2. *said to the students* → *told / ordered the students* + *not to*.
3. V1 after *to*: *talk*.

**Answer:** *The teacher **told** the students **not to talk** in the library.*

### Worked example 5 (exclamation)

> *Meena said, "What a lovely garden this is!"*

1. Kind: exclamation.
2. *said* → *exclaimed that*.
3. *What a lovely garden this is!* → statement with *very*: *it was a very lovely garden*.
4. *This* → *that* (or *it*).

**Answer:** *Meena **exclaimed that it was a very lovely garden**.* (or *Meena exclaimed with delight that the garden was very lovely.*)

### Worked example 6 (indirect → direct)

> *Ravi told his mother that he was hungry.*

1. The reporting verb *told his mother* → *said to his mother*.
2. Put the words back into quotation marks.
3. Pronoun: *he* (Ravi, the speaker) → *I*.
4. Tense: *was* → *am* (the original present).

**Answer:** *Ravi said to his mother, "**I am** hungry."*

## Quick check

Change into indirect speech.

1. *Divya said, "I am learning to dance."*
2. *He said to me, "Do you know Hindi?"*
3. *Amma said to Ravi, "Please bring some vegetables."*
4. *The teacher said, "Honesty is the best policy."*
5. *She said, "Alas! My cat is dead."*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***Divya said that she was learning to dance.*** *I → she*; present continuous → past continuous.
2. ***He asked me if (or whether) I knew Hindi.*** Yes/no question → *asked if*; *do you know* → *I knew* (statement order, no *do*).
3. ***Amma requested Ravi to bring some vegetables.*** *Please* shows a request → *requested ... to*; *please* is removed.
4. ***The teacher said that honesty is the best policy.*** A universal truth: the tense does not change.
5. ***She exclaimed with sorrow that her cat was dead.*** *Alas* → *with sorrow*; *my → her*; *is → was*.

</details>

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- *said* → ***said that***; *said to* + person → ***told*** + person + ***that***. Never *said me* or *told that*.
- Past reporting verb → **tense one step back**: *is → was, has → had, did → had done, will → would, can → could, may → might, must → had to*.
- No change: present or future reporting verb; universal truths.
- Pronouns: 1st person → speaker; 2nd person → listener; 3rd person → no change.
- *now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, ago → before, here → there, this → that*.
- Questions: ***asked if / whether*** (yes/no) or ***asked*** + wh-word; **statement order**; full stop.
- Commands: ***told / ordered / requested / advised*** + person + ***to*** (negative: ***not to***).
- Exclamations: ***exclaimed with joy / sorrow that***. Wishes: ***wished, prayed, thanked, bade***.
:::
