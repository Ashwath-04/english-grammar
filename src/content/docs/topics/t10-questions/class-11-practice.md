## Part A: Add suitable question tags

**1.** *Ravi is your brother, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***isn't he?*** Positive sentence → negative tag. Helping verb *is*. *Ravi* → *he*.

</details>

**2.** *They don't like coffee, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***do they?*** The sentence is negative (*don't*), so the tag is positive. Helping verb *do*, subject *they*.

</details>

**3.** *You can swim, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***can't you?*** Positive sentence → negative tag. Helping verb *can* → *can't*.

</details>

**4.** *Meena went to Madurai yesterday, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***didn't she?*** No helping verb. *Went* is past tense (V2), so the tag uses *did*. Positive → negative. *Meena* → *she*.

</details>

**5.** *The children have finished their homework, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***haven't they?*** Helping verb *have* (followed by V3 *finished*). *The children* → *they*.

</details>

**6.** *I am late, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***aren't I?*** Special case. The tag for *I am* is *aren't I?* (not *amn't I?*).

</details>

**7.** *Let's start the class, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***shall we?*** Special case. *Let's* always takes *shall we?*

</details>

**8.** *Close the window, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***will you?*** A command (imperative) usually takes *will you?* *Won't you?* or *could you?* are also acceptable.

</details>

**9.** *Don't make a noise, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***will you?*** A negative command (*Don't...*) always takes *will you?*

</details>

**10.** *There is a bank near the bus stand, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***isn't there?*** When the sentence starts with *there is*, the tag uses *there*. Positive → negative.

</details>

**11.** *Nobody called me, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***did they?*** *Nobody* makes the sentence negative, so the tag is positive. *Called* is past → *did*. *Nobody* is a word for people → *they*.

</details>

**12.** *Everyone enjoyed the match, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***didn't they?*** *Everyone* is a word for people → *they*. *Enjoyed* is past → *did*. Positive → negative.

</details>

**13.** *He never tells lies, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***does he?*** *Never* makes the sentence negative, so the tag is positive. *Tells* is V5 → *does*.

</details>

**14.** *She has a new phone, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***doesn't she?*** Here *has* is a main verb (it means "owns"), so the tag uses *does*. (*Hasn't she?* is also heard in British English, but *doesn't she?* is the safer answer.)

</details>

**15.** *You had better see a doctor, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***hadn't you?*** *Had better* uses *had* in the tag.

</details>

**16.** *He used to live in Coimbatore, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***didn't he?*** *Used to* is past tense with no helping verb, so the tag uses *did*.

</details>

**17.** *Nothing is impossible, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is it?*** *Nothing* makes the sentence negative, so the tag is positive. *Nothing* is a word for things → *it*.

</details>

**18.** *Few students came to the meeting, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***did they?*** *Few* (without *a*) means "almost no", so the sentence is negative and the tag is positive.

</details>

**19.** *A few students came to the meeting, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***didn't they?*** *A few* means "some", so the sentence is positive and the tag is negative. Compare this with question 18.

</details>

**20.** *I think she is right, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***isn't she?*** With *I think*, the speaker is really asking about the second part (*she is right*), so the tag matches that part.

</details>

## Part B: Find and correct the mistake

**21.** *You are coming, isn't it?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*You are coming, **aren't you**?* The tag must use the helping verb (*are*) and the pronoun (*you*) from the sentence. *Isn't it?* does not fit every sentence.

</details>

**22.** *Kavya can sing, can't Kavya?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Kavya can sing, **can't she**?* The tag uses a pronoun, never the name.

</details>

**23.** *He hardly studies, doesn't he?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*He hardly studies, **does he**?* *Hardly* makes the sentence negative, so the tag must be positive.

</details>

**24.** *This is your bag, isn't this?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*This is your bag, **isn't it**?* Use *it* in the tag for *this* and *that*.

</details>

**25.** *She went home early, wasn't she?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*She went home early, **didn't she**?* There is no helping verb in the sentence. *Went* is past tense, so the tag uses *did*.

</details>
