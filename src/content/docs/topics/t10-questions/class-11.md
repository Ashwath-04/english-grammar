## What you will learn

- What a question tag is, and why people use it
- The main rule: a positive sentence takes a negative tag, and a negative sentence takes a positive tag
- How to choose the right helping verb and the right pronoun for the tag
- Every special case: *I am*, *Let's*, commands, *there is*, *everyone*, *never*, and more
- How to answer the "Add question tags" exercise without mistakes

## Why this matters

Question tags come up in everyday speech all the time: *It's hot today, isn't it?* They are also a regular exercise in the Class 11 textbook. The exercise looks easy, but it has many small traps. A student who knows the five main rules and the special cases can get every question right.

## The idea in simple words

Sometimes we say something, and we want the other person to agree with us. Or we are fairly sure about something, and we just want to check.

To do this, we add a very short question at the end of the sentence.

> *Priya is your sister, **isn't she**?*

The main sentence is *Priya is your sister*. The short question at the end, *isn't she?*, is the **question tag**.

The speaker is almost sure that Priya is the listener's sister. The tag invites the listener to say "Yes, she is."

Here are a few more:

> *You can drive, **can't you**?*
> *They didn't come, **did they**?*
> *Arun plays cricket, **doesn't he**?*

Notice the pattern. When the sentence is **positive** (*You can drive*), the tag is **negative** (*can't you?*). When the sentence is **negative** (*They didn't come*), the tag is **positive** (*did they?*).

## Grammar words you need

**Statement:** a sentence that tells something. *Ravi is tall.* is a statement.

**Positive statement:** a statement with no *not* or other negative word. *Ravi is tall.*

**Negative statement:** a statement with *not* (or *n't*), or with a negative word like *never* or *nobody*. *Ravi is not tall.* *Ravi never smiles.*

**Helping verb (auxiliary verb):** a verb that helps the main verb. The helping verbs are:
- forms of *be*: am, is, are, was, were
- forms of *have*: have, has, had
- forms of *do*: do, does, did
- **modal verbs**: can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must

In *She **is** reading*, the helping verb is *is*. In *They **have** gone*, it is *have*. In *You **can** swim*, it is *can*.

**Pronoun:** a word used in place of a noun: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.

## The five main rules

### Rule 1: Positive sentence → negative tag. Negative sentence → positive tag.

This is the most important rule. Think of a see-saw: when one side goes up, the other side comes down.

| Statement | Tag |
|---|---|
| Positive: *She is a doctor,* | negative: ***isn't she?*** |
| Negative: *She isn't a doctor,* | positive: ***is she?*** |
| Positive: *You will come,* | negative: ***won't you?*** |
| Negative: *You won't come,* | positive: ***will you?*** |

### Rule 2: Use the same helping verb that is in the sentence.

Find the helping verb in the statement. Use the same one in the tag.

> *Kavya **has** finished her work, **hasn't** she?*
> *The bus **was** late, **wasn't** it?*
> *We **should** leave now, **shouldn't** we?*
> *You **can't** swim, **can** you?*

If there is more than one helping verb, use the **first** one.

> *He **has** been working all day, **hasn't** he?* (not *~~hasn't been he?~~*)
> *She **will** have reached home by now, **won't** she?*

### Rule 3: If there is no helping verb, use *do*, *does* or *did*.

Some sentences have only a main verb and no helping verb:

> *Arun **plays** cricket.*
> *They **live** in Salem.*
> *Meena **went** to Madurai.*

Here, we use *do*, *does* or *did* in the tag. Choose it like this:

| The verb in the sentence | Tag uses | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present tense, with he / she / it / one person (V5: *plays*, *goes*) | **does** | *Arun **plays** cricket, **doesn't** he?* |
| Present tense, with I / you / we / they / more than one person (V1: *play*, *go*) | **do** | *They **live** in Salem, **don't** they?* |
| Past tense (V2: *played*, *went*) | **did** | *Meena **went** to Madurai, **didn't** she?* |

A quick way to check: turn the statement into a question. *Arun plays cricket* → ***Does** Arun play cricket?* The word you used at the start of the question (*does*) is the word for the tag.

### Rule 4: Use a pronoun in the tag, never the noun.

The tag always uses a **pronoun** (he, she, it, they...), even when the sentence uses a name or a noun.

> *Ravi is tall, isn't **he**?* (not *~~isn't Ravi?~~*)
> *The children are playing, aren't **they**?*
> *Your phone is new, isn't **it**?*
> *My parents and I went to the temple, didn't **we**?*

Use ***it*** for *this* and *that*. Use ***they*** for *these* and *those*.

> ***This** is your bag, isn't **it**?*
> ***Those** are your shoes, aren't **they**?*

### Rule 5: A negative tag uses the short form.

Negative tags use the short form with *n't*: *isn't, aren't, wasn't, don't, doesn't, didn't, can't, won't, shouldn't*.

> *She is coming, **isn't** she?* (not *~~is not she?~~*)

The long form *is she not?* is correct grammar, but it is very formal and rarely used today. In exams, always use the short form.

Remember: *will not* becomes ***won't***, and *can not* becomes ***can't***.

## Special cases

These are the cases where the five main rules alone don't give the right answer. Most exam mistakes happen here.

### 1. *I am* → *aren't I?*

The tag for *I am* is ***aren't I?***. There is no word *amn't* in standard English.

> *I am late, **aren't I**?* (not *~~amn't I?~~* or *~~am not I?~~*)

But for the negative *I am not*, the tag is the normal ***am I?***

> *I'm not late, **am I**?*

### 2. *Let's* → *shall we?*

*Let's* (= *let us*) is used to suggest something that we will do together. Its tag is always ***shall we?***

> ***Let's** go for a walk, **shall we**?*
> ***Let's** start the class, **shall we**?*

Be careful: *Let us* can also mean "allow us". Then it is a request, and the tag is ***will you?***

> *Let us go home early, **will you**?* (= Please allow us to go home early.)

### 3. Commands and requests → *will you?*

A command or request (an **imperative** sentence) starts with a verb: *Close the door. Sit down. Help me.*

The usual tag is ***will you?***

> *Close the door, **will you**?*
> *Pass me the salt, **will you**?*

You may also see ***won't you?*** (a friendly invitation) and ***can you? / could you?*** (a polite request):

> *Have some more sweets, **won't you**?*
> *Help me with this box, **could you**?*

A **negative** command (starting with *Don't*) always takes ***will you?***

> ***Don't** be late, **will you**?*
> ***Don't** touch that wire, **will you**?*

### 4. *There is / There are* → *isn't there? / aren't there?*

When the sentence begins with *there*, use ***there*** in the tag too.

> ***There is** a bank near the bus stand, **isn't there**?*
> ***There were** many people at the wedding, **weren't there**?*
> ***There isn't** any milk, **is there**?*

### 5. *Everyone, someone, no one* → *they*. *Everything, something, nothing* → *it*.

Words for **people** (everyone, everybody, someone, somebody, anyone, no one, nobody) take ***they*** in the tag.

> ***Everyone** enjoyed the match, didn't **they**?*
> ***Somebody** called you, didn't **they**?*

Words for **things** (everything, something, nothing, anything) take ***it*** in the tag.

> ***Everything** is ready, isn't **it**?*
> ***Nothing** happened, did **it**?*

Notice the last example. *Nothing* makes the sentence negative, so the tag is positive (*did it?*). See the next special case.

### 6. Negative words make the sentence negative → positive tag

Some sentences have no *not*, but they are still negative because of a negative word. These words are:

*never, no, nobody, no one, nothing, none, nowhere, hardly, scarcely, barely, seldom, rarely, few, little*

A sentence with one of these words is **negative**, so the tag is **positive**.

> *He **never** tells lies, **does** he?* (not *~~doesn't he?~~*)
> *She **hardly** eats anything, **does** she?*
> ***Nobody** came to the party, **did** they?*
> ***Few** students passed the test, **did** they?*
> *There is **little** time left, **is** there?*

Be careful with ***a few*** and ***a little***. They are **positive** (they mean "some"), so the tag is negative.

> ***A few** students passed the test, **didn't** they?*
> *There is **a little** time left, **isn't** there?*

### 7. *have* as a main verb

*Have* can be a helping verb or a main verb.

As a **helping verb** (followed by V3), use *have / has / had* in the tag:

> *She **has finished** her work, **hasn't** she?*

As a **main verb** (meaning "own" or "possess", or in phrases like *have lunch*), use *do / does / did*:

> *She **has** a new phone, **doesn't** she?*
> *They **had** lunch at noon, **didn't** they?*

In British English, you may also see *She has a new phone, **hasn't** she?* This is correct too, but *doesn't she?* is more common today and is the safer choice in exams.

### 8. *had better*, *used to*, *ought to*

> *You **had better** see a doctor, **hadn't** you?* (*had better* uses *had* in the tag)
> *He **used to** live in Coimbatore, **didn't** he?* (*used to* is past tense, so use *did*)
> *We **ought to** help them, **shouldn't** we?* (*shouldn't* is the usual tag for *ought to*; *oughtn't we?* is also correct but very formal)

### 9. Sentences with two clauses

When a sentence has two parts (two clauses), the tag matches the **main clause**, that is, the part with the main subject and verb.

> *He **said** that he was tired, **didn't** he?* (main clause: *He said*)
> *You **know** where she lives, **don't** you?* (main clause: *You know*)

There is one exception. When the sentence starts with ***I think, I believe, I suppose, I'm sure***, the speaker is really asking about the **second** part. So the tag matches the second part:

> *I think **she is** right, **isn't she**?* (not *~~don't I?~~*)
> *I suppose **they will** come, **won't they**?*

## How to say it: rising and falling tone

The same tag can mean two different things, depending on how your voice moves.

**Falling tone** (voice goes down on the tag): you are sure, and you expect the listener to agree.
> *It's a lovely day, isn't it?* ↘ (I'm sure it is. Agree with me.)

**Rising tone** (voice goes up on the tag): you are not sure, and you are really asking.
> *You've paid the fees, haven't you?* ↗ (I'm not sure. Please tell me.)

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *You are coming, ~~isn't it~~?* | *You are coming, **aren't you**?* | The tag must use the verb (*are*) and the pronoun (*you*) from the sentence. *Isn't it?* is not a general tag for every sentence. |
| *Kavya can sing, ~~can't Kavya~~?* | *Kavya can sing, **can't she**?* | The tag always uses a pronoun, not the name. |
| *He hardly studies, ~~doesn't he~~?* | *He hardly studies, **does he**?* | *Hardly* makes the sentence negative, so the tag must be positive. |
| *I am right, ~~amn't I~~?* | *I am right, **aren't I**?* | The tag for *I am* is *aren't I?* |
| *This is your bag, ~~isn't this~~?* | *This is your bag, **isn't it**?* | Use *it* for *this* and *that*. |
| *She went home, ~~wasn't she~~?* | *She went home, **didn't she**?* | The sentence has no helping verb. *Went* is past tense, so the tag uses *did*. |
| *Let's play, ~~will we~~?* | *Let's play, **shall we**?* | The tag for *Let's* is always *shall we?* |
| *He has a car, ~~isn't he~~?* | *He has a car, **doesn't he**?* | *Has* here is a main verb (possession), so the tag uses *does*. |

## How it comes in the exam

The usual exercise is: **"Add suitable question tags."** Follow these four steps for every sentence.

1. **Is the sentence positive or negative?** Look for *not / n't* and for negative words (*never, hardly, nobody, few...*).
2. **Which helping verb is in the sentence?** If there is none, choose *do / does / did*.
3. **What is the subject?** Change it to a pronoun.
4. **Put the tag together:** comma, then helping verb (with *n't* if the tag is negative), then pronoun, then a question mark.

Then check the special cases: *I am, Let's,* commands, *there, everyone / nothing, I think.*

### Worked example 1

> *Your brother works in Chennai, ______?*

1. Positive or negative? Positive (no *not*, no negative word). So the tag is **negative**.
2. Helping verb? None. *Works* is present tense with one person (V5), so use **does**.
3. Subject? *Your brother* → **he**.
4. Tag: **doesn't he?**

**Answer:** *Your brother works in Chennai, **doesn't he**?*

### Worked example 2

> *Nobody knew the answer, ______?*

1. Positive or negative? *Nobody* is a negative word, so the sentence is negative. The tag is **positive**.
2. Helping verb? None. *Knew* is past tense (V2), so use **did**.
3. Subject? *Nobody* is a word for people → **they**.
4. Tag: **did they?**

**Answer:** *Nobody knew the answer, **did they**?*

### Worked example 3

> *I am your best friend, ______?*

Special case: *I am* → ***aren't I?***

**Answer:** *I am your best friend, **aren't I**?*

### Worked example 4

> *Don't forget to call me, ______?*

Special case: a negative command (*Don't...*) → ***will you?***

**Answer:** *Don't forget to call me, **will you**?*

## Quick check

Try these before you look at the answers.

1. *The shop opens at nine, ______?*
2. *You haven't seen my keys, ______?*
3. *Let's have tea, ______?*
4. *She seldom goes out, ______?*
5. *There are two exams tomorrow, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***doesn't it?*** No helping verb; *opens* is present with one thing (V5) → *does*. Positive sentence → negative tag. *The shop* → *it*.
2. ***have you?*** The sentence is negative (*haven't*), so the tag is positive. Helping verb *have*, subject *you*.
3. ***shall we?*** Special case: *Let's* always takes *shall we?*
4. ***does she?*** *Seldom* is a negative word, so the tag is positive. *Goes* is V5 → *does*.
5. ***aren't there?*** Sentences with *there are* use *there* in the tag. Positive → negative.

</details>

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- Positive sentence → negative tag. Negative sentence → positive tag.
- Use the same helping verb. No helping verb → *do / does / did*.
- Always use a pronoun in the tag.
- Special cases: *I am → aren't I?* · *Let's → shall we?* · commands → *will you?* · *there is → isn't there?* · *everyone → they* · *nothing → it* · *never / hardly / few → positive tag*.
:::
