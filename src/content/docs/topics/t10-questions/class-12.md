## What you will learn

- How to make **yes/no questions** (helping verb first)
- How to make **wh- questions** with *what, when, where, who, whom, whose, which, why, how* (and *how much, how many, how long, how often, how far*)
- The difference between **subject questions** and **object questions**: *Who **told** you?* vs *Who **did you tell**?*
- How to **frame a question** for a given answer (the most common exam task): *He went to Chennai **by train**.* → *How did he go to Chennai?*
- **Indirect (embedded) questions**: *Can you tell me where the office **is**?*
- **Negative questions**: *Didn't you see him?*

## Why this matters

Asking questions correctly is one of the most used skills in English, in class, at work and in daily life. Many students say *~~Where you are going?~~* or *~~What he said?~~* in speech.

In the exam, "Frame questions so as to get the underlined words as answers" is a common task. It needs three things: the right question word, the right helping verb, and the right word order. This lesson gives a fixed method that works every time. Question tags, the other part of this topic, are in [Question tags (Class 11)](/topics/t10-questions/class-11/).

## The idea in simple words

There are two main kinds of questions.

**1. Questions that can be answered with "Yes" or "No":**

> ***Is** Ravi at home?* — *Yes, he is.*
> ***Did** you finish your homework?* — *No, I didn't.*

**2. Questions that ask for information**, starting with a question word (*what, where, when...*):

> ***Where** does Ravi live?* — *In Salem.*
> ***When** did you finish your homework?* — *At nine o'clock.*

In both kinds, the **helping verb comes before the subject**:

> *Ravi **is** at home.* → ***Is** Ravi at home?*
> *Ravi **lives** in Salem.* → *Where **does** Ravi live?*

That swap (helping verb before the subject) is the heart of English questions.

## Grammar words you need

**Interrogative sentence:** a sentence that asks a question. It ends with a question mark (?).

**Yes/no question:** a question whose answer is *yes* or *no*. It starts with a helping verb.

**Wh- question:** a question that starts with a question word (*what, who, where, when, why, how...*). It asks for information.

**Question word (interrogative word):** *what, which, who, whom, whose, when, where, why, how*.

**Helping verb (auxiliary):** *am, is, are, was, were, has, have, had, do, does, did, can, will, should...* See [Auxiliary verbs](/foundation/f3-auxiliaries/).

**Inversion:** putting the helping verb **before** the subject: *She **is** → **Is** she?*

**Subject:** the person or thing that does the action. **Object:** the person or thing that receives it. See [Subject, verb, object and complement](/foundation/f4-sentence-parts/).

## The rules

### Rule 1: For a yes/no question, move the first helping verb before the subject.

| Statement | Yes/no question |
|---|---|
| *She **is** reading.* | ***Is** she reading?* |
| *They **have** finished.* | ***Have** they finished?* |
| *Arun **can** swim.* | ***Can** Arun swim?* |
| *We **will** be late.* | ***Will** we be late?* |
| *He **has been** working.* | ***Has** he been working?* (only the first helping verb moves) |

When *be* is the **main verb**, it moves too:

> *Meena **is** a doctor.* → ***Is** Meena a doctor?*
> *They **were** at the temple.* → ***Were** they at the temple?*

### Rule 2: If there is no helping verb, add *do, does* or *did*, and change the main verb to V1.

| Statement | Question | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *They **play** cricket.* | ***Do** they **play** cricket?* | present, *they* → *do* |
| *Kavya **plays** the veena.* | ***Does** Kavya **play** the veena?* | present, *she* → *does*; *plays → play* |
| *Ravi **went** to Madurai.* | ***Did** Ravi **go** to Madurai?* | past → *did*; *went → go* |

The *-s* and the past tense move into *does* and *did*. The main verb is always **V1**:

> *~~Does she plays?~~* ✘ / *~~Did he went?~~* ✘

*Have* as a **main verb** (= own) also takes *do*: *She **has** a car.* → ***Does** she **have** a car?*

### Rule 3: For a wh- question, use the order: question word + helping verb + subject + main verb.

| Question word | Helping verb | Subject | Main verb | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Where* | *do* | *you* | *live* | *?* |
| *What* | *is* | *she* | *cooking* | *?* |
| *When* | *did* | *they* | *arrive* | *?* |
| *Why* | *have* | *you* | *come* | *late?* |
| *How* | *can* | *I* | *help* | *you?* |

> *~~Where you live?~~* ✘ → *Where **do you** live?* ✔
> *~~What she is cooking?~~* ✘ → *What **is she** cooking?* ✔

### Rule 4: Choose the question word by the kind of answer you want.

| Question word | Asks about | Example question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| *what* | a thing, an action, an idea | *What did you buy?* | *A book.* |
| *which* | a choice from a known set | *Which colour do you like, red or blue?* | *Blue.* |
| *who* | a person (subject, or object in speech) | *Who broke the window?* | *Arun.* |
| *whom* | a person (object; formal) | *Whom did you meet?* | *My teacher.* |
| *whose* | the owner | *Whose bag is this?* | *Priya's.* |
| *when* | time | *When does the bus leave?* | *At six.* |
| *where* | place | *Where is the post office?* | *Near the bank.* |
| *why* | reason or purpose | *Why are you late?* | *Because the bus was late.* |
| *how* | manner, means, condition | *How did you come?* | *By bus.* / *How are you?* — *Fine.* |
| *how much* | amount (uncountable), price | *How much milk do we need?* / *How much is this?* | *Two litres.* / *Fifty rupees.* |
| *how many* | number (countable) | *How many brothers do you have?* | *Two.* |
| *how long* | length of time, length | *How long did you wait?* | *For an hour.* |
| *how often* | frequency | *How often do you visit Ooty?* | *Every year.* |
| *how far* | distance | *How far is Trichy from here?* | *About 60 km.* |
| *how old* | age | *How old is your grandmother?* | *Eighty.* |
| *what time* | clock time | *What time does the film start?* | *At 6.30.* |
| *what kind of* | type | *What kind of music do you like?* | *Carnatic music.* |

### Rule 5: When the question word is the subject, do not use *do / does / did*. Keep the statement order.

Compare these two questions about the same event: *Arun called Meena.*

> ***Who called** Meena?* — *Arun.* (*who* = the subject, the caller)
> ***Who did** Arun **call**?* — *Meena.* (*who* = the object, the person called)

| Subject question (no *do*) | Object question (with *do / does / did*) |
|---|---|
| ***Who told** you?* | ***Who did** you **tell**?* |
| ***What happened**?* | ***What did** you **do**?* |
| ***Which bus goes** to Salem?* | ***Which bus did** you **take**?* |
| ***How many students came**?* | ***How many students did** you **see**?* |

**Test:** in the answer, is the unknown word the **subject** (the doer)? Then the question word takes its place, and the verb stays as it is (*called, goes, happened*).

> *~~Who did call Meena?~~* ✘ (when you mean "Who was the caller?")
> *~~What did happen?~~* ✘ → *What **happened**?* ✔

### Rule 6: *Who* or *whom*: use *whom* for the object in formal English, and always after a preposition.

> ***Who** is at the door?* (subject)
> ***Whom** did you invite?* (object: formal) / ***Who** did you invite?* (object: normal speech)
> ***To whom** did you give the money?* (after a preposition: always *whom*)
> ***Who** did you give the money **to**?* (preposition at the end: *who* is normal)

In exams, use ***whom*** for the object, especially in formal answers.

### Rule 7: To frame a question for a given answer, follow five steps.

This is the main exam task. The given sentence has some words underlined (or in bold). You must write a question whose answer is those words.

1. **Choose the question word** that matches the underlined words (time → *when*; place → *where*; reason → *why*; person as subject → *who*; person as object → *whom*...).
2. **Is the underlined part the subject?** If yes, replace it with *who / what / which* + keep the rest in the same order (Rule 5). Stop here.
3. **Otherwise, find the helping verb.** If there is none, use *do / does / did* and change the main verb to V1.
4. **Put it in order:** question word + helping verb + subject + main verb + the rest. **Remove** the underlined words.
5. **Change the pronouns** if the question is asked to the speaker: *I / we → you*, *my / our → your*, *me / us → you*.

### Rule 8: An indirect (embedded) question uses statement order, with no *do / does / did*.

When a question is placed inside another sentence (*Can you tell me...*, *I wonder...*, *Do you know...*), it is no longer a real question. Its word order goes back to **statement order**: subject + verb.

| Direct question | Indirect question |
|---|---|
| *Where **is the office**?* | *Can you tell me where **the office is**?* |
| *What time **does the train leave**?* | *Do you know what time **the train leaves**?* |
| *Why **did she cry**?* | *I wonder why **she cried**.* |
| ***Is** he coming?* | *I don't know **if / whether he is** coming.* |
| ***Did** they win?* | *Could you tell me **whether they won**?* |

Rules:
- No inversion: *~~Can you tell me where is the office?~~* ✘
- No *do / does / did*: the verb takes its normal form (*leaves, cried*).
- Yes/no questions use ***if*** or ***whether***.
- The punctuation follows the **outer** sentence: *Can you tell me ...?* (question mark) / *I wonder ...* (full stop).

Indirect questions are polite. They are also the basis of reported questions. See [Reported speech (Class 11)](/topics/t12-reported-speech/class-11/).

### Rule 9: A negative question uses the short form *n't* with the helping verb. It often shows surprise or expects "yes".

> ***Didn't** you see him?* (I'm surprised. I thought you saw him.)
> ***Isn't** she your cousin?* (I think she is. Please confirm.)
> ***Why didn't** you call me?* (a complaint)
> ***Won't** you have some more?* (a friendly offer)

The full form puts *not* after the subject, and is formal: ***Did** you **not** see him?*

**Answering a negative question:** answer about the **fact**, not about the question.

> *Didn't you go to school? — **Yes**, I did.* (I went.) / ***No**, I didn't.* (I didn't go.)

### Rule 10: Choice questions use *or*. Wh- questions with a preposition can put it at the end.

**Choice questions:**

> *Do you want tea **or** coffee?*
> *Shall we go by bus **or** by train?*

**Prepositions in questions:**

> *Who are you waiting **for**?* (normal)
> ***For whom** are you waiting?* (formal)
> *Where are you **from**?*
> *What are you talking **about**?*

## Special cases

### 1. *What* or *which*

> ***What** is your favourite subject?* (open: any subject)
> ***Which** is your favourite subject, maths or science?* (a choice from a small set)

### 2. *Whose* and *who's*

> ***Whose** pen is this?* (owner)
> ***Who's** that boy?* (= *who is*)

### 3. *How long*, *how far* and *how often*

> ***How long** does the journey take?* — *Three hours.* (time)
> ***How far** is Kodaikanal from Madurai?* — *About 120 km.* (distance)
> ***How often** do you go to the temple?* — *Every Friday.* (frequency)

### 4. *What ... like?* and *How ...?*

> ***What is** your new school **like**?* — *It's big and friendly.* (description)
> ***How is** your grandmother?* — *She is fine, thank you.* (health)

### 5. Questions with *there is / there are*

> *There is a bank near here.* → ***Is there** a bank near here?*
> *There are 40 students in the class.* → ***How many students are there** in the class?*

### 6. Questions with *have to* and *used to*

These work like main verbs, so they take *do / did*:

> *She has to go.* → ***Does** she **have to** go?*
> *He used to live here.* → ***Did** he **use to** live here?*

### 7. Questions in the passive

> *The letter was written by Kavya.* → ***By whom was** the letter written?* / ***Who was** the letter written **by**?*

See [Active and passive voice (Class 12)](/topics/t11-active-passive/class-12/).

### 8. Statement questions in speech

In friendly speech, people sometimes ask a question with statement order and a rising voice: *You're from Madurai?* This is fine in conversation, but in written exams always use inversion.

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *~~Where you are going?~~* | *Where **are you** going?* | The helping verb comes before the subject. |
| *What did he ~~said~~?* | *What did he **say**?* | After *did*, use V1. |
| *Does she ~~likes~~ mangoes?* | *Does she **like** mangoes?* | After *does*, use V1. |
| *Who ~~did break~~ the glass?* | *Who **broke** the glass?* | *Who* is the subject. No *did*. |
| *What ~~did happen~~?* | *What **happened**?* | *What* is the subject. No *did*. |
| *Can you tell me where ~~is the station~~?* | *Can you tell me where **the station is**?* | Indirect question: statement order. |
| *I don't know what ~~does he want~~.* | *I don't know what **he wants**.* | Indirect question: no *does*, normal verb form. |
| *How ~~many~~ money do you have?* | *How **much** money do you have?* | *Money* is uncountable → *how much*. |
| *~~Whom~~ told you this?* | ***Who** told you this?* | The person is the subject → *who*. |
| *Do you know ~~that~~ he is coming?* (meaning "is he coming?") | *Do you know **whether / if** he is coming?* | A yes/no indirect question uses *if / whether*. |
| *~~What time the train leaves?~~* | *What time **does** the train **leave**?* | A direct question needs *does* and V1. |

## How it comes in the exam

Common exercise types:

- **Frame questions** to get the underlined words as answers.
- **Change statements into questions** (yes/no or wh-).
- **Add question tags** (see [Question tags (Class 11)](/topics/t10-questions/class-11/)).
- **Correct** a wrongly formed question.

### Steps for "Frame a question for the underlined words"

1. **What kind of information** is underlined? Person, thing, time, place, reason, manner, number, amount, owner, distance, frequency?
2. **Choose the question word** (Rule 4).
3. **Is the underlined part the subject?** → question word + verb (no *do*), keep the rest.
4. **If not:** find the helping verb or add *do / does / did*. Change the main verb to V1.
5. **Order:** question word + helping verb + subject + main verb + rest. Drop the underlined words.
6. **Pronouns:** if the sentence uses *I / we / my / our*, the question to that person uses *you / your*.
7. **Punctuation:** end with a question mark.

### Worked example 1

> *Ravi went to Chennai **by train**.*

1. Underlined: *by train* → means of transport → manner.
2. Question word: ***How***.
3. Subject? No (*Ravi* is the subject).
4. Helping verb? None. *Went* is past → *did*. *Went → go*.
5. Order: *How + did + Ravi + go + to Chennai?*

**Answer:** ***How did Ravi go to Chennai?***

### Worked example 2

> ***My uncle** gave me this watch.*

1. Underlined: *My uncle* → a person.
2. Question word: ***Who***.
3. Subject? **Yes** (the giver). Replace it with *who* and keep the verb as it is.
4. The speaker said *me*. In a question to the speaker, *me → you*.

**Answer:** ***Who gave you this watch?*** (not *~~Who did give you~~*)

### Worked example 3

> *Kavya practises the veena **every day**.*

1. Underlined: *every day* → frequency.
2. Question word: ***How often***.
3. Subject? No.
4. Helping verb? None. Present, *she* → *does*. *Practises → practise*.

**Answer:** ***How often does Kavya practise the veena?***

### Worked example 4

> *I have been waiting here **for two hours**.*

1. Underlined: *for two hours* → length of time.
2. Question word: ***How long***.
3. Helping verbs: *have been*. The first one, *have*, moves.
4. Pronoun: *I → you*.

**Answer:** ***How long have you been waiting here?***

### Worked example 5

> *The children were crying **because they were hungry**.*

1. Underlined: *because they were hungry* → reason.
2. Question word: ***Why***.
3. Helping verb: *were*. Move it before the subject.

**Answer:** ***Why were the children crying?***

### Worked example 6

> *This is **Priya's** bag.*

1. Underlined: *Priya's* → owner.
2. Question word: ***Whose*** + noun (*whose bag*).
3. Main verb *is* moves before the subject *this*.

**Answer:** ***Whose bag is this?***

## Quick check

Frame questions for the words in bold.

1. *The train leaves **at 6.15**.*
2. *Meena bought **three** kilos of rice.*
3. ***The postman** brought this letter.*
4. *We met **our class teacher** at the market.* (formal)
5. Correct the error: *Could you tell me where does Arun live?*

<details>
<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. ***When does the train leave?*** (or *What time does the train leave?*) Time → *when*. No helping verb, present → *does* + V1.
2. ***How many kilos of rice did Meena buy?*** Number of countable units → *how many*. Past → *did*; *bought → buy*.
3. ***Who brought this letter?*** *The postman* is the subject → *who* + the verb as it is. No *did*.
4. ***Whom did you meet at the market?*** The person is the object → *whom* (formal). Past → *did*; *met → meet*. *We → you*.
5. ***Could you tell me where Arun lives?*** An indirect question uses statement order, without *does*.

## Summary

:::tip[Remember]
- **Yes/no:** helping verb + subject + main verb? No helping verb → *do / does / did* + V1.
- **Wh-:** question word + helping verb + subject + main verb?
- **Subject question:** the question word replaces the subject; no *do* (*Who told you? What happened?*).
- **Framing:** choose the word → subject or not? → helping verb / *do* → invert → drop the answer → change *I / my* to *you / your*.
- **Indirect question:** statement order, no *do*, *if / whether* for yes/no (*Can you tell me where the office is?*).
- *how much* + uncountable · *how many* + countable · *how long* time · *how far* distance · *how often* frequency · *whose* owner.
:::
