## Part A: Frame questions to get the words in bold as answers

**1.** *Arun lives **in Coimbatore**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Where does Arun live?*** Place → *where*. No helping verb; present with *he* → *does*; *lives → live*.

</details>

**2.** *The exam starts **on Monday**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***When does the exam start?*** Time → *when*. Present, singular subject → *does* + V1.

</details>

**3.** ***Kavya** won the first prize.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Who won the first prize?*** **Trap:** *Kavya* is the subject. Replace her with *who* and keep the verb *won*. No *did*.

</details>

**4.** *Kavya won **the first prize**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***What did Kavya win?*** Now the bold part is the object (a thing) → *what*. Past → *did*; *won → win*. Compare with question 3.

</details>

**5.** *They are laughing **because the film is funny**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Why are they laughing?*** Reason → *why*. The helping verb *are* moves before the subject.

</details>

**6.** *My father goes to work **by bus**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***How does your father go to work?*** Means of transport → *how*. *My → your* (asking the speaker). *Goes → does ... go*.

</details>

**7.** *This is **Meena's** cycle.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Whose cycle is this?*** Owner → *whose* + noun.

</details>

**8.** *She has **two** brothers.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***How many brothers does she have?*** Number (countable) → *how many*. *Has* is the main verb here (there is no other verb), so the question uses *does* + *have*.

</details>

**9.** *I need **two litres** of milk.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***How much milk do you need?*** Amount of an uncountable noun → *how much*. *I → you*. Present with *you* → *do*.

</details>

**10.** *The shirt costs **four hundred rupees**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***How much does the shirt cost?*** Price → *how much*.

</details>

**11.** *We have lived here **for ten years**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***How long have you lived here?*** Length of time → *how long*. The helping verb *have* moves. *We → you*.

</details>

**12.** *Madurai is **about 450 kilometres** from Chennai.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***How far is Madurai from Chennai?*** Distance → *how far*. The main verb *is* moves before the subject.

</details>

**13.** *Ravi visits his grandparents **once a month**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***How often does Ravi visit his grandparents?*** Frequency → *how often*.

</details>

**14.** *I met **the principal** at the function.* (formal)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Whom did you meet at the function?*** The person is the object → *whom* in formal English. (*Who did you meet...?* is fine in speech.)

</details>

**15.** *The letter was written **by Divya**.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***By whom was the letter written?*** (or *Who was the letter written by?*) The doer in a passive sentence → *by whom*.

</details>

**16.** ***The heavy rain** spoiled the match.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***What spoiled the match?*** The subject is a thing → *what* + the verb as it is. No *did*.

</details>

**17.** *I prefer **the blue** shirt.* (choice from two)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Which shirt do you prefer?*** A choice from a known set → *which* + noun.

</details>

**18.** *My grandmother is **eighty** years old.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***How old is your grandmother?*** Age → *how old*. *My → your*.

</details>

## Part B: Change into yes/no questions

**19.** *The children have finished their lunch.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Have the children finished their lunch?*** Move the first helping verb (*have*) before the subject.

</details>

**20.** *Priya plays chess very well.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Does Priya play chess very well?*** No helping verb. Present with *she* → *does*; *plays → play*.

</details>

**21.** *They went to the beach yesterday.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Did they go to the beach yesterday?*** Past → *did*; *went → go*.

</details>

**22.** *There is a hospital near your house.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Is there a hospital near your house?*** With *there is*, the verb *is* moves before *there*.

</details>

## Part C: Indirect questions

**23.** *Make it indirect: Where is the bus stand? (Can you tell me ...)*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Can you tell me where the bus stand is?*** An indirect question uses statement order: subject (*the bus stand*) + verb (*is*).

</details>

**24.** *Make it indirect: What time does the shop open? (Do you know ...)*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Do you know what time the shop opens?*** Remove *does* and use the normal verb form (*opens*).

</details>

**25.** *Make it indirect: Is the principal in the office? (Could you tell me ...)*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***Could you tell me if (or whether) the principal is in the office?*** A yes/no question becomes *if / whether* + statement order.

</details>

**26.** *Make it indirect: Why did Ravi leave early? (I wonder ...)*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***I wonder why Ravi left early.*** No *did*; the verb goes back to its past form (*left*). The outer sentence is a statement, so it ends with a full stop.

</details>

## Part D: Find and correct the mistake

**27.** *Where you are going?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Where **are you** going?* In a direct question, the helping verb comes before the subject.

</details>

**28.** *What did she said to you?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*What did she **say** to you?* After *did*, use V1.

</details>

**29.** *Who did break the window?* (meaning: who was the person that broke it)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Who **broke** the window?* *Who* is the subject, so there is no *did*.

</details>

**30.** *Can you tell me what is your name?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Can you tell me what **your name is**?* In an indirect question, use statement order.

</details>

## Part E: Add question tags (revision)

**31.** *You haven't seen my phone, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***have you?*** Negative sentence → positive tag. Helping verb *have*. See [Question tags (Class 11)](/topics/t10-questions/class-11/).

</details>

**32.** *Nobody came to the meeting, ______?*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***did they?*** *Nobody* makes the sentence negative → positive tag. Past → *did*. Words for people → *they*.

</details>
