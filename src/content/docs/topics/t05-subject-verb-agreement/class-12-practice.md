## Part A: Choose the correct verb

**1.** *The box of sweets (is / are) on the table.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is.*** The real subject is *box* (singular). *Of sweets* only describes it.

</details>

**2.** *Idli and sambar (is / are) my favourite breakfast.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is.*** *Idli and sambar* is one dish, one idea → singular.

</details>

**3.** *Ravi and his sister (go / goes) to the same school.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***go.*** Two people joined by *and* → plural.

</details>

**4.** *The secretary and treasurer of the club (has / have) resigned.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***has.*** **Trap:** *the* is used only once, so it is one person who has two jobs → singular.

</details>

**5.** *The principal and the headmaster (has / have) signed the letter.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***have.*** *The* is repeated, so there are two different people → plural. Compare with question 4.

</details>

**6.** *Neither the captain nor the players (was / were) happy with the result.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***were.*** *Neither ... nor*: the verb agrees with the nearer subject, *the players* (plural).

</details>

**7.** *Either the students or the teacher (has / have) the key.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***has.*** *Either ... or*: the nearer subject is *the teacher* (singular).

</details>

**8.** *Not only the parents but also the child (was / were) injured.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***was.*** *Not only ... but also*: the nearer subject is *the child* (singular).

</details>

**9.** *The minister, along with his officers, (is / are) visiting the flood area.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is.*** *Along with* does not join two subjects. The real subject is *the minister* (singular).

</details>

**10.** *Kavya, as well as her friends, (enjoy / enjoys) classical music.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***enjoys.*** *As well as* adds extra information. The subject is *Kavya* (singular).

</details>

**11.** *Every boy and every girl (was / were) given a sapling.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***was.*** *Every* before nouns joined by *and* makes the verb singular.

</details>

**12.** *Each of the players (has / have) a new jersey.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***has.*** *Each* (even with *of* + plural noun) takes a singular verb.

</details>

**13.** *Nobody (know / knows) the answer.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***knows.*** *Nobody* is singular.

</details>

**14.** *The team (is / are) playing very well this season.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is.*** The team acts as one unit → singular. (British English also allows *are*.)

</details>

**15.** *The committee (is / are) divided in their opinions about the new rule.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***are.*** *Divided* and *their opinions* show that the members are acting separately → plural.

</details>

**16.** *The number of road accidents (is / are) rising.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is.*** *The number of* = one figure → singular.

</details>

**17.** *A number of students (has / have) joined the science club.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***have.*** *A number of* = many → plural. Compare with question 16.

</details>

**18.** *One of my cousins (live / lives) in Trichy.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***lives.*** The subject is *one* → singular.

</details>

**19.** *Meena is one of those girls who (work / works) very hard.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***work.*** **Trap:** *who* refers to *those girls* (plural), not to *Meena*. Many girls work hard, and Meena is one of them.

</details>

**20.** *The news of his success (has / have) made us happy.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***has.*** *News* ends in *-s* but is singular.

</details>

**21.** *Economics (is / are) a difficult subject.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is.*** The name of a subject ending in *-ics* is singular.

</details>

**22.** *My spectacles (is / are) broken.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***are.*** *Spectacles* (a thing with two parts) is always plural.

</details>

**23.** *A pair of scissors (is / are) on the shelf.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is.*** **Trap:** the subject is *a pair* (singular), not *scissors*.

</details>

**24.** *The police (has / have) arrested the thief.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***have.*** *Police* has no *-s*, but it is always plural.

</details>

**25.** *Five thousand rupees (is / are) a big amount for a student.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is.*** An amount of money seen as one total → singular.

</details>

**26.** *"Gitanjali" (is / are) a collection of poems by Tagore.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***is.*** The title of one book → singular.

</details>

**27.** *There (is / are) many temples in Kanchipuram.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***are.*** After *there*, agree with the noun that follows: *many temples* (plural).

</details>

## Part B: Fill in with *is, are, has* or *have*

**28.** *Half of the mangoes ______ rotten.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***are.*** With *half of*, look at the noun after *of*: *mangoes* (plural).

</details>

**29.** *The rich ______ a duty to help the poor.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***have.*** *The* + adjective (*the rich* = rich people) takes a plural verb.

</details>

**30.** *Your advice ______ been very useful.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

***has.*** *Advice* is uncountable → singular.

</details>

## Part C: Find and correct the mistake

**31.** *The list of selected students are on the notice board.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The list of selected students **is** on the notice board.* The subject is *list*, not *students*.

</details>

**32.** *The teacher, with all her students, were in the hall.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*The teacher, with all her students, **was** in the hall.* *With* does not join two subjects. The subject is *the teacher*.

</details>

**33.** *Mathematics are taught by Mr Karthik.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Mathematics **is** taught by Mr Karthik.* *Mathematics* is singular.

</details>

**34.** *Neither you nor I are to blame.*

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

*Neither you nor I **am** to blame.* With *neither ... nor*, the verb agrees with the nearer subject, *I* → *am*. (Many speakers avoid this by saying *You are not to blame, and neither am I*.)

</details>
